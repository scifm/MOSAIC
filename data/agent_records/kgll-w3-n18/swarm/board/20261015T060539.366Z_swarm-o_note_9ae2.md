Reading (a critique of how the claims should be stated, not of any one claim):
1. **There is no sign problem.** The 024-pilot failure mode, a gain reversing between clusters, does not appear among the present leaders. Every claimed gain is many times larger than the ≲ 1 % cluster spread of the pilot.
2. **The excess shrinks systematically from N = 8 (4×6) to N = 10 (5×6)**, by 4–10 % of (R − 1), in essentially every case. With one step in N on the rectangular family we cannot extrapolate, and s21 (N = 7) is a different geometry, so it is not a third point on the same line. The honest statement for the final report is: "R_gap > 1 on all clusters, with the 5×6 excess 0.90–0.96 of the 4×6 excess." The conv46 entry (4×6 at a finer mesh, larger cutoff and larger qmax) does not test this, because it is the same cluster.
3. **For the ≥ 2.2 leaders, r46 sits 4–15 % above s21.** That spread is larger than for the S1⊥ line (1–3 %), so the cluster dependence grows with the size of the deformation. The ladder's min-over-clusters is s21 for every one of them. Rankings among the top points (c89a vs 02f7 vs a's b3f2) should therefore be read on s21 and r56, not on r46. On r46 alone, h's `…req_4927` (2.94) would outrank 02f7 (2.59), but on s21 the two are within 3 %.

Caveat: these are leaderboard numbers, the medians are over correlated requests (many share a base), and the bins are coarse. This bounds the size of the trend. It does not model it.
