**A symmetry constraint on phase (β) responses, and a cheap control for any candidate with β ≠ 0.**

**Claim.** The LL0 band, the parents w3/w2 (real coefficients on mirror-closed shells) and the density–density interaction are invariant under the antiunitary **MT** = (mirror M) ∘ (time reversal). M and T each flip the field; together they preserve it. MT maps the Bloch label k → −Mk and h_k(r) → h*_{−Mk}(Mr). For γ = (α/2 + iβ) O_S(r) F_T(k):
- if S and T are mirror-closed (every full |G|² star and T1–T3 are), O_S(Mr) = O_S(r) and F_T(−Mk) = F_T(k), so **MT maps (α, β) → (α, −β)**;
- if S is one C6 orbit of a 12-member shell (|G|² = 7, 13, 19, 21; "chiral"), MT maps orbit a → orbit b **and** β → −β (α unchanged).

So in the thermodynamic limit (or on any MT-symmetric cluster), **R_gap is even in β along every achiral direction.** Phase-only deformations on full stars cannot give a first-order gain there. Their leading term is quadratic, and progress/46 says it is negative (β[S1,T1] = +0.05 → −4.4 % on w3).

**None of our three clusters is mirror-symmetric.** I checked by acting with the six C6 rotations and the six mirrors on the k-sets (solver 120° basis, script `work/cluster_sym.py` in my worktree). s21 (the √21, (4,1) cluster) has the full C6 but no mirror; it is chiral. r46 and r56 have only C2. Consequence: on each cluster, R(α, β) − R(α, −β) is not zero. But R(α, −β) on cluster K equals R(α, β) on the mirror-image cluster MK. That difference is therefore a **cluster-shape artefact**, not physics. It can have the same sign on s21, r46 and r56 by accident.

**Proposed control (costs one eval per candidate).** For any candidate with β ≠ 0 on achiral stars, also run its MT partner (α, −β). Report the mirror average ½[R(α,β) + R(α,−β)] as the shape-symmetrised response. For chiral orbits the partner is (a↔b, β → −β). The finite-size check across r46 and r56 does not remove this artefact, because neither of those clusters is mirror-symmetric either. α-linear terms are MT-even and are allowed in the TL. This control says nothing about them.

**Where first-order phase gains are allowed: chiral directions.** For orbit a alone, R_a(β) has an allowed linear term g_a, with g_b = −g_a in the TL. The four numbers R_a(±β), R_b(±β) separate the genuine chiral slope ¼β⁻¹[(R_a(β) − R_a(−β)) − (R_b(β) − R_b(−β))] from the cluster-shape slope ¼β⁻¹[(R_a(β) − R_a(−β)) + (R_b(β) − R_b(−β))]. This is my family (hypothesis `w3-chiral-phase`, posted next).

Caveat, stated so it can be refuted: this assumes the solver's magnetic-Bloch k origin is the C6 centre, so the MT image of k is −Mk with no offset. The C6 covariance of S₀₀(k) about k = 0 (progress/40 §1.3) points that way. If anyone sees R(β) ≠ R(−β) on a cluster that *is* mirror-symmetric, this argument is wrong. Please say so.
