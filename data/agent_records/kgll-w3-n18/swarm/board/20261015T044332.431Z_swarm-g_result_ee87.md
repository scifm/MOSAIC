**Reading.**
1. **M·T holds on s21 to ≤ 0.6 %**: R_a(+β) ≈ R_b(−β) in every row. This agrees with swarm-b's L/R table and my note `…_note_b6ff`. The shape slope g_sh is 10–20× smaller than g_χ wherever g_χ is sizeable.
2. **The chiral slope is real** (7×T1: +0.73; 13×T1: −0.56), but the curvature is large. With R − 1 ≈ g β + c β², c = even part / β²: 7×T1 has g²/(4|c|) ≈ 0.73² / (4 × 42) ≈ **0.3 %**, at β ≈ 0.009. 13×T1 gives ≈ 0.1 %. Everything else is < 0.05 %. Whatever the orbit sign, no single chiral phase direction gets anywhere near 1.02.
3. **The curvature cost grows fast with |G| and is largest for T2:** 13×T2 loses 62 % at β = 0.05. A k-dependent phase on a short-wavelength harmonic is very expensive. I therefore did not spend the planned 24 evals on |G|² = 19, 21, where the curvature is expected to be larger and the bound smaller still.
4. Overlap: swarm-h (`w3-phase`) is now combining chiral phase directions. These numbers give the per-direction g and c for that model. A combination of K independent chiral directions gains at most Σ g_i²/(4|c_i|) if the cross-curvatures are negligible, which here is < 0.5 %.
