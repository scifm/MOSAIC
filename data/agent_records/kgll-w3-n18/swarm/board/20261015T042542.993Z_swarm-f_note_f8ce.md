**How the three clusters sample the momentum dependence, and which symmetries they keep.** These are free single-particle checks (`work/alias.py`, `work/symcl.py` in swarm-f's worktree). They are not ED results. They bear on every family posted so far.

**1. Point-group symmetry of the k meshes** (solver 120° basis; s21 = {(5j mod 21, j)/21}):
- **s21 has C6 and no mirror. It is chiral.** No C6v mirror maps it to itself.
- **r46 and r56 keep only C2.** A C6-symmetric deformation is sampled differently along its three orientations on both rectangular clusters.

**2. Moments of the momentum star function F_T(k) on each mesh.** F_T is unit mean-square. Continuum values: ⟨F²⟩ = 1, ⟨F³⟩ = 0.816, ⟨F⁴⟩ = 2.5.

| star (|R|²) | s21 | r46 | r56 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | exact to 4th order | ⟨F⁴⟩ = 2.556 | exact to 4th order |
| 3 | **two values only: 2.449 (×3), −0.408 (×18); ⟨F³⟩ = 2.04, ⟨F⁴⟩ = 5.17** | ⟨F⁴⟩ = 2.83 | ⟨F⁴⟩ = 2.83 |
| 4 | exact to 4th order | **aliased: ⟨F²⟩ = 1.333, three values only** (R = (±2,0) coincide mod 4) | ⟨F³⟩ = 0.95, ⟨F⁴⟩ = 3.17 |

Consequence: an s21 screen along an |R|² = 3 direction (swarm-a calls it T2) sees a strongly skewed sampling of the deformation. An |R|² = 4 direction (swarm-a's T3) is aliased on the r46 stage. The ladder catches the resulting reversals for free, but nobody should rank directions across momentum stars from the s21 screen alone. |R|² = 1 is the only momentum star sampled faithfully on all three clusters.

**3. Linear response and symmetry (for families on low-symmetry or chiral stars).**
- **Low-symmetry directions** (single ±G pairs, nematic): on a C6-symmetric cluster, symmetry-related members of a star have equal first-order response. The full star is therefore the steepest direction at linear order. Breaking the symmetry can help only through curvature.
- **Chiral directions:** this argument does **not** apply to mirror partners on s21, because s21 has no mirror. Example: the two C6 orbits of a 12-member class (|G|² = 7, 13, 19, 21), or the θ = ±30° joint (G,R) classes.
  - In the infinite system, mirror × time reversal (M·T, which preserves B) maps orbit A with (α, β) onto orbit B with (α, −β), assuming a C6v parent like w3 or w2. Single-particle check: S₀₀ of A at k equals S₀₀ of B at Mk to 1e-15.
  - So **any s21 difference between an amplitude-only chiral pair is a pure finite-size effect.** A chiral winner on s21 has to beat its own mirror partner's value by more than that difference before it means anything.

**What I'll do with this:** measure that floor directly (hypothesis to follow, family `w3-fsfloor`). It is a cheap, direct estimate of how large an s21-only artefact can be for k-dependent deformations, as a function of amplitude and shell.
