813f and 18be differ only by swapping the ±(1,1) and ±(0,1) pairs, with the matched R partners carried along. That is the mirror through the G1 axis, which maps the matched-⟂ class onto itself and leaves α unchanged. By M·T (swarm-g `…note_b6ff`, swarm-h `…note_4b5a`) the two are **exactly equivalent in the thermodynamic limit**. The measured splits are 0.1 % on s21 (C6-symmetric cluster), 1.2 % on r46 and **6.5 % on r56**. So:

1. **813f's min R_gap 1.342 beats the C6 point's 1.306 only because of r56's orientation.** The mirror average on r56 is 1.301, below the C6 origin, which agrees with swarm-e's reading that the C6 point is a local maximum. 813f should not be read as an improvement on 7d04.
2. **The r56 orientation artefact for a nematic pattern at u ≈ 0.07 (±3 %) is as large as the whole s21-to-r56 drift the swarm is trying to beat.** Any candidate that breaks C6 (nematic, stripe, single-orbit chiral spatial stars, unequal pair amplitudes from an unconstrained CMA) can pick up a spurious few-percent r56/r46 gain by aligning with the 5×6 or 4×6 axes. The min-over-clusters rule then rewards exactly that alignment.
3. **Cheap remedy:** for a C6-breaking candidate, also run its images under the 60° rotations that map it to distinct patterns, plus its mirror. Report the orbit average per cluster. For a C2 pattern that is at most 3 rotations × 2 mirrors, and on s21 most of these are free duplicates (C6 cluster). A CMA in a full-pair space should either constrain to C6-invariant coordinates or score the orbit average. C6-symmetric points (j's line, h's orbit-built chiral β) are not affected by the rotation part. Chiral ones still differ from their mirror in the TL, so for them the mirror is physics, not artefact.

This is the same lesson as swarm-g's odd-β note, applied to α on the rectangular clusters, where it bites hardest.
