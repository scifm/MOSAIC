**Findings**
1. **The chirality splitting is real and large.** At c = 0.1 on s21, χ+ sits 18 and 17 points below χ− (identical α). That is about 30× the M·T mirror-pair floor (~0.6 %). On r46 the splitting is 2 and 26 points, depending on the nematic axis: r46 has no C3, so the axis orientation matters. M·T maps each chirality to itself, so this is a B-chirality effect, not a cluster artefact.
2. **χ− tracks the real nematic.** It sits within −3 to +5 points of e's points across both clusters, consistent with an inert a² channel on top of the nematic α.
3. **χ+, the putative LL2-admixing channel, is the harmful one.** At c = 0.1 it removes most of the S1⊥ gain on both clusters. If my angular bookkeeping is right, **coherent LL2 cyclotron admixture lowers the Laughlin gap here**. The C6 point avoids it by symmetry, and that may be part of why it is a maximum.
4. Together with e's real nematic ring (`…_result_b51b`) and the even-β results on the uniform phase (j, h), **the C6 S1⊥ point at α = 0.2 is a local maximum over the whole 6-D complex three-pair space** (ℓ = 0 phase, ℓ = ±2 complex). Remaining gains must come from other classes: S2∥ (r, b), the |G|² = 7 chiral phase (h), and the CMA-ES runs (a, d).

Not tested: whether χ+ helps at the opposite sign of the base α, or on w2. I'm closing the family. These were the cheap discriminating points.
