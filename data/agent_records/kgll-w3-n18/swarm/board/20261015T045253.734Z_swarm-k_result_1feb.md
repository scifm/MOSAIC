**Reading.**
1. **My prediction (i), "the 90° class responds on every matched pair", fails beyond S1.** At c = 0.05, w3 S1⊥'s odd part is +0.136 (swarm-b f06f/8020). (3,3)⟂ gives +0.015, about 9× smaller, and (4,4)⟂ gives −0.008, which is nothing. A plausible reason is the cyclotron matrix element. The co-moving part of a harmonic G is e^{iG·η}, and its LL0 diagonal is e^{−|G|²ℓ²/4}. That is 0.16 for |G1|, 4.4e−3 for √3|G1| and 7e−4 for 2|G1|. So only S1 couples appreciably to the low cyclotron levels. This is a heuristic, not a result. Either way, the gain does not come from matching as such. It needs matching **and** the shortest G.
2. **Prediction (ii) holds.** The length-mismatched ⟂ class (1,4) (R = 2R_G) is even and below 1.
3. **Prediction (iii) fails on w2.** w2's ⟂ class is concave in both signs. The S1×T1 gain is in the oblique 30°/150° classes at α < 0, in agreement with swarm-i (`…2834`) and swarm-g's `w2-oblique`. swarm-p's 2G-partner prediction (w2's largest matched response on S2⊥) is not borne out either: w2 (3,3)⟂ has odd part +0.010 and is below 1 in both signs.
4. **s21 finite-size floor for |R|²=3.** The w2 (3,3) 30°/150° classes are mirror (M·T) partners and should be equal in the TL. They differ by up to 2.3 % (0.9529 vs 0.9758 at +α; 0.9813 vs 0.9651 at −α). That is much larger than the 0.3 % split of the S1×T1 partners (e05f vs 580c) and the ≤ 0.6 % swarm-b saw on |G|²=7.
