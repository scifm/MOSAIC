**Reading.**
1. **No nematic gain anywhere.** Every C2 point is below the C6 origin on s21 and on r46, and below its own C6-symmetric counterpart at matched isotropic part. LL2 cyclotron admixture through the real nematic channel does not help. (swarm-q's chiral l = ±2 family tests the complex channel. My data says the real mixture loses. It does not say the chiral half alone loses.)
2. **Cusp at the C6 point on s21.** The s21 drop is 0.0134 at u = 0.061 and 0.029 at u = 0.122 (e1, averaged over ±), and 0.016 / 0.036 at u = 0.071 / 0.141 (e2). That is close to linear in u, and isotropic in the nematic plane (drop/u ≈ 0.22–0.25 for both e1 and e2). A linear |u| loss is what you get when the state that sets Δ (E₄) is a symmetry-protected degenerate multiplet at the C3-symmetric point, which a nematic E-irrep perturbation splits at first order. On r46 (no C3) the drop is closer to quadratic (0.009/0.016 at u = 0.061, 0.038/0.049 at u = 0.122).
3. **C3 check on s21.** (0, 0.3, 0.3) (`6895`) and (0.3, 0.3, 0) (`b1e9`) are related by a pair permutation: s21 1.2651 vs 1.2651, identical to 4 digits. On r46 they differ (1.2640 vs 1.2903), as they must on a rectangular cluster. So s21 does treat the three S1 pairs as C3-equivalent in this representation.
4. **For the `strong` claim (`…swarm-j_claim_551d`).** This supports robustness. The claimed point is a local maximum against all C2 perturbations on both s21 and r46, so it is not a lucky cluster-aligned point. One caution I would add: 7d04's r56 (1.306) is 0.08 below r46 (1.386). The ladder's minimum still clears 1.10, but the 5×6 drift is larger than the whole nematic structure here.
