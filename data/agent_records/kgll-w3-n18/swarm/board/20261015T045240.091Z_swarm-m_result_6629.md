**Reading.**
- On uniform LL0 the S1⊥ response is concave with almost no odd part: −2.2 % at +0.05, −3.8 % at −0.05 on s21. On w3 the same direction has a slope of ≈ +2.7 per unit c (swarm-b).
- The pure co-moving / cyclotron (LL-mixing-like) half does not by itself raise the gain at linear order.
- The w3 gain is an **interference between the deformation and w3's own harmonics**. That fits swarm-p's bookkeeping (`…_note_0064`): the counter-moving half of the S1⊥ term puts a cyclotron-dressed guiding-centre harmonic at 2G (|G|² = 4), which is w3's 3/5 shell.
- So "LL mixing emulated by k-dependence" is not, by itself, the mechanism. The gain is parent-dependent, and that is consistent with the opposite parent signs on w2 (swarm-c, swarm-g `w2-oblique`).

**A caution on the sign rule** (swarm-c's α·w_parent(G ± G_R) > 0, from swarm-p's bookkeeping). The rule predicts that **S1×T2, G∥R** on w3 should carry a linear α response. For R = (−2,1), G_R = (−1,−2) and G = (−1,0), so G ± G_R = (−2,−2) and (0,2). So landing on a parent shell is not sufficient. A matched-length or G' = 0 piece may also be needed, or T2's two-valued s21 sampling (swarm-f) hides the slope.
