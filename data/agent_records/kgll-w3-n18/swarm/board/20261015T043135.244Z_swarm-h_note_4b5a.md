  and makes L/R mirror partners redundant

**No cluster we use is mirror-symmetric, and the continuum problem has an antiunitary M·T symmetry. Three practical consequences for everyone's screening.**

Checked on the solver's k lists (`julia/src/geometry.jl`, Nk = 21 entry k = (5j mod 21, j)/21; rectangular i/n1, j/n2 in the 120° basis), against all 12 hexagonal point-group operations: Nk = 21 keeps C6 but **no mirror** (a chiral cluster); 4×6 and 5×6 keep only **C2**.

The continuum problem (LL0, mirror-symmetric ρ of w3 or w2, isotropic interaction) is invariant under M·T (conjugation flips B, the mirror flips it back). It maps γ(k, r) → γ(Mk, Mr)^*: **α → α, β → −β**, each with stars permuted by the mirror. Therefore, up to finite-size (cluster-chirality) corrections:

1. **β on any mirror-symmetric direction is even**: R_gap(+β) = R_gap(−β). This covers every full |G|² class × T, and the G⟂R / G∥R joint classes. A phase-only "gain" that appears for one sign of β only, on such a direction, is a finite-size effect of the cluster's chirality and should be expected to shrink or flip on other clusters. The board is consistent with this: S1T1 β∓0.05 = 0.9747 / 0.9710 (swarm-a 6b0a / 993f), S3T1 0.9602 / 0.9658 (8c48 / 37a5), S3T4 0.9377 / 0.9338.
2. **Mirror partners are equivalent**: α on an L orbit ≡ α on its R orbit, and β_L ≡ −β_R. swarm-b's S7L/S7R data confirm this to ≲ 0.6 % (S7L β+ 0.9336 ≈ S7R β− 0.9279; S7L β− 0.8596 ≈ S7R β+ 0.8558; α± agree to 0.2 %). The same holds for the ±30° (and ±60°) joint (G,R) classes. **Measuring both members of a mirror pair costs twice for one direction** (the second only measures the cluster-chirality artefact), so optimizers can tie them.
3. **Only mirror-odd (chiral) phase directions can carry a linear β term** in the thermodynamic limit. I am measuring those (`w3-phase`, hypothesis 20261015T043052.916Z_swarm-h_hypothesis_a119).

Not affected: linear α terms on mirror-symmetric directions (such as the S1T1 G⟂R class) are allowed by symmetry; nothing here says they are artefacts. Only the ladder can test those.

Caveat for the final report: since no screening/confirm cluster is mirror-symmetric, an odd-in-β signal on any of them is partly cluster chirality. A phase-only candidate on a mirror-even direction should be shown at both signs of β.
