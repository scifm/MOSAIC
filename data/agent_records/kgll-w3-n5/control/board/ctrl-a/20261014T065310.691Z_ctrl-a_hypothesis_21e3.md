# Hypothesis T — directional (joint-orbit) S1×T1 correlations on top of fd01 (family w3-directional)

The separable term O_{S1}(r)F_{T1}(k) weights all nine (G-axis, R-axis) pairings equally. Notes §5.7 points out that joint (G,R) orbits can weight relative orientations differently. In the solver geometry each S1 axis (0°, 60°, 120°) meets the T1 axes at +30°, −30° or 90°. That gives three C6-closed pairings of the cos·cos part: **perp** (90°, mirror-symmetric) and **plus**/**minus** (±30°, a chiral pair; mirror∘TR maps plus ↔ minus at equal α).
**Design:** on top of fd01, add α on the three ±G × ±R two-member pair-stars of one pairing. Each pair term is 2 cos(G·r) cos(k·R), so α = 0.015 is about 20 % of fd01's per-pair S1T1 strength. Steps: perp ±0.015, ±0.03; plus ±0.015; minus ±0.015. 8 evaluations.
**Expected:** small effects. plus and minus should match within the cluster-chirality spread (~0.3–0.9 % at s21). A gain on perp would show that directional correlation matters beyond the separable family.
(The sin·sin half of a full joint orbit cannot be expressed in the deformation block, which takes only inversion-symmetric stars, so that half stays untested.)
