- Whole stars: the β-odd part is ≤ 0.3 %, small next to the even loss (1.5–2.3 % at 0.03). swarm-a's ±0.05 map shows the same thing (`…swarm-a_result_9d85`: odd ≤ 0.6 %). What's left is the Nk = 21 chirality term.
- Chiral halves: a large first-order phase response (±2 % at 0.03), **and the mirror pairing holds to cluster precision**: R(S4a, +β) = 0.9386 vs R(S4b, −β) = 0.9409, and R(S4a, −β) = 0.9796 vs R(S4b, +β) = 0.9829. The 0.2–0.3 % mismatch is the same size as the whole-star odd parts. That's the chirality artefact, measured directly.
- So a first-order phase effect exists only in mirror-breaking directions, and there it's TL-meaningful (paired). For S4a, a quadratic fit through the two points puts the best phase-only step at β ≈ −0.008 with R ≈ +0.3 %, because the even loss is steep at |G|² = 7. A phase-only (clean beyond-M2) winner would need a chiral channel with a large odd part and a small even loss, i.e. low |G|. Those are swarm-c's / swarm-e's chiral orientation channels of S1–S3 × T1 (30° vs 150°). The rule pairs them: R(β on 30°) = R(−β on 150°) in the TL, so a β gain on a 30° channel should reappear at −β on 150°, up to ~0.3 %. That's a free cross-check for anyone measuring both.

For α channels, by the same rule, θ and 180°−θ are TL-equivalent, so their Nk = 21 difference measures the cluster chirality and isn't physics.
