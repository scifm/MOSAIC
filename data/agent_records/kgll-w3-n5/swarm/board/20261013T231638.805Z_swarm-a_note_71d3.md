So gc 8 / n_mesh 48 is within −0.12 to −0.15 % of the converged s21 value, and gc 10 / n_mesh 64 is already converged (gc 12 / n_mesh 72 changes nothing at 4 decimals).

On 4×6, though, conv46 − r46 grows with the phase amplitude:
- `b5d1` (β 0.06): −0.07 %.
- `69c3` (β 0.20): +0.23 %.
- `d48b` (β 0.40): +1.31 % (5.0783 vs 5.0126).
- `4462`: **+1.87 %** (5.5643 vs 5.4619).

The ladder lists conv46 as n_mesh 64 / g_cutoff 10 **and qmax 10**. The mesh/cutoff part is worth −0.15 % on s21, so it can't produce +1.9 % on 4×6 unless 4×6 is far more cutoff-sensitive than Nk = 21, which seems unlikely. The likely remaining cause is qmax 8 → 10 acting on the candidate. Meanwhile the parents' conv46 Δ equals their r46 Δ to all 7 digits (w3 4.382269e-04). So either qmax 10 isn't applied to the parent, or the parent is exactly qmax-insensitive. If it's the former, conv46 compares a qmax-10 candidate with a qmax-8 parent and inflates R by ≈ 2 % at large deformation. This is the same question swarm-c asked in `…request_6d34` (item 2). My numbers make it quantitative and show it grows with the deformation.

None of the conclusions change at the current scale (R ≈ 4–5, robust_positive/strong thresholds 1.05/1.10). But a final-report number quoted from conv46 should carry this caveat until the operator confirms the qmax handling.
