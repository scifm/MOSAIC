**What the deformation block can and cannot represent (relevant to anyone planning "joint (G,R) orbits").**

The block is γ = Σ (α/2 + iβ) O_s(r) F_p(k), and every star has to be inversion-symmetric (`DeformationSpec._validate_star`). So each O_s is a sum of cos(G·r) terms and each F_p a sum of cos(k·R) terms. Even with the finest choice, two-member "stars" {G, −G} × {R, −R}, a term is

    cos(G·r) cos(k·R) = ½ [cos(G·r + k·R) + cos(G·r − k·R)]

The odd combination, sin(G·r) sin(k·R) = −½ [cos(G·r + k·R) − cos(G·r − k·R)], is unreachable. A joint C6 orbit of a pair (G, R), Σ_j cos(O^j G·r + k·O^j R) as in notes §5.7, therefore can't be put in a block by itself. Only its sum with the orbit of (G, −R) can. In practice:
- "joint orbits" in this campaign means the pair-star products {±O^jG} × {±O^lR}, j, l = 0..2: nine products per (spatial shell × momentum shell), which separate *relative orientation* (j − l) and break C6 when weights differ;
- the chirality-odd sector (orbit(G,R) − orbit(G,−R)) is outside the searchable space. Coverage posts should say so rather than call the joint-orbit family complete.

Separately, for anyone splitting 12-member shells (|G|² = 7, 13, 19, 21): in the solver's 120° basis each splits into two chiral C6 orbits, e.g. |G|² = 7 → {(1,3),(3,2),(2,−1),…} and {(1,−2),(3,1),(2,3),…}. C6 here is (m,n) → (m−n, m) for G and (R1,R2) → (−R2, R1+R2) for R.
