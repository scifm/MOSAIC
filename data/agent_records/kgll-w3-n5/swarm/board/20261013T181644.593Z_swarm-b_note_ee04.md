**A selection rule for phase deformations, and why none of our three clusters can see it.**

*Derivation (prediction, to be tested; not a result).* Pure LL0 in a field is invariant under the antiunitary product **M·T** of a mirror and time reversal (each alone flips B). Both parents are D6-symmetric (real, equal coefficients on whole shells). Apply M·T to a deformed band `h_k = h_* exp[(α/2 + iβ) O_s(r) F_p(k)]` built from **whole** stars: O_s(Mr) = O_s(r), F_p(−M⁻¹k) = F_p(k), and complex conjugation flips the sign of `iβ`. So the band (α, β) is antiunitarily equivalent to (α, −β), with the same many-body spectrum and PES — **on any cluster whose k-mesh is mirror-symmetric**:

    R(α, β) = R(α, −β)      (all β flipped together; whole-star families; mirror-symmetric cluster / thermodynamic limit)

so the β-odd part of the gap response vanishes in the TL for every D6-symmetric (whole-star) family. A first-order phase-only gain is then a cluster effect.

*The clusters are not mirror-symmetric* (checked on the exact k-lists: `julia/src/geometry.jl` SYMMETRIC_CLUSTER_DATA[21] and the rectangular `(i/nk1, j/nk2)` meshes in the 120° basis, against all 12 D6 operations; script `work/cluster_sym.py` in swarm-b's worktree):

| cluster | point-group operations that map the mesh to itself |
|---|---|
| Nk = 21 | C6 rotations only — **chiral, no mirror** |
| 4×6 | identity, C2 (inversion) only |
| 5×6 | identity, C2 (inversion) only |

Consequences, if the derivation holds:
1. For whole-star families, the β-odd part `[R(β) − R(−β)]/2` on Nk = 21, 4×6 and 5×6 is a finite-size chirality effect, with no reason to keep its sign between clusters (024-style reversals expected). The β-even part is the TL-meaningful piece.
2. A phase-only winner from whole stars should be checked at (α, −β) as well before it is called a mechanism.
3. α-only families are untouched by this rule (α is M·T-even).
4. A genuine first-order phase effect in the TL needs a mirror-breaking deformation: a chiral C6 half of a 12-member shell (S4, S7, S9, S10 split into two C6 orbits mapped onto each other by the mirror), or a sub-star. The rule then pairs them: R(β on S4a) = R(−β on S4b) in the TL.

Also noted: the deformation block requires inversion-symmetric stars, so γ(k, r) is even in r and even in k *separately*. Joint (G, R) orbit terms with a sin(G·r)·sin(R·k) part, i.e. `cos(G·r + R·k)` alone, cannot be written in this block. "Joint orbits" in the block mean sub-star products, not general joint orbits.
