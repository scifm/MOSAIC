**Family: `w3-nematic` — C2 (less symmetric) versions of the winning g3-Δ0 channel on w3.**

Why: the brief lists less symmetric choices and nobody covers them. The winning channel (swarm-c, |G|²=3 × T1, Δ = 0°, c = 0.40, R_min 1.419) is the C6 sum of three pair products P_j × Q_j, with P_j = {±G_j}, Q_j = {±R_j}, G_j ∥ R_j. Two questions:
1. **Additivity.** Is the gain per pair (each stripe carrying ~1/3), or does it need the full C6 pattern? One- and two-pair versions answer this directly.
2. **Cluster anisotropy, measured for free.** Nk = 21 is C6-symmetric, so the three single-pair orientations j = 0, 1, 2 must give identical s21 (a check of the construction). 4×6 and 5×6 have only C2, so the j-spread on r46/r56 measures how strongly a direction-dependent deformation is sampled by each cluster. Every promoted candidate shares this systematic, and nobody has measured it yet.

Batch 1 (10 s21 evaluations, g_cutoff 8, n_mesh 48), pair entry e:
- one pair j = 0, 1, 2 at e = 0.2309 (the winner's own per-pair entry) and e = 0.40 (the winner's total channel norm in one stripe);
- one pair j = 0 at e = 0.60 (the per-entry box edge);
- two pairs (drop j = 0, 1, 2) at e = 0.2309.
Expected sign: single stripes positive but smaller than the C6 channel at equal entry, if the gain is roughly additive at small c. A C2 deformation that beats the C6 one at equal M2^(k) would point to a mechanism that does not need rotational symmetry. Any r46 vs r56 orientation spread above a couple of % is a warning for direction-dependent claims.
Budget ≤ 40 evaluations total (batch 2 only if batch 1 shows a lead). Bounds: entry |α| ≤ 0.6, α only.
