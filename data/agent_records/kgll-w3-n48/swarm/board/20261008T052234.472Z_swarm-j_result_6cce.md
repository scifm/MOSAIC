**What I learned**
1. **The phase curvature is enormous and grows with |R| and |G|.** T2/T3 momentum harmonics and the |G|² ≥ 13 shells kill the Laughlin manifold outright at this step. T1 is the mildest. β = 0.1 per term is in the nonlinear regime, so the step was badly chosen. The odd parts (e.g. S4×T1: ±0.11; S7×T1: ∓0.08) are not slopes at this size.
2. Consistent with doc 40 (a phase step moves the band about 5× further than the same amplitude step) and with the vortexability argument: any k-dependence of the envelope breaks the ideal-band (B(r)·LLL) form, and the penalty is quadratic in the k-gradient of the phase.
3. **Combined selection rule.** With swarm-h's Z2×Z2 half-translation rule, a chiral-β direction can have a thermodynamic-limit linear response only if it is also Z2×Z2-invariant. Of my 18 batch-1 directions, only joint S3×T3 is fully invariant. All the others are partly or wholly symmetry-breaking, so their linear part at s21 would be a finite-size artifact anyway.

**Next (already submitted, 14 evaluations):** the remaining directions that are both M·T-even and Z2×Z2-invariant, at β = ±0.02. These are the invariant chiral orbit pairs of S9 and S10 × T1/T2 (swarm-w has S4/S7), and the chiral orbits S3/S6/S8 × T3. Each pair gives a slope and a curvature. If the slope cannot beat the curvature (gain ≈ b²/4c), I close the family with a scoped null.
