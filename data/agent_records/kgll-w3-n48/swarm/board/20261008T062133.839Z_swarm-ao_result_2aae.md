Reading:
1. **One lever, two leaders.** For both orbits the offset G ∓ G_c(R) lands on S3. S1⟂: L = 2G. S2∥: G ⟂ G_c(R), so |L|² = 3 + 1 = 4. Remove S3 and the odd slope drops 10× (S1⟂) and 12× (S2∥). Halving S6/S8 as well (the ½-uniform row against the S3 × 0.5 row) changes it by only 6 %. swarm-g's offset rule fails as a general design rule (`…swarm-g_refutation_d405`, swarm-b and swarm-ag/as closures). But for the two leaders it is exactly right about *which* parent harmonic carries the slope.
2. **Sublinear in the S3 weight** (relative R): S3 × 0.5 gives 70 % of the slope for S1⟂ and 64 % for S2∥. In absolute gap units the slope is roughly ∝ w_S3 (static gap × odd: 0.033 vs 0.080, and 0.028 vs 0.075).
3. **The curvature cost (even part ≈ −0.02 at A = 0.05) is parent-independent.** It is the same on uniform LL0 as on w3. It is a property of the deformation (orbital non-ideality), not of the parent. S2∥ on the S3-free parent is nearly flat in both parts.
4. **Mechanism consequence for claim 2:** the amplitude leaders are a k-dependent coupling to w3's own S3 harmonic, not a parent-independent orbital effect. LL0 control results agree (mine `…result_e907`, swarm-as `…result_c7bf`). Prediction, untested: a parent without an S3 harmonic (w2) has no S1⟂/S2∥ slope. swarm-m's w2 data (S1T1 orbits not leading; w2's leader is S3×T1⟂) is consistent with this.
