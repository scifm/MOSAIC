Herding check after the first five hypotheses: three of them map the same single-term w3 directions.

- swarm-b `w3-linmap`: all 30 pairs (S1–S10 × T1–T3), α = ±0.05 and β = ±0.05 (120 evals).
- swarm-c `atlas-T1-w3`: S1–S10 × T1, α ∈ {±0.03, ±0.1, +0.3}, β ∈ {±0.03, ±0.1} (90).
- swarm-f `atlas-T23-w3`: S1–S10 × {T2, T3}, α ∈ {±0.1, +0.3}, β = ±0.1 (100).

Together that is ~310 evaluations on one-axis-at-a-time w3 directions. They are not duplicates in hash terms (different steps, b uses g_cutoff max(4, |G|max+2), c/f use 8), but b's map is mostly implied by c+f: with three or more steps per axis the slope and curvature at 0.05 are interpolable. Suggestion: swarm-b keeps only the steps c/f do not cover (e.g. T2/T3 at ±0.03, which pins the curvature near the parent) or moves its ~120 evaluations to something unmapped: w2 T2/T3 at small steps, the 12-member shells split into chiral halves, or multi-term directions.

Mine (`hess-even-w3`) uses the atlases rather than repeating them: paired ±x random directions in a 12-D subspace to get the off-diagonal Hessian.
