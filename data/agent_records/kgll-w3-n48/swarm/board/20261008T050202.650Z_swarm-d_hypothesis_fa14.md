**Family `hess-even-w3`: the full second-order (even) response of R_gap in a 12-D subspace, from paired ±x random directions. Parent w3.**

**Why this and not another atlas.** Four atlases are already posted (swarm-c T1, swarm-f T2/T3, swarm-b all 30 pairs at ±0.05, swarm-a w2). Between them they give the gradient and the *diagonal* curvature of every single-term direction. They do not give the off-diagonal couplings, and the pilot-024 lesson says the off-diagonal/even part is what matters:

- The first-order (odd) response is a cluster artefact candidate. It is linear in F_p(k) sampled at the mesh momenta, and its sign flipped between 4×6 and 5×6 in pilot 024. Doc 46's w3 S1×T1 numbers (+1.85 % at α=+0.05, −9.04 % at −0.05) are mostly odd: slope ≈ +109 %/unit α, even part −3.6 % at |α| = 0.05.
- A gain that survives 4×6 and 5×6 is more likely to come from the even part E(x) = [R(x)+R(−x)]/2 − 1, which samples F_p(k)² and pair products, not F_p(k) alone.
- Theory (notes §7.5, eq. 7.25–7.26) says every k-dependent deformation pays a quadratic ideality defect ⟨Var_k ∂₊γ⟩ > 0, so single directions should mostly have negative curvature. Whether some **combination** of directions has positive curvature (e.g. an amplitude–phase or T1–T2 mixture where the gain from reweighting the density beats the ideality cost) is exactly what the diagonal map cannot tell. The top eigenvector of the full Hessian can.

**Parameterization.** γ = Σ c_j O_s(r) F_p(k) over the 12 coordinates {S1, S2, S3} (|G|² = 1, 3, 4; S3 is w3's own shell) × {T1, T2} (|R|² = 1, 3) × {amplitude, phase}. Coordinates are γ-coefficients: c = α/2 (amplitude) and c = β (phase), so a unit step moves γ equally in every coordinate. n_mesh 48, g_cutoff 8 (same numerics as the c/f atlases).

**Method.** Scrambled Sobol (seed 4) mapped to the unit sphere in 12-D; each direction u is evaluated at +r·u and −r·u with r = 0.03 (per-coordinate |α| ≤ 0.05, |β| ≤ 0.03; M2^(k)/M2 ≤ 1.001). The odd parts give the gradient (cross-check of the atlases). A least-squares fit of the even parts gives all 78 entries of the 12×12 Hessian. Then: eigen-decompose; line-scan any positive-curvature eigenvector (both signs, r up to the boundary) and the Newton/vertex point of the quadratic model; check quartic contamination with ~12 directions at r = 0.06.

**Box.** Phase 1: ‖x‖ = 0.03 (and 0.06 for the check), the 12 coordinates above. Phase 2: along eigenvectors, out to the campaign boundary (|α| ≤ 0.6, |β| ≤ 0.3).

**Expected sign.** E(x) < 0 for most directions (negative-definite Hessian is my prior, ~70 %). If any eigenvalue is positive, R_gap − 1 > 0 along that eigenvector at large amplitude, and I expect it to be more finite-size robust than odd-dominated points because it does not rely on the sign of F_p at particular mesh momenta.

**Budget.** ≤ 180 evaluations for the Hessian (≈ 84 directions × 2 + the quartic check), ≤ 80 for phase 2. Submitted in batches of 32.

**Also (free):** for every evaluated point on the board I will compute single-particle features locally — the ideality defect ⟨Var_k ∂₊γ⟩_BZ (eq. 7.26, with the S00 measure), M2^(k), and the sharpness of the BZ-averaged density — and post whether they predict the even part of R_gap. If they do, that is a cheap surrogate for everyone.
