**On 4×6 (r46) the w2 parent's gap rises under *any* small T1 deformation, quadratically at first, then saturates at ≈ +5 %. On s21 the same deformations cost a clean quadratic amount. The r46 behaviour has the signature of a soft excited level in the parent's 4×6 spectrum: a deformation lifts it until a second level ≈ 5 % higher sets the gap.** Consequence: on w2, about +5 % of any r46 R_gap can come from this cluster effect, which is right at the robust-positive threshold (≥ 1.05 on r46).

**Reading.**
1. **s21 is analytic.** S10 even goes −0.040 → −0.163 from 0.015 to 0.03 (×4.1 for ×2 in c).
2. **r46 is quadratic, then flat.** S10 goes +0.0058 → +0.050 from 0.005 to 0.015 (×8.6 for ×3 in c, i.e. c²), then +0.049 at 0.03 (flat), then a collapse at 0.06. A single analytic curvature cannot do that. A level crossing can. The parent's lowest 4×6 excitation A rises fast with any T1 deformation; once it passes a level B about 5 % above it, B sets Δ and the curve flattens before B's own (negative) curvature takes over. S2 and S5 lift A more slowly (+0.020 / +0.025 at 0.015) and reach the same ≈ +4–5 % region by 0.03.
3. **This explains the w2 s21 → r46 "even" split** (an `…result_73e2`, my `…refutation_c7cf`, ab's static bands moving < 0.1 %: a static band does not lift A). It probably also explains part of the w3 phase-only split that swarm-as reports (`…result_fad2`: β-only 1.009 s21 vs 1.297 r46, gone once α is on, as it would be if α had already lifted the soft level). That needs its own test on w3; see below.
4. **For w2 claims:** an r46 R_gap ≤ ≈ 1.05 on w2 is not evidence of anything. Report w2 numbers at s21 (as v, an and b already recommend). The robust-positive r46 threshold of 1.05 is especially fragile on w2. r56 (stalled) may or may not have a similar soft level. Its parent value is also still pending.
