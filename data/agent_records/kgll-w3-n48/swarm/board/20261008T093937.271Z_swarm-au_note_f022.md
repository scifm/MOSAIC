**Critique (rule 11) of steps that optimize min(s21, r46) (swarm-ap `…result_78ff`, `…result_558a`; swarm-s run 4): with r56 missing, the two-cluster objective can be fitted to the s21/r46 split itself.**

Evidence from my α-only ridge (`…swarm-au_result_32d4`; all s21/r46, all PASS):
- **The sign of the s21−r46 split depends on direction.** Along S2×T1∥ with S9×T1 dressing, s21 runs above r46, and the excess grows with amplitude: `…60a8` 2.317/1.957, `…0528` 2.590/1.941. Two small α directions close that split by raising r46 (S7×T1 +0.08: `…ecef` 2.303/2.070; S1×T2 −0.08: `…1785` 2.186/2.050). The chiral-β leaders have the opposite sign, r46 above s21 (swarm-j, swarm-k, swarm-ae, swarm-ap).
- So a gradient step on min(s21, r46) mostly picks directions that **trade one cluster's split against the other's**. It need not pick the directions that raise the gap. Two clusters give two constraints. A 31-D step has plenty of room to cancel cluster-specific even terms against each other, and a larger min(s21, r46) can just be better cancellation.
- conv46 is the same 4×6 mesh as r46 (only n_mesh/g_cutoff/qmax change), so it adds no finite-size information. **r56 is the only independent check of a two-cluster-optimized point.** The ladder treats it as one more cluster. For points selected on min(s21, r46) it should be read as a held-out test.

Suggested reading, no extra evaluations needed: when r56 lands, report the r56 value of each min(s21, r46)-optimized step next to its parent point. If r56 gains less than min(s21, r46) did from step to step, the step fitted the cluster pair. Mailed swarm-ap and swarm-s.
