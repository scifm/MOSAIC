**Family `chiral-beta-w3`: mirror-odd (chiral) phase-only deformations, parent w3. ≤ 200 evaluations.**

### Why this sector: a selection rule
Take the antiunitary symmetry M·T (mirror × complex conjugation). B flips under each, so M·T is a symmetry of LL0 in a field with an isotropic V(q). Both parents put real, equal weights on six-member shells, so they are C6v. Apply M·T to a deformed band `h_k(r) = h_*(r) exp[(α/2 + iβ) O(r) F(k)]`. It gives the band at momentum −Mk with `(α, β) → (α, −β)` when `O` and `F` are mirror-even (every full star in the atlases), and `(α, β) → (−α, β)` when `O` or the joint (G,R) coupling is mirror-odd (chiral). So, on a cluster closed under k → −Mk:

| direction | under M·T | linear response allowed? |
|---|---|---|
| α, full stars (atlas) | even | yes (doc 46: S1×T1 +1.85 % / −9.0 %) |
| **β, full stars (atlas)** | **odd** | **no: R(β) = R(−β)** |
| α, chiral | odd | no |
| **β, chiral** | **even** | **yes** |

(Cross terms α_sym·β_chiral and β_sym·α_chiral are even, so they can enter bilinearly.)

**Finite-size caveat, checked (`work/cluster_sym.py`):** none of the three ED clusters is mirror-closed. s21 has full C6 but is chiral (its mirror image is a different 21-site cluster), and r46/r56 have only C2. Any β-odd part of R on a full star is therefore a cluster-chirality effect with no thermodynamic limit. It can flip sign from cluster to cluster, which is the 024 failure mode. The atlases' ±β data test this directly: I predict `R(+β) ≈ R(−β)` on full stars at s21, with only a small odd part.

**Consequence:** among phase-only deformations, which are automatically beyond-M2 (|h_k|² unchanged), only the **chiral** ones can carry a first-order gain that survives to large clusters. Nobody covers this sector systematically. swarm-e has the joint-orbit chiral direction for S1/S2/S3 × T1, and swarm-c plans chiral sub-stars later. I take the rest and coordinate with both.

### Parameterization (solver 120° basis, ADR-0035; orbits built in Cartesian, `work/stars.py`)
- **Spatial-chiral:** each 12-member shell (|G|² = 7, 13, 19, 21) splits into two C6 orbits that are mirror images of each other (verified). The deformation is `γ = iβ (O_a − O_b) F_T`, with T = T1, T2, T3 full stars. That gives 12 directions.
- **Joint-chiral:** the inversion-pair coupling `Σ_i P_{G_i} Q_{R_{i+d}}`, taken as (orbit at +θ) − (orbit at −θ). For S1, S2, S3 × T2, T3 that is 6 directions (θ = 30° or 60°). I skip the ×T1 blocks, which are swarm-e's.
- α = 0 throughout phase 1. n_mesh 48, g_cutoff 8, the same as the atlases.

### Method and budget
1. **Screen (36 evals):** 18 directions × β = ±0.1 per term (Σ|β| = 0.2 for sub-stars and 0.6 for joint). The odd part `[R(+)−R(−)]/2` is the slope; the even part is the curvature.
2. **Line searches (~40):** along the 3–5 best signed directions, out toward the box (|β| ≤ 0.3, Σ|β| ≤ 2).
3. **CMA-ES (~100, seeds stated then):** in the top chiral-β subspace, then jointly with the best full-star α from the atlases. The α_sym·β_chiral cross term is allowed.

**Expected sign of R_gap − 1:** a nonzero linear term along each chiral direction, so one sign of each is positive at small β. Its size is unknown, plausibly small, since the 12-member shells are Gaussian-suppressed in LL0. The curvature is negative, because breaking k-independence breaks vortexability. My prior on a robust +5 % is low. The scientific value is either a clean beyond-M2 positive or a scoped null for the only TL-allowed linear phase sector.
