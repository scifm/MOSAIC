**Family `lll-mech`: mechanism ablation of the S1×T1 ⟂-orbit leader on the uniform LL0 band (controls, never ranked).** Nobody has run this; it is a mechanism check, not a search, which is why it uses `--control`.

**Why.** On LL0, write r = R + η (guiding centre + cyclotron). With one flux quantum per cell, e^{iG·R} is a lattice magnetic translation and acts on φ_k^LLL as a phase. So every single-component k-GLL band factorizes exactly:

  ψ_k = χ_k ⊗ φ_k^LLL,  χ_k = Σ_G η_G c_G(k) e^{±iG·X_k} e^{iG·η}|0⟩_cyc,

where c_G(k) are the Fourier coefficients of h_k(r) and X_k is the guiding centre of Bloch state k (sign depends on convention). The envelope is therefore a **k-dependent cyclotron orbital**: e^{iG·η}|0⟩ is a cyclotron coherent state with mean occupation |G|²l_B²/2 ≈ 3.6 for S1. A static h gives χ_k whose components rotate as e^{iG·X_k}; the co-moving envelope (swarm-p, swarm-ad: R_G = (−n, m)) cancels that rotation and gives a **k-independent χ**, i.e. an ideal uniform band with a non-Gaussian, C6-anisotropic orbital (a backdoor to LL mixing through the cyclotron sector, at a_n = (1,0,0)). The ⟂ orbit is half co-moving, half anti-moving (anti-moving gives χ_k ∝ e^{2iG·X_k}).

This predicts that the leader's gain need not come from the parent's density at all. The clean test is to put the **same deformation on the uniform LL0 band** (W_G = δ_G0, M2 = 1, no lattice potential), where the M2 lever is nearly absent (M2^(k) rises only 1.00 → 1.02 at α_orb = 0.15, `swarm check`).
