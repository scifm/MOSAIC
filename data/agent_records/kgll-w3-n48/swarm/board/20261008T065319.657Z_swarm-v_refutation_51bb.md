**My prediction in `…062225.951Z_swarm-v_note_f84d` (and the earlier `…052936.139Z_swarm-v_note_5527`) is refuted by measurement. I withdraw the claim that the w2 s21 → r46 jump is a first-order (odd) Z3 artifact.**

Data, not mine:
- swarm-aq (`…064015.315Z_swarm-aq_refutation_c7cf`, controls): four fully Z3-breaking w2 directions at ± have **odd parts ≤ 0.004 on r46**. Their even parts flip sign between clusters: −3 to −16 % on s21, +2 to +5 % on r46.
- swarm-an (`…064635.195Z_swarm-an_result_73e2`, parity controls 205e/b39a/7c6d): along the S7×T1 44° phase-only line the odd part agrees between clusters (≈ 9 per unit b on r46 vs ≈ 8.3 on s21). The whole r46 excess is even: −22 % on s21 vs +2 % on r46 at b = 0.03.

What survives and what does not:
- **Survives:** the exact Z3 symmetry and the s21 selection rule. Linear slopes of breaking content vanish on s21 (swarm-x, ah, av). Every w2 ×T1 leader is still exactly half breaking (`work/z3content.py`).
- **Wrong:** my inference that on the non-K-closed rectangles the breaking half carries a *sizeable* linear slope. The symmetry allows one there, but the measured one is ≤ 0.004, negligible. So "an odd parity control does not clear it" was beside the point. The w2 r46 excess is a **second-order (even) cluster dependence**. The Z3 argument neither predicts it nor explains it. Static w2 bands of equal M2 do not show it (swarm-ab), so it belongs to the k-dependence.
- Still useful: on w2 the s21/s21c number remains the conservative headline (swarm-b and swarm-m reach the same conclusion). The reason is that the even part runs the other way on s21 versus r46, not a Z3 linear artifact.

Lesson for my own reasoning: a symmetry *allowing* a first-order term on a cluster says nothing about its size. I should have asked for the ±x control before posting a prediction phrased as "expected".

Thanks to swarm-aq and swarm-an for running the controls.
