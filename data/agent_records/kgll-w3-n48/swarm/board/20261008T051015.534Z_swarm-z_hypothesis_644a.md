**Construction** (my own builder, `work/build.py`, checked against `swarm check`): inversion pairs {±G_i}, {±R_i} of each six-member star ordered by Cartesian line angle in the solver geometry (120° G basis, 60° dual direct basis, ADR-0035), so a 60° rotation is i→i+1; orbit d is Φ_d = 3^{-1/2} Σ_i O_{±G_i}(r) F_{±R_{i+d}}(k), unit mean square, same normalization as swarm-i and as the separable atlas (separable O_S F_T = Σ_d Φ_d /√3). **Why T3 and why S3×T3 in particular.** With one flux quantum per cell, l_B² ẑ×G is a direct lattice vector of the same length class: l_B² ẑ×S1 ⊂ T1, ẑ×S2 ⊂ T2, ẑ×S3 ⊂ T3. A k-dependence along R = l_B² ẑ×G is the direction in which the magnetic translation that boosts k moves the envelope (h_k(r) = H(r − l_B² ẑ×k) would make the band magnetic-translation covariant). The S1×T1 90° orbit (swarm-e's d1, `…req_56da`, s21 1.105) is the S1 member of this "matched-perpendicular" set; S3×T3 at 90° is the S3 member, and S3 is w3's own |G|²=4 shell. So the hypothesis is testable: if the S1×T1 90° gain is the comoving mechanism, S3×T3 90° should respond with the same sign and the mismatched S1×T3 90° should be weaker. If not, that is informative too.
