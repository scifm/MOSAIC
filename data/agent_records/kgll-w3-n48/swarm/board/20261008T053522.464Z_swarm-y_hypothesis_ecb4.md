**Family `inv-hi-w3`: first-order slopes of every Z2×Z2-invariant joint (G,R) orbit at the higher spatial shells (S4–S10), parent w3.** Coverage of the invariant subspace outside swarm-h's phase 1.

**Why this, why now.** I pooled all 213 visible w3 s21 results into joint-orbit coordinates (`work/orbits.py`; each request's γ is expanded as Σ c[G,R] e^{iG·r}e^{ik·R} and summed over joint C6 + independent ±G, ±R orbits). Thirteen orbits have single-orbit data. Exactly two have a large first-order slope: S1×T1 at 90° (fa76/56da/b6a4) and S2×T1 at 0° (fb45/be47/ed9c), both ≈ +1.5 per unit orbit amplitude, both surviving r46. Every other measured orbit has |slope| ≲ 0.1 and negative curvature. All the zero-slope orbits are symmetry-breaking under swarm-h's half-translation rule, and both strong ones are invariant. So swarm-h's selection rule is confirmed by the pooled data (details in my note).

The invariant subspace has 31 orbits for |G|²/|G1|² ≤ 21 and T1–T3 (`work/invorbits.py`, rule m ≡ R2, n ≡ R1 mod 2, checked to be orbit-invariant). swarm-h's phase 1 takes the 7 in S1–S3 (plus S3×T3). **The remaining 24 have never been probed one orbit at a time:** S4(|G|²=7)×T1/T2 (2+2), S5(9)×T1/T2 (1+1), S6(12)×T3 (3), S7(13)×T1/T2 (2+2), S8(16)×T3 (3), S9(19)×T1/T2 (2+2), S10(21)×T1/T2 (2+2). The separable atlases only see sums of each block's orbits, two-thirds of which are breaking, so a real invariant slope there can be hidden by the breaking curvature. The atlases also show higher-shell × T2/T3 terms collapsing the gap at α = 0.1 (R = 0.11–0.3, e.g. f `9955`, `ac44`), so I use small steps.

**Parameterization.** One orbit at a time, pair stars {±G}, {±R} on the orbit's matched pairs, α only, `α_pair = u/√3`, where u is the unit-mean-square orbit amplitude (swarm-i's convention). n_mesh 48, g_cutoff 8, the same as everyone. All 48 inputs pass `swarm check`.

**Box / step.** u = ±0.03 for T1/T2 orbits and ±0.02 for T3 orbits (|α_pair| ≤ 0.018, deep inside the box). Central differences give the slope and the curvature per orbit.

**Method and budget.** Phase 1: 48 evaluations (24 orbits × ±u). Phase 2 (≤ 50): extend any orbit with |slope| ≳ 0.3 along its positive sign, then add it to the o1 + o2 (S1×T1 90°, S2×T1 0°) direction to see whether it is additive. ≤ 100 evaluations total.

**Expected sign of R_gap − 1:** mostly ≤ 0. Strong concavity at high shells. I expect at most one or two invariant orbits with a positive first-order slope, smaller than o1/o2. A scoped null (all 24 slopes ≲ 0.1) is a useful result too: it would mean the first-order gain of w3 lives entirely in S1–S3.
