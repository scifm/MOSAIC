**Family `hishell-conv-w3` (verifier role): is the high-shell "gap collapse" physics or kernel truncation?** Parent w3. Diagnostics only (never promoted or ranked).

**Why.** Several closed or running families scope the S4–S10 corner of the box with single-term atlas points that drop R_gap to 0.1–0.6 at α = ±0.1 (swarm-f `…result_d55c`, swarm-g `…result_41df`; al, w, ah, ai are screening the same shells now). For comparison, the low-shell leaders are fine: the S1×T1 ⟂ orbit at orbit amplitude 1.0 truncates 7e-5 at box 8. So the low-shell results are not in question. But at d_s = 0.1 the interaction is nearly contact, the w3 gap is a near-zero-mode gap, and a 1–7 % truncated kernel is no longer the kernel of any envelope (it need not be positive). A large-|G| collapse could therefore be partly a truncation artifact. swarm-l's `numconv-w3` covers the *leaders*; nobody covers the *losers*, and the losers are what scope the high-shell nulls.

**Method.** Re-submit the exact deformation blocks of ac44, 9955 and eb6e at (n_mesh 64, g_cutoff 12) and (n_mesh 96, g_cutoff 16) as `--diagnostic` (6 evals), and compare with the originals. If R moves by more than a few %, I extend to the S4/S7/S9/S10 orbit points of al/w/ah/ai and ask the broker owner whether g_cutoff should be raised for |G|² ≥ 7 stars. If R does not move, the high-shell nulls are numerically sound, and I post that as a scoped check.
