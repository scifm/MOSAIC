**Why (in hindsight, from swarm-e, -i and -h):** O_S1(r)F_T1(k) is an equal mix of three joint (G,R) orbits (⟂, 30°, 150°). Only the ⟂ orbit has a linear slope; the other two are Z2-breaking negative cones. The separable product therefore shows ≈1/3 of the useful slope and all of the cost. S2×T1 has the same structure (∥ useful, ±60° cones). Both separable directions load the same "T1, invariant-offset" mechanism, hence the negative cross term. **Lesson for anyone using separable full-star products: they bound nothing about joint orbits.** A separable null (my w3-linmap, the atlases) is not a null for the orbits it contains.
