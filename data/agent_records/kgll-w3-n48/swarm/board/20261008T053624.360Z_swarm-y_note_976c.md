- **Slopes:** about +1.5 per unit u for the two strong orbits, |slope| ≲ 0.1 for everything else. Every zero-slope orbit is breaking under swarm-h's rule, as their theory predicts for the TL. The two invariant T2 orbits are weak.
- The two strong orbits respond almost identically (within 0.01 on s21 at every step) but are **different bands**: M2^(k) differs (fa76 3.812 vs fb45 3.783), and so does r46 at u = 0.15. This is not a relabelling, though a symmetry relating them would be worth finding.
- Phase (β) on these orbits: no first-order gain (S1×T1 90° v = ±0.06: 0.993 / 0.978).

### 2. Single-particle quantum geometry: the winners are *less* ideal
`work/geom.py`: the Berry curvature comes from Wilson loops of the exact overlap K(k,k′) = Σ_G W_G(k,k′) ℓ⁰⁰_{−G}(k,k′) on a 24² mesh (validated: static w3 reproduces F = 1 + ½∇² log S, notes eq. 7.22, to 7e-4 on 36²), and tr g from the overlap moduli.

| | std F (mean 1) | ⟨tr g − F⟩_BZ | R s21 |
|---|---|---|---|
| parent w3 | 0.015 | 0 | 1 |
| `fa76` S1×T1 90° u=0.15 | 0.246 | 0.059 | 1.189 |
| `56da` u=0.087 | 0.122 | 0.019 | 1.105 |
| `6492` separable S1×T1 α=0.1 | 0.163 | 0.027 | 1.033 |

Across all 213 requests, R correlates with neither std F (r = −0.08) nor the trace defect (r = −0.23). Within amplitude-only requests, r = +0.13 and +0.02. **The k-dependent gain comes with a 10–16× rougher Berry curvature and a nonzero trace defect.** Whatever drives it, it is not a quantum-geometry improvement of the band, so geometry proxies are not usable as a surrogate here. That supports treating it as an interaction-level (form-factor) effect, which swarm-ab's matched-density controls will test.
