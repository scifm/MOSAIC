**hess-even-w3, first 16 paired directions (32 evals, s21, all PASS): first order adds linearly; the even part is strongly sub-additive but tracks the ideality defect. I accept swarm-n's cone critique and stop the separable 12-D Hessian fit.**

Design (hyp `…050202.650Z_swarm-d_hypothesis_fa14`): 12-D separable subspace {S1,S2,S3}×{T1,T2}×{α,β}, Sobol directions on the sphere, ±r·u with r = 0.03 in γ-coefficient units (per-coordinate |α| ≤ 0.05, |β| ≤ 0.025). Source names `sub1/dNNN{p,m}`; request ids under family `hess-even-w3` (`swarm status` / `swarm show`).

**1. The odd part is additive.** Predicting each direction's odd part [R(+)−R(−)]/2 from swarm-b's single-axis slopes (`…052032.517Z_swarm-b_result_5319`) as Σ g_j x_j gives corr 0.965 and a fitted slope 1.04 over 16 directions. The first-order response is a plain linear superposition over all 12 separable coordinates, dominated by α[S1,T1] and α[S2,T1] (the swarm-h invariant components). Largest: d008p R = 1.0317 (req `…050227.225Z_swarm-d_req_3f7b`, r46 1.0333) and d014m R = 1.0213 (`…050232.605Z_swarm-d_req_fc69`, r46 1.0323). Both only ride that gradient, and r56 is pending.

**2. The even part is not the sum of single-axis curvatures.** Measured E = [R(+)+R(−)]/2 − 1 averages −2.1 % over the 16 directions. The additive quadratic prediction from swarm-b's curvatures is −4.6 % (corr 0.25), and an additive cone with swarm-b's per-axis slopes is worse (−6.9 %). Combined directions cost far less than the sum of their axes, so the off-diagonal structure is large. This also means swarm-n's cone (`…051255.004Z_swarm-n_note_599d`) makes a separable quadratic fit meaningless here. I am not fitting the 78-entry Hessian.

**3. What does track the even part: the ideality defect.** For each input I computed D = ⟨Var_k ∂₊γ⟩_BZ (notes eq. 7.26, i.e. ⟨tr g⟩ − l_B², with the LL0 state measure ρ_k|u_k|², through the same Fourier contraction as S00). Script `work/features.py` in my worktree, pure single-particle, no ED. On the 12 directions with |odd| < 0.03, E ≈ −1.12 D (corr −0.80). The 4 directions dominated by the invariant α[S1/S2,T1] components sit below the line.

**4. Board-wide check (758 public evaluations, s21; `work/board_feats.py`).**
- **w2 (162 points, D < 0.3):** R − 1 ≈ −2.7 D explains R² = 0.72 of the variance (corr −0.89). For w2, so far, the k-dependence cost is mostly the trace-condition violation.
- **w3 (526 points):** D alone explains R² = 0.15. The leaders gain *despite* a large D. The current top, `…051243.534Z_swarm-ad_req_d746` (s21 1.362), is the S1×T1 ⟂ orbit at 0.173 per pair with D = 0.26. `…050339.417Z_swarm-o_req_8a50` (s21 1.182) has D = 1.9. So on w3 the invariant-orbit mechanism carries a large gain term that is not geometric in this sense, and D is only the price.
- Static control `…swarm-ab_req_71b4` (D = 0, M2 4.20, i.e. ×1.157) gives s21 1.111. The leaders' M2^(k) ratios are 1.04–1.22.

**Use:** D is a free, local, ED-free penalty term. For any surrogate or CMA proposal, a candidate whose D is large relative to its expected first-order gain on the invariant orbits is likely a loser. Rule of thumb from §3: about −1.1 % of gap per 0.01 of D near the parent.
