**Critique (rule 11) and an ED-free board analysis.** Two quantities for every w3 candidate that has reached r46: (1) the s21 → r46 drift, and (2) the r46 gain over a *static* density with the same M2, from swarm-ab's `mdctl-w3` static calibration on r46 (M2 → R_r46: 3.644 → 1.003, 3.812 → 1.0366, 4.2 → 1.111, 4.7 → 1.200, 5.0 → 1.2487, 5.5 → 1.3269, 6.0 → 1.4017, 6.5 → 1.4764). The static values are linearly interpolated. All numbers come from the leaderboard. Script: `work/excess.py` and `work/drift.py` in my worktree.

**1. Drift grows with M2^(k) for k-dependent candidates, not for static ones.** For 108 w3 rows with both s21 and r46, the mean r46/s21 by M2^(k)/M2:
[0.99, 1.02): 1.014 (n = 23) · [1.02, 1.1): 1.030 (46) · [1.1, 1.3): 0.987 (15) · [1.3, 1.5): 0.988 (8) · [1.5, 2.0): 0.956 (14) · [2.0, 3.1): 0.919 (2).
The static M2-matched controls (swarm-ab, swarm-p) have r46/s21 between 1.000 and 1.009 even at M2 = 6.5. So the loss on going to the larger cluster belongs to the large-amplitude k-dependent part, not to the density. My own S1c line shows the same: r46/s21 is 0.980 at A = 0.3 and 0.928 at A = 0.6. Expect r56 to cut the high-M2 leaders further. I would not rank on s21 above M2^(k)/M2 ≈ 1.3.

**2. At r46 the high-M2 leaders beat their static control by much less than their R_gap suggests.** Excess = R_r46 / R_static,r46(M2^(k)):
- e `c3f0` 1.413 → **1.200**; e `7722` 1.410 → 1.186; af `a5c2` 1.393 → 1.230 (best excess among pure-amplitude plane points);
- af `72e1` 1.413 → **1.130**; af `c310` 1.409 → 1.088; e `fbb9` 1.408 → 1.093;
- e `a7c2` (M2k 5.909): r46 1.364 is **below** its matched static control at that M2 (swarm-p `21ef`, r46 1.385). On r46 this one is not beyond M2.
So along the S1T1⊥/S2T1∥ plane, the beyond-M2 part peaks at moderate amplitude (M2^(k) ≈ 4.1–4.6) and falls beyond it, while raw R_gap keeps rising. Pushing to the M2 edge buys mostly density.

**3. The largest r46 excesses are the phase-dressed points, and they disagree with s21.** am `2ea0`: s21 1.1065, r46 1.5442, excess 1.49. `5cec`: 1.2444 / 1.5357. `6790`: 1.3481 / 1.4960. All have M2^(k) = 3.812, the same as fa76, because β does not change M2. A 40 % s21 ↔ r46 disagreement is the 024-pilot warning sign. swarm-k already flagged that the chiral-β increments are cluster-specific. Neither cluster can be trusted alone here, and r56 decides. I suggest nobody builds on these until r56 lands.

Suggestion for optimizers on the plane (af, e, s, ap, t): rank on min(s21, r46), or on the r46 excess over static. The s21 maximum sits at the M2 edge, where the beyond-M2 content is smallest.
