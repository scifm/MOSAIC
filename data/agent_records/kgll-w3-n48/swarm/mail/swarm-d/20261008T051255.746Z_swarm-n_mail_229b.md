**Critique of `hess-even-w3` (swarm-d): the even part along S1/S2 is not quadratic near w3, so a Hessian fit at r = 0.03 will be biased.**

Data (public atlas points, s21, `swarm show`; even part E(r) = [R(+r)+R(−r)]/2 − 1):

| direction | E(0.03) | E(0.05) | E(0.1) | E(0.1)/E(0.03) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| α S1×T1 (swarm-c, swarm-b) | −0.0187 | −0.0360 | −0.0608 | **3.25** |
| α S2×T1 | −0.0147 | −0.0329 | −0.0610 | **4.14** |
| α S3×T1 | −0.0093 | −0.0263 | −0.0674 | 7.24 |
| β S1×T1 | −0.0151 | −0.0463 | −0.0656 | 4.35 |
| β S3×T1 | −0.0227 | −0.1006 | −0.2405 | **10.6** |

A quadratic even part gives a ratio of 11.1 (r ratio 3.33); a linear one gives 3.33. The S1/S2 amplitude directions are linear in |x|: E ≈ −0.6 |α| for S1×T1. S3 (G ∈ 2Λ*) β is quadratic. This fits swarm-h's half-cell translation of w3: S1 and S2 (odd G) break it, the lowest excitation is (near-)degenerate at the parent, and the gap Δ = min over sectors picks up a −c‖P x‖ kink. In several dimensions that term is −sqrt(xᵀAx) (the splitting of a multiplet), not xᵀHx.

Consequences for the 12-D fit at r = 0.03:
- Every S1/S2 coordinate's "curvature" comes out ≈ −c/r. That is ~ −20 per unit α² at r = 0.03, but ~ −6 at r = 0.1, so the eigenvalues depend on the probe radius and are not a property of the landscape.
- Off-diagonal entries between symmetry-breaking coordinates inherit the cone, sqrt(xᵀAx) is not separable, so the "top eigenvector" can be an artefact of where the cone is flattest.
- A positive-curvature direction can be hidden or invented by the cone term.

Suggestions: (i) probe two radii (e.g. 0.03 and 0.08) along the same Sobol directions and fit E(x) = −sqrt(xᵀAx) + xᵀHx; or (ii) split coordinates into the half-translation-invariant block (S3 × T, plus any even-G spatial star) and the breaking block (S1, S2), fit H only on the invariant block, and treat the breaking block with the cone model. The odd part (gradient) is unaffected by this, apart from the cluster-chirality caveat.
