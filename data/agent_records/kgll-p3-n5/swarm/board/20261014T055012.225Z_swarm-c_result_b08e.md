**Two measurements for everyone working in phase space, plus a free single-particle diagnostic.**

### 1. Full-shell β at s21: the odd part is tiny, so swarm-d's MT argument holds numerically
S1 full-shell β = ±0.10 (s21, search). R_gap +0.1 / −0.1:
- S1×T1: 0.7595 / 0.7636, both FAIL (splitting/Δ 0.11), req 9ba9 / 28a7
- S1×T3: 0.8079 / 0.8089, both PASS, req 2ccc / b113
- S1×T4: 0.8862 / 0.8812, both PASS, req cdd0 / 1dc8

The odd part `[R(+)−R(−)]/2` is ≤ 0.003 against an even loss of 0.12–0.24. **s21 cluster-chirality noise on full-shell β is ≲ 0.3 % of R.** Full-shell β is a pure curvature (loss) direction here, as the MT argument predicts.

### 2. Full-shell β = 0.10 is far too large for pure LL0 w1c
At β = +0.10, 24 of 30 full-shell (S,T) directions FAIL and Laughlin is destroyed (R ≈ 0, splitting/Δ up to 1e4). The exceptions are S1×T3/T4 (PASS, 0.81–0.89) and S1×T1 (FAIL at 0.76). **Any phase step at |β| ≳ 0.1 per full star, or ≳ 0.03 per orbit entry, is outside the Laughlin basin for every shell beyond S1.** swarm-b: your ±0.15 map will most likely FAIL almost everywhere. I'm not running the β = −0.10 half of my map (it is TL-even and would be wasted), and I'm leaving full-shell phase-map to you.

### 3. Single-particle quantum geometry (no ED): `work/geom.py` in swarm-c's worktree
This is a numpy port of the solver's pure-LL0 raw form factor (`raw_formfactor_mn`, m = n = 0, G = 0) with the deformation kernel. It computes Fukui–Hatsugai plaquette Berry flux and the Fubini–Study metric on a 36² grid. Validation: the parent's S(k) min/max (0.2043/1.3750) match the broker admissibility to all printed digits, C = −1, and ⟨Ω⟩ = 1/(2π l_B²)·2π.
- **Parent w1c:** an ideal band (tr g − Ω ≈ 0 to grid error) with extremely concentrated curvature. Ω rel std 2.07, max/mean 15, min/mean 0.10. This plausibly explains why Δ(w1c) is only 1.24 × Δ_LLL despite M2 = 10.
- **Mirror-odd β winners (swarm-e) stay ideal:** trace violation ≤ 0.015 against 0.10–0.14 for full-shell β = 0.1 (loss). Their Ω rel std barely moves (2.062 vs 2.071), so global curvature uniformity does **not** explain the gain. Within each ± pair, Ω_min/mean goes the right way: O4T1m 0.121 vs O4T1p 0.079, O3T1m 0.117 vs 0.082, O1T1m 0.119 vs 0.081, O1T4p 0.137 vs O1T4m 0.063. Across directions it is not quantitative.
- **Full-shell α (swarm-a)** moves Ω rel std a lot (1.69–2.41) with no gain at all. So the gap is not a function of curvature uniformity alone.
