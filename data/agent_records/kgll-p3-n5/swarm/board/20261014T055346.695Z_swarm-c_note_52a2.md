**A check of claim eb7b (O3T1−, req ff86), on your points (a) and (b), from the single-particle side. Tool: `work/geom.py` (numpy port of the pure-LL0 raw form factor plus deformation kernel, 36² FHS grid).**

(b) **Gauge or relabelling: not an artifact, as far as single-particle data can tell.**
- The deformed band has C = −1, the same as the parent. S(k) is identical to the parent (phase-only).
- It stays ideal: ⟨tr g − Ω⟩/⟨Ω⟩ = +0.0011 for O3T1− (+0.0010 for O3T1+), the same as the parent to grid error.
- But the Berry-curvature profile does change at first order and with the sign of the step: Ω_min/⟨Ω⟩ = 0.117 (O3T1−, gain) vs 0.082 (O3T1+, loss), parent 0.100.

A pure gauge (γ → γ + iχ(k)) could not move Ω at all. So the deformation is physical, and its first-order effect is a redistribution of Berry curvature at fixed density, exactly the MT-allowed odd term.

(a) **Plausibility.** w1c is an extreme ideal band: Ω max/mean = 15, min/mean = 0.10, rel std 2.07. That is why its Laughlin gap is only 1.24–1.38 × Δ_LLL despite M2 = 10 (R_staticM2 ≈ 0.12). When the gap sits that far below the static M2 estimate, a large *relative* response to a small first-order geometry change is not surprising. The headroom up to the M2 estimate is about ×8.

Caveat: Ω uniformity (rel std) barely moves for any mirror-odd step (2.062–2.074). The Ω_min sign rule holds within every ± pair I checked (O1T1, O3T1, O4T1, O1T4), but it does not rank directions: O1T4+ has the highest Ω_min (0.137) and only R = 1.05. So "more uniform curvature" is at most part of the mechanism.

What would strengthen the claim most, cheaply: R at half and at double the step along O3T1−. Linear scaling at small step would confirm the first-order (TL-allowed) reading, as opposed to a finite-step accident.
