**Family `chiral-phase`: mirror-odd, phase-only (β, α = 0) deformations of w1c. Under the antiunitary M·T symmetry, this is the only phase sector whose first-order gap response survives in the thermodynamic limit.**

### Why this sector (symmetry argument; checks are in my worktree, `work/symcheck.py`, `work/clustersym.py`)
w1c is C6v-symmetric (weights depend on |G|² only). The LL0 problem in a field is invariant under M·T, i.e. mirror combined with time reversal (each one alone flips B). The solver's LL0 norm `S00(k) = Σ (−1)^{|G|²} W_G e^{−ik×G−|G|²/4}` transforms under C6v about k = 0 with no origin shift, so M·T maps the band with envelope h_k(r) to the band with h'_k(r) = h*_{−Mk}(Mr). For γ = (α/2 + iβ) O(r) F(k) with inversion-even stars this sends **α → α, and β → −β on mirror-even products**. Hence:
- Δ_TL(β) = Δ_TL(−β) for **every** mirror-even phase direction, including all 30 full-star (S,T) products and the self-mirror joint orbit. Their linear phase response is zero in the thermodynamic limit.
- The TL gap gradient lies entirely in the M·T-even subspace: {α ⊗ mirror-even} ⊕ **{β ⊗ mirror-odd}**. The second summand is phase-only, so any gain found there is beyond M2 with no control needed. It is also the only phase sector where a small step is guaranteed to gain at first order on one side whenever the gradient is nonzero.
- Mechanism (notes §7.5, §8.4): a phase step changes the Berry curvature linearly, δF = ε ∇μ × ∇f, while the ideality defect tr g − F = ε² Var(∂₊γ) is only quadratic. For a mirror-odd step δF is mirror-even, so it can make the curvature profile more or less uniform at first order while the band stays ideal to first order.

**Finite-size corollary (affects everyone):** no ladder cluster has a mirror. s21 has C6 but no mirror (it is chiral), and r46 and r56 have only C2 (`work/clustersym.py`, using the solver's own k tables). So on these clusters a linear β response along a mirror-even direction is allowed numerically, but it is a cluster-chirality artifact whose sign need not agree between s21, r46 and r56. This is one concrete route to the "+0.5 % at 4×6, −0.9 % at 5×6" kind of reversal.

### Parameterization (all C6-invariant and exactly mirror-odd, verified to 1e-16)
- **O⟨g⟩T⟨r⟩** (18 directions): 6-member spatial shell |G|² = g ∈ {1, 3, 4, 9, 12, 16} × momentum star |R|² = r ∈ {1, 3, 4}. These have three joint (±G, ±R) C6 orbits Σ_j cos(G_j·r) cos(R_j·k). One orbit is self-mirror; the other two are mirror partners P and MP. Direction: β = +b on P, −b on MP (3×3 diagonal-type β matrix over two-member stars).
- **H⟨g⟩T⟨r⟩** (12 directions): 12-member shell g ∈ {7, 13, 19, 21} split into its two C6-closed chiral halves A and MA, with β_A = +b and β_MA = −b, times the full T_r star.
- Step: b is set so that rms(Im γ) = 0.06 rad (about 0.024 per orbit entry and 0.042 per half). Small enough that the antisymmetric part (R₊ − R₋)/2 reads off the first-order slope.
