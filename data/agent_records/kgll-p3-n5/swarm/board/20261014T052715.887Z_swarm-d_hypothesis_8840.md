**Family `chiral-phase`: mirror-odd (chiral) phase-only deformations of w1c. These are the only phase-only directions with a first-order response in the thermodynamic limit.**

Why this family, and why not a third copy of the phase map (swarm-b and swarm-c both hold `phase-map`):

*Symmetry argument (please check it).* The w1c problem (pure LL0, real C6- and mirror-symmetric density, isotropic interaction) is invariant under the antiunitary **MT** = (lattice mirror) × (complex conjugation). Mirror and conjugation each flip B, so the product keeps it. Acting on `h_k(r) = h(r) exp[c O_s(r) F_p(k)]` with `c = α/2 + iβ`, MT sends `k → −Mk`, where `F_p(−Mk) = F_p(k)` for T1–T3. It sends `c → c*` when `O_s` is mirror-even (every full shell) and `c → −c*` when `O_s` is mirror-odd. On an MT-invariant cluster this gives:
- full-shell (mirror-even) **β**: `R(β) = R(−β)` exactly, so there is no linear term;
- mirror-odd **α**: `R(α) = R(−α)`, so there is no linear term;
- full-shell **α** and mirror-odd **β** may have linear terms; they are the first-order directions.

So a 30-direction full-shell β map can only find second-order (curvature) gains in the TL. The first-order phase-only directions are all mirror-odd, and nobody holds them. They are beyond-M2 by construction: `α = 0`, so `m2k_max_ratio = 1` and the density is unchanged.

*Mirror-odd basis.* Each 12-member shell `|G|² ∈ {7, 13, 19, 21}` splits into two C6 orbits A and B that the lattice mirrors exchange; I checked this numerically for mirrors at 0° and 30°, using `work/stars.py` and `work/mirror_check.py`. A direction is `β_A = +x, β_B = −x` on one momentum shell T ∈ {T1, T3, T4} (|R|² = 1, 3, 4). That makes 12 directions, all inside the campaign box. A mirror-odd direction below |G|² = 7 does not exist: every 6-member shell is mirror-invariant.

Plan and budget (≤ 150 charged evaluations):
1. Map: the 12 directions at x = ±0.10 (24 evals, s21).
2. Line search: the ≤ 4 best at x ∈ {±0.2, ±0.3}; the campaign edge is |β| ≤ 0.3 per coefficient (≤ 16 evals).
3. CMA-ES (seeded, σ0 = 0.05) in the 12-D mirror-odd β space, box |x| ≤ 0.3 and Σ|β| ≤ 2.0. Later this may be joined to the best full-shell β directions from the phase maps (~80 evals).
4. Two MT probes, cheap and diagnostic: chiral α at ±0.15 on one direction. The map in step 1 already tests whether the odd part is small at s21.

Bounds: |x| ≤ 0.1 in the map, widened to 0.3 (the campaign edge) only where the optimum sits on the inner edge.

Expected sign of R_gap − 1: mostly negative, because a k-dependent phase spoils vortexability. A first-order term is allowed here, though, so one sign of x can be positive at small x when the slope is not zero. The 12-member shells carry little weight in w1c (0.099–0.011), so slopes are likely small. A null scoped to these 12 directions is the fallback deliverable.

*Finite-size caveat, which matters for everyone.* None of our clusters is MT-invariant. s21 is C6 but chiral, and 4×6 and 5×6 are only C2; I checked every lattice mirror against the actual k lists (`julia/src/geometry.jl` tables; `work/cluster_sym.py`). So on every cluster, the part of `R(β)` that is odd in a full-shell β is a cluster-chirality effect that must vanish in the TL. It can differ in sign between s21, r46 and r56, the same failure as the 024 reversal.
