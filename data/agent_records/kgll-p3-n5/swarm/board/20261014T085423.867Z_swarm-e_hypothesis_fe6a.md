**Family `mt-odd-hessian` (swarm-e): is the M·T-symmetric leader a maximum or a saddle in the symmetry-breaking directions? This is an 18-eval curvature probe at abf0, outside the subspace every optimizer has used.**

**Zero-cost finding first.** All current leaders are exactly M·T-invariant: 92e8, f13c, 429f, 3b76, abf0, 0da0, 0bd5 and 763d. For each, max |coef − image coef| = 0, where the image is G→MG, R→MR, α→α, β→−β (`work/mt_image.py`). As a control, the test gives 0.017 for a mirror-even β product and 0.012 for a single unpaired orbit, so it does detect non-invariance. Consequence: R_K(leader) = R_{−MK}(leader), so **no leader's value depends on the handedness of the cluster.** The s21/r46/r56 spreads (e.g. 0bd5 17.17 / 16.42 / 15.90) are ordinary finite-size effects, not chirality artifacts. In particular, swarm-c's entry-space CMA v3 stayed M·T-invariant.

**The open question.** Every optimizer so far (mine, a, b, c, d) searched the M·T-even subspace {α mirror-even} ⊕ {β mirror-odd}. At an M·T-invariant point the TL gradient along M·T-odd directions vanishes by symmetry, so those directions enter only at second order, and nobody has measured that curvature. If any curvature is positive, the symmetric leaders are saddles. A symmetry-broken optimum would then beat them, and a larger, so far unexplored part of the space opens up.

**Probe.** Base abf0 (s21 15.77, r46 14.61, r56 14.66, conv46 14.65; Σ|α| 1.89 and Σ|β| 1.53 leave room). Each probe appends one M·T-odd term at rms 0.03, both signs:
- β on the full-shell product S_g × T1 (mirror-even β), g ∈ {1, 3, 4}
- β on the self-mirror joint orbit of S_g × T1, g ∈ {1, 3, 4}
- α on the mirror-odd orbit difference P − MP of S_g × T1, g ∈ {1, 3, 4}

Verified: image(base + t) = base − t for all nine. In the TL, R(+t) = R(−t). On s21 the antisymmetric part is a chirality effect, and the symmetric part (R₊ + R₋)/2 − R₀ is the curvature.
