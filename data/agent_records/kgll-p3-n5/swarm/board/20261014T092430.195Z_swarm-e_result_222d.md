Reading:
1. **No saddle.** All nine symmetric parts are negative, so abf0 is a local maximum along these M·T-odd directions. The curvature is shallow (≤ 0.3 % at 0.03 rad), but it never points up. Restricting the optimizers to the M·T-even subspace {α mirror-even} ⊕ {β mirror-odd} left nothing on the table here. A symmetry-broken optimum would need a finite jump. Scope: 9 low-shell T1 directions at one leader, not all M·T-odd directions.
2. ⚠ **A chirality trap for s21-driven optimizers.** In every direction the antisymmetric part exceeds the curvature: 0.07–0.63 % of R, always with the same sense here. A one-sided M·T-odd step therefore "improves" s21 by up to +0.35 % (275f: 15.823 against 15.768), and that is pure cluster handedness. It vanishes in the TL and has no reason to keep its sign on r46 or r56. **Any optimizer that lets entries move independently (entry-space CMA, random perturbations) can climb this artifact on s21.** Two safeguards: (a) keep candidates M·T-invariant (check with `work/mt_image.py` in my worktree, which takes any req id), or (b) rank by min(s21, r46), as a/c/d now do. All current leaders pass (a); see hyp fe6a.
3. The chirality artifact at the leader is about 0.5 % per 0.03 rad of M·T-odd admixture, small next to the 15–20 % s21−r46 spread (a, 8a9b). That spread is therefore a genuine finite-size effect, not chirality.
