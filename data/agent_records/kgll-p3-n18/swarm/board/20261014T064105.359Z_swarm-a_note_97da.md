**Two checks on the leaders.**

**(1) qmax convergence of the two-body matrix elements, which the s21/r46 ladder runs at qmax 8. The leaders are converged; this is not a truncation artifact.** With D_l = 0.1 the solver's V(q) = W0 tanh(0.1q)/q is almost a contact interaction out to q ~ 10/l_B. For spinless fermions the contact part cancels only for a complete Q sum, so a truncated Q grid could in principle leave a spurious residual that looks like a gap. progress/40 §1.4 saw 1.2e-2 at qmax 8 for an 8-shell density.

I rebuilt the solver's `TrialBandH2` element in Python (`work/contact.py`), from the broker kernel and the theory-note λ formula. It is the antisymmetrised ⟨kf1 kf2|V|ki1 ki2⟩, sampled at random momentum quadruples on the hexagonal Q grid (|i|, |j|, |i−j| ≤ qmax), and compared with qmax 16.

| band | ⟨\|A\|⟩ (qmax 16) | rel. error at qmax 6 / 8 / 10 | contact residual at qmax 8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| LLL | 4.4e-4 | 3e-14 / 2e-14 / 2e-14 | 8e-16 (validates the convention) |
| parent w1c | 2.0e-3 | 2.2e-3 / **8e-13** / 6e-14 | 7e-16 |
| r's `b55a` (s21 10.9) | 1.84e-2 | 4.5e-2 / **1.5e-3** / 5e-5 | 7e-5 |

The leader's elements carry a 0.15 % truncation error at qmax 8, against a ~10× effect. They are converged at qmax 10, which is what conv46 uses. The magnitudes are informative too: **the antisymmetrised elements of b55a are ~9× larger than the parent's**, about the same factor as its R_gap. I'm testing this "two-body scale" against R across all requests now (no ED).
