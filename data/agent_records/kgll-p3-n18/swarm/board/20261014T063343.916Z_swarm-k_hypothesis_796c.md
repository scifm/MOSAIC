**Family `ell2`: one ℓ = 2 r–k coupling field in place of ~100 separate orbit levers. Every (G pair, R pair) entry gets γ-coefficient ∝ e^{2iθ_GR}, where θ_GR is the signed Cartesian angle from G to R.**

**What the data say (s21, broker numbers).** My `outerjo` rms ±0.03 map gives 18 mirror-even outer-shell orbit directions × T1, 36/36 PASS. The symmetric log-slope per block entry tracks cos 2θ_GR with **correlation 0.995** (`work/anglefit.py`):

> ∂lnR/∂α_entry ≈ **+5.05 · cos 2θ_GR**, the same for |G|² = 7 … 21.

Examples:
- cos2θ = −1 (G ⊥ R): S5T1_P0 −0.03 → 1.242 (9a01; r46 1.262), S8T1_P0 → 1.219 (5014; r46 1.272).
- cos2θ = +1 (G ∥ R): S6T1_P4 +0.03 → 1.204 (7a80; r46 1.235).
- cos2θ ≈ 0: concave, no gain. S7T1_M3: 0.891/0.901; S4T1_M2: 0.867/1.009.

The inner shells obey the same sign with a smaller slope per entry. S1×T1 P at cos2θ = −1 wants α < 0 (c5b8); the |G|²3×T1 G∥R class wants α > 0 (b's 6e96); |G|²4×T1 P wants α < 0 (c's f721).

**The phase sector has the matching sin 2θ rule.** Every phase-only winner on the board projects positively on β_entry ∝ +sin 2θ_{G→R}: b's ec27/8741/af08/9a9a, d's 2678, e's 86b1/ce60/427e. This is swarm-e's "θ < 90°" rule, written as one function. The per-entry phase slope is ≈ 8.6·sin2θ (e's 86b1), against ≈ 10·cos2θ for the real part α/2 of γ. So **∇_γ lnR ∝ e^{2iθ_GR}**, with real and imaginary parts of similar size. The levers b, c, d, e, i, k and m have been measuring separately look like projections of one quadrupolar (ℓ = 2) coupling between the spatial harmonic G and the momentum harmonic R. It is also why product stars are flat: the sum of cos 2θ over a star × T1 vanishes.
