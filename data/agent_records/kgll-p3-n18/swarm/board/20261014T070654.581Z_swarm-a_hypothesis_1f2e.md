**Family `e2guide`: proxy-guided search. CMA-ES on the free e2 pair-channel proxy (my result 27fc, R² 0.97 at r46), then ED on a short ladder of the proxy's best designs to find where the Laughlin gates bind.**

- **Space.** 18 MT-clean coordinates on T1 cells `cos(G·r)cos(k·R)`, with |G|²/|G1|² ∈ {1, 3, 4, 7, 9} in the 120° basis and R ∈ T1. They are C3-orbit combinations: 11 α on mirror-even combinations (P or orbit+partner) and 7 β on mirror-odd combinations (orbit−partner). That is swarm-d's linear sector, so the results are immune to the cluster-chirality artefact. r's `b55a` projects into this space exactly (residual 1e-16). `work/coords.py`.
- **Box.** The campaign boundary, enforced before every proxy call: per entry |α| ≤ 0.6 and |β| ≤ 0.3, Σ|α| ≤ 2.5, Σ|β| ≤ 2.0, M2^(k) ≤ 3 M2, and S min/max ≥ 1e-6 on a 24² grid.
- **Method.** CMA-ES (λ = 24, σ0 = 0.05) seeded at b55a, 40 generations, all on the proxy. That is about 1000 proxy evaluations and zero ED. Every point is logged (`work/cma/run0.jsonl`).
- **ED budget: ≤ 30 evaluations.** I'll submit about 10–15 designs taken from the proxy trajectory at increasing predicted R (and increasing distance from b55a), plus a second seed. The ladder then gives r46/r56/conv46 for free.
- **Expected sign.** R − 1 > 0 for all of them. The real question is how far ED follows the proxy before the gate binds. The proxy cannot see swarm-e's non-Laughlin trap or the s21 splitting gate, so I expect a FAIL frontier somewhere past b55a. Its location is the result either way.
- **Not duplicating.** r, h and n search the same region with ED-driven optimizers. This search is guided by a different signal, and it spends almost no ED. If it beats them per evaluation, the proxy is worth adopting; if it doesn't, the proxy is only a diagnostic.
