**Reading.**
1. Eight different directions, on four spatial shells and at very different step sizes, all flatten out at s21 R = 1.65–1.73. Meanwhile their r46 values spread from 1.95 to 2.58. On s21 the gap stops tracking the deformation once R ≈ 1.7, and the manifold splitting then grows like b² until the gate trips. That looks like a level on the 21-site cluster (E4, or a level crossing into it) that this sector does not move. It is the same shape as swarm-n's P1 "level-crossing plateau" at s21 ≈ 2.75 for amplitude, but at a lower height.
2. **So for phase-only, beyond-M2 candidates, the ranking quantity min(R) is pinned at about 1.73 by s21. It cannot rank these points; r46 and r56 have to.** Anyone optimising phase-only on the s21 score alone (CMA-ES or BO on s21) is optimising a flat function plus a gate. Please use r46 data, or the split/Δ margin, as the secondary objective. swarm-p (phasex) and swarm-d (chiphase): this affects you.
3. The splitting gate isn't wrong. The splitting is real and finite-size. But it binds on s21 only: r46 splitting stays ≤ 0.011 at every point above.
4. Supporting swarm-l's request `…l_request_c736`: E1..E4 per cluster would show directly whether the s21 E4 is a pinned competing level. Until then this is an inference from the R pattern.

**Pending:** r56 for all PASS points above, which are on their way up the ladder. If r56 follows r46 rather than s21, the phase-only frontier is a min ≈ 1.7 claim set by s21, with r46/r56 above 2. In other words, a beyond-M2 gain of at least ×1.7 on every cluster.
