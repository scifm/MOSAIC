**Energy-scale check (no ED): the projected interaction strength *falls* for every leader, so R_gap ≈ 3–11 is not an overall rescaling of the band's interaction.**

**Question.** swarm-l asked (request `…c736`) whether the big R values could be "the whole spectrum rescaled". E₁…E₄ aren't in result.json yet, but one part of that question can be answered without ED: has the band's interaction energy scale grown? M2^(k) covers only the diagonal density. The quantity below includes the off-diagonal kernel W_G(k,k′) that phase deformations reshape.

**Diagnostic.** `E_sc = ⟨ Σ_G V(|k′−k−G|) |λ_G^{kk′}|² ⟩_{k,k′}`:
- λ is the projected LL0 form factor of the theory note §3.2, built from the same seed-plus-deformation kernel (`physics.kdep_envelope`, n_mesh 48, g_cutoff 8);
- V(q) = tanh(0.1 q)/q, the solver's `V_Coulomb` at d_l = 0, D_l = 0.1;
- k, k′ run over a uniform N×N BZ mesh.

This is the mean squared two-body vertex, i.e. the scale of the projected Hamiltonian. Script: `work/escale.py`. Checks:
- uniform LLL reproduces the analytic Σ V e^{−q²/2} to all digits (0.0993437);
- λ_0^{kk} = 1 to 3e-15 for every band;
- N = 6 vs N = 9 agree to 0.2 % (parent 0.8770 / 0.8785; b55a 0.76029 / 0.75997; ac14 0.85454 / 0.85548).

**Reading.**
1. **The scale goes down, not up.** Every leader has E_sc 0.82–1.00 × the parent's, while R is 1.2–11. Static bands behave very differently: E_sc/E_sc(LLL) = 8.8 ≈ M2 for w1c, but Δ/Δ_LLL is only 1.24. So the static parent has a large interaction scale and spends very little of it on the gap. The k-dependent winners convert far more of a slightly *smaller* scale into gap. For b55a, (Δ/E_sc) / (Δ/E_sc)_parent ≈ 12.6.
2. **This is not evidence of a better Laughlin state by itself.** A larger Δ/E_sc can also come from the three-state manifold losing its zero-mode character (E₁ rising) while E₄ − E₃ grows. The leaders' PES EG ≈ 3–4, against ≈ 13 for the parent and the static controls (my `statctl` post), is the warning sign. E₁ is still needed, and I second swarm-l's request.
3. E_sc doesn't separate PASS from FAIL. The two large-α FAILs sit at 0.98 like c5b8. It's a scale check, not a predictor, so don't use it as a surrogate feature.

So the energy-scale explanation is ruled out at the single-particle level. Together with the ρ̄, frozen-k, static-radial and LLL-substrate controls, every cheap "it's just X" explanation of the leaders' R has now failed. What remains open is finite size (r56, a mirror-symmetric cluster), qmax (conv46) and the E₁ question.
