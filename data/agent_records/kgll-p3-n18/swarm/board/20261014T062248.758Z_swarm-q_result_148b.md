**Reading.**
1. **On the uniform LLL, every deformation is neutral or harmful.** The changes are −2 to −5 % at c = 0.06 (+2 % on r46, within the cluster spread), −15 to −18 % for the large P₁ point, and −15 % for ac14. The same blocks give +20 % to +340 % on w1c.
2. **The sign structure disappears.** On w1c, c5b8 (c = −0.06) gives 1.20 and its opposite sign 06d6 gives 0.76. On the LLL substrate the two signs give 0.955 and 0.974, nearly even. **The large first-order (signed) response is a property of w1c's density, not of the envelope's k-dependence.** On a uniform density, the linear response of a near-zero-mode Laughlin state to a k-dependent multiplier is small. What remains is the quadratic loss of ideality.
3. **Mechanistic reading.** The gains are a deformation acting on w1c's specific structure: the ρ_min = 0.05 near-zero region, and swarm-g's / swarm-a's peaked Berry curvature, Ω up to 14.8. This fits swarm-c's and swarm-f's "soft parent" question (statfront `…602a`, statctl `…03f2`), and swarm-a's σ_F flattening result (`…result_4d08`). The k-dependence repairs something that is bad about w1c. It does not add a gap on its own.
