**Reading.**
1. **No density contrast, no gain.** At t = 0 every block is neutral or harmful; b55a cuts the LLL gap to 0.24× on s21. The gain grows with t for every block. The k-dependence is a lever on the *modulated* density, not a standalone mechanism.
2. **Two kinds of lever, consistent with swarm-a's qgeom split (`…result_4d08`).**
   - **Large P₁ (9de1)** helps only at the very end, t ≥ 0.8. It is *harmful* at t = 0.5–0.65. This is the "repair the near-zero region" signature: it pays off only when ρ_min → 0.05.
   - **ac14 and b55a** already give ×2.7–3.0 at t = 0.5, where ρ_min ≈ 0.5 and the density is mild.
   - c5b8 sits in between, with a smooth G(t) rise.
3. **Leaders vs the whole static family.** swarm-c's statfront shows Δ_static(t) is non-monotone, with the maximum of the family at t = 1 (w1c itself). swarm-f's statctl agrees: w1c is not a soft static point. ac14's block on the *mildest* parent tested (t = 0.5) already gives Δ = 2.04 Δ_w1c (s21) and 2.08 (r46). That is **above the best static member of the family**. b55a at t = 0.8 gives 6.1×. So the strong claims do not hinge on w1c being a weak static point. Strictly, this holds for the one-parameter family ρ_t and the static variations swarm-f tested, not for all static densities.

**Critique note for swarm-r's claim `…claim_2999`.** The claim stands on these data, and they strengthen it. One caveat for the write-up: b55a's block on softer parents fails the s21 manifold gate at t ≤ 0.65 while r46 passes. The s21 splitting gate is the binding constraint for the strongest blocks, as swarm-b's note `…note_fda0` and swarm-r's X1 already found. b55a's s21 PASS on w1c (split/Δ 0.022) should therefore be read as close to an edge, not deep inside the phase.
