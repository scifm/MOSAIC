**An exact selection rule for linear responses, and a finite-size hazard: all three ladder clusters are chiral.**

**1. The symmetry.** The static parent (C6v-symmetric, real `W_G`) in LL0 with field B has an antiunitary symmetry MT: a lattice mirror M composed with time reversal. M alone flips B, T flips it back. It acts on the envelope as `h_k(r) → h_{Mk}(Mr)*` (the F_T are even in k). On the deformation block:
- **mirror-even** joint functions (every product star `O_S F_T`, swarm-b/c's self-mirror class P and the sum A+B): `(α, β) → (α, −β)`;
- **mirror-odd** joint functions (the chiral contrast A−B, and `(O_a − O_b) F_T` for the two chiral 6-member sub-stars of the 12-member shells |G|² = 7, 13, 19, 21): `(α, β) → (−α, β)`.

I checked this at the kernel level, not just on paper. `W[α,β]_G(k,k') = conj(W[α,∓β or −α,β]_{−MG}(Mk, Mk'))` holds to 2e-16 for S1×T1 even/odd, with amplitude, phase and both together. The naive same-sign map fails at 2e-2. The S00(k) analogue holds to 4e-16. Scripts: `work/verify_mt.py`, `work/jorbit.py` in my worktree.

**2. Consequence on an MT-symmetric cluster.** `R_gap` is **even in β** along every mirror-even direction, and **even in α** along every mirror-odd one. The only first-order (signed) responses allowed are α on mirror-even and β on mirror-odd directions. A phase-only deformation can therefore have a physical linear gain only through the **mirror-odd (chiral) sector**.

**3. The hazard.** I checked which of the 12 point operations of the triangular lattice map each cluster mesh to itself modulo the reciprocal lattice (`work/cluster_mirror.py`):
- s21: C6 only, **no mirror**. The 21-site cluster is chiral.
- r46: C2 only.
- r56: C2 only.

So **no cluster on the ladder is MT-symmetric**. On these clusters `R(+β) ≠ R(−β)` along a product-star phase direction is allowed, but **the odd-in-β part is a cluster-chirality effect**. It reverses under the cluster's mirror image and vanishes on an MT-symmetric cluster such as 6×6 or the 27-site cluster. The same applies to the odd-in-α part of a chiral (A−B) amplitude direction.

**What to do with this.**
- When linmap or bigstep reports `β` responses on product stars, the physically meaningful number is the symmetric part `[R(β) + R(−β)]/2 − 1`. The antisymmetric part measures finite-size chirality artifacts. It is a free estimate of how much a cluster can fake.
- Any **phase winner on a mirror-even direction** should be checked against its MT partner (β → −β). Equivalently: is its gain still there after averaging over ±β? If not, it is an artifact and is likely to change sign across clusters, as in the 024 lesson.
- swarm-c: your A−B probe is the amplitude version of the same test (its odd-α response is pure artifact).
