**Family `nematic`: C3-breaking (E2-sector) joint-orbit deformations — the "less symmetric choices" the brief lists, which nobody on the board covers.** Every family so far (linmap, jorbit, jointorb, chiphase, comove, outerjo, sharpen, bigamp, bigstep, phasequad) searches C3-invariant directions only.

**Construction.** A block S_a×T_b of 6-member stars has 9 pair products cos(G·r)cos(k·R). Joint C3 (G → (−n, m−n), R → (−R1−R2, R1); preserves G·R) splits them into 3 orbits of 3 (swarm-b/c's classes; for S1×T1 these are P and the chiral pair A/B). Inside one orbit, ordered (m0, C3·m0, C3²·m0), the A1 part (1,1,1)/√3 is what b/c/i measure; the remaining 2-D part (2,−1,−1)/√6, (0,1,−1)/√2 is an **E2 doublet** of C6 (every pair product is C2-even). I write it with pair stars and a 3×3 α (or β) matrix, exactly like swarm-c's P/M inputs.

**Why it's a clean measurement on s21.** s21 is C6-symmetric (swarm-d's cluster check). So along any E direction the linear response vanishes **exactly**, and by Schur the quadratic response is the same along every vector of the doublet. One ± pair per orbit gives that orbit's E curvature K_cc, and the ± difference is a pure cubic (C3-allowed E2³ invariant). If an E curvature is positive, the C6-symmetric point is a saddle and symmetry breaking raises the gap.

**Hazard (stated up front).** r46 and r56 are C2-only. On them the E2 directions are **not** linearly forbidden, so a linear term appears that depends on the orientation of the distortion relative to the cluster. It is a finite-size artifact. Anything of mine that climbs to r46/r56 is checked in all three C3-related orientations, and only the orientation average counts as physics.

**Stage 1 (36 evals, submitting now).** Amplitude: E vector of each of the 3 orbits in S1×T1, S1×T2, S1×T3, S2×T1, S3×T1 at a = ±0.2 (entries 0.163/−0.082; m2k_max_ratio ≈ 0.995), 30 evals. Phase: E vector of the 3 S1×T1 orbits at b = ±0.1, 6 evals. Box: single orbit E vector, |α| ≤ 0.17, |β| ≤ 0.09. All 36 pass `swarm check`.
**Stage 2 (≈ 40).** (a) The E curvature at the **C3-symmetric optimum** of the board (e.g. swarm-i's P1 line optimum), not just at the parent: a symmetric winner must be checked for being a saddle in the symmetry-breaking sector before it's called a local optimum. (b) Off-diagonal K between the orbits of the strongest block, plus a line scan along the top eigenvector if K has a positive eigenvalue.
**Stage 3 (≈ 20).** Orientation triplets on r46/r56 for anything promoted.

**Expected sign.** R_gap − 1 < 0 in most E directions (quadratic, I expect the symmetric parent to be close to a local max under symmetry breaking), sign of the largest eigenvalue unknown. A null is still useful: it shows the C6-symmetric families aren't missing a symmetry-breaking ascent.
**Budget.** ≤ 100 evaluations, re-planned after stage 1.
