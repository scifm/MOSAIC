**Reading.**
1. **One law for all three stars:** log-slope ∝ −η_R · cos 2θ_GR (even α) or −η_R · sin 2θ_GR (odd β), with η_R = (−1)^{R1+R2+R1R2}. η = −1 on T1 and T2 and +1 on T3. This is the magnetic-translation sign: for one flux per cell, `T_R ψ_k = η_R e^{ik·R} ψ_k`. So the harmonic `cos(k·R)` acts on the band as η_R × (T_R + T_{−R})/2, and its physical content carries η_R. That would explain why swarm-d saw "T3 phase sign flips vs T1/T2" (`…d_result_1c9b`), and it predicts the T3 amplitude flip I measure here. It is a conjecture with a mechanism sketch, not a derivation. It does match 5 of 5 star × sector fits in sign.
2. **Magnitudes are similar across stars:** |coef| ≈ 5–7 per entry for even α and ≈ 15–16 for odd β. So T2/T3 are not weak because their coupling is weak. They are weak because **curvature dominates.** sym ln R is −0.02 to −0.26 at these small steps. The quadratic vertex along the strongest directions is only ≈ +2 % (e.g. S13 |R|²=3 X24: ln R slope 0.146 per step, curvature −0.233).
3. **T3 (|R|²=4) is cliff-prone.** 11 of 19 FAILs are T3 points (manifold splitting 0.10–1.1), even at rms 0.02–0.03.
4. **For combined steps (swarm-d `kprof`, swarm-k `ell2`, the surrogates):** with T1 + c·T2 and T1 + c·T3 mixtures of the ℓ = 2 field, the gaining sign should be c > 0 for T2 and **c < 0 for T3**, if the law holds at the incumbent and not only at the parent.
