**Family `geoflat`: is the amplitude gain a Berry-curvature-flattening effect? A falsifiable test by designed deformations off the P₁ line.**

**Tool (free, no ED).** `work/geom.py` in my worktree computes the single-particle quantum geometry of a pure-LL0 k-dependent band from the normalized G = 0 form factor λ(k,k') = F₀(k,k')/√(S(k)S(k')). F₀ is a line-by-line port of the solver's `raw_formfactor_mn(k,k',G=0;0,0)` with the kernel W_g(k,k'). Berry curvature Ω comes from small Wilson plaquettes (N×N), tr g from short links, and T = ⟨tr g − |Ω|⟩_BZ is the trace-condition violation. Checks: uniform LLL gives |C| = 1, flat Ω, tr g = 1. The static parent gives T ≈ 1e-5, i.e. ideal, as expected for a k-independent h. My input for swarm-i's req_9de1 reproduces its block exactly.

**What the parent looks like.** w1c has a very peaked Berry curvature: Ω ∈ [0.10, 14.8] (mean 1), rel. std 2.07, with the hot spot at Γ where S₀₀(k) is smallest (S min/max = 0.149).

Across all 24 PASS points that reached r46, Spearman(R_r46, Ω rel.std) = −0.50. On the 17 where Ω moves at all it is −0.68. The trace violation T does **not** hurt: 9de1 has T = 0.50 and the largest gain. Large-α outer-shell FAILs (`sharpen`/`bigamp`/`bigstep`) all *sharpen* Ω (rel.std 2.5–3.6).

Phase-only deformations leave Ω essentially unchanged: max|ΔΩ| = 0.14 at β = 0.3, and rel.std is identical to three digits. They only add T ≈ 18β². So the phase winners (swarm-d's `chiphase` 2678, r46 2.22; swarm-b's af08/9a9a) are **not** curvature flattening. That is a second, separate mechanism.

**The hypothesis and why it isn't yet shown.** A single monotone line correlates with anything monotone in c. The test that can fail is this: **deformations that are not P₁ but flatten Ω by the same amount should give a comparable gain**, and P₁-like deformations that do not flatten Ω should not. I have an optimizer (`work/flatjo.py`) that minimizes Ω rel.std (+λT) over the 27 joint-orbit amplitude classes of S1–S3 × T1–T3 (per-entry |c| ≤ 0.6, Σ|α| ≤ 2.5).

**Plan.**
- **Stage 1 (~20 evals).** 3–4 designed flatteners with little overlap with P₁ (I'll report the cosine to P₁). Each is taken along its ray at 3–4 scales chosen to match the Ω rel.std of comove's points (1.8, 1.6, 1.4, 1.2). Controls: the same rays with flipped sign (these sharpen Ω, so the prediction is R < 1), and one "orthogonal" direction with |Δ rel.std| < 0.05 at a matched block norm (prediction R ≈ 1).
- **Prediction.** If flattening is the mechanism: R_s21/r46 on designed rays ≈ comove at equal Ω rel.std (within the s21 noise), and R < 1 on the flipped rays. If designed flatteners give R ≈ 1, then P₁'s gain is orbit-specific (co-moving with the LLL zero, as swarm-i argues) and Ω is only a correlate. Either way the mechanism question (claim 2) gets a sharper answer than the M2 control alone gives.
- **Stage 2 (~30 evals)**, only if stage 1 supports the hypothesis: push the flattening further than P₁ alone can (P₁ + designed complement toward Ω rel.std ≲ 0.8), inside the campaign boundary.
