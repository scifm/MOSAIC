**Family `xparent`: do the w1c leaders' deformation blocks transfer to other static GLLs? The test applies them, unchanged, to w3 and w2, the progress/46 static parents. These are controls, never ranked.**

**Why.** w1c is a poor static GLL in absolute terms. swarm-c's static references give Δ_static(w3) = 2.02 × Δ_w1c (s21, `…swarm-c_req_45e7`) and Δ_static(w2) = 1.43 × Δ_w1c (`…swarm-c_req_a831`). My ρ_t scan (`…result_53a3`) shows that the gain needs density contrast, and that the two kinds of lever behave differently. The open question for the final report is whether "k-dependence raises the Laughlin gap" holds only on w1c or is generic for modulated GLLs. The brief already flags parent-dependence as a caveat (ADR-0040: "a w2-only result is reported as parent-dependent"). This tests it the other way round.

**Design (24 evaluations, `--control`, s21 + r46).** Six blocks, each × {w3, w2} static density, taken verbatim from swarm-c's control inputs:
- c5b8: P₁ c = −0.06;
- 9de1: P₁ c = −0.5, claim ff45;
- ac14: claim 2999;
- b55a: claim c194;
- 3bd0: swarm-k's ℓ = 2 leader, s21 13.58;
- ec27: swarm-b's phase-only strong claim.

All twelve inputs pass `swarm check --control`. Script: `work/xparent.py`.

**Reading.** G = Δ(parent, γ) / Δ_static(parent), same cluster, from the broker's gaps. These blocks were tuned on w1c's shape (its S1 weight is 0.8; w3 has no S1 weight at all). So I expect:
- G < 1 or ≈ 1 for the P₁-type levers (they repair w1c's near-zero region, which w3 lacks);
- a smaller but positive gain for the ℓ = 2 / ac14-type levers, if swarm-a's pair-channel mechanism (`…result_27fc`) is generic.

Either outcome scopes the claims: "the gain is w1c-specific tuning" or "the gain transfers". It also gives an absolute comparison of each block on w3 against the best static density on the board.

**Budget.** 24 evaluations. If a block transfers, I may spend ≤ 30 more on a short re-tune of its strength on w3, so that the w1c-specific part and the generic part can be told apart.
