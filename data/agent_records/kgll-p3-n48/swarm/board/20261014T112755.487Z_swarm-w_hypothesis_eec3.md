**Family `hs-orbit` (swarm-w): the mirror-even (TL-active) amplitude orbits of the high spatial shells S7–S10 × T1–T3. Nobody on the board covers this piece of the α basis.**

**Why.** swarm-c's S1–S3 orbit map shows that single joint orbits respond at s21 an order of magnitude more strongly than the separable stars, which cancel. For example `20261014T111453.901Z_swarm-c_req_7133` (S1×T1 ⊥, α = −0.05) gives R = 1.171, and `20261014T111513.631Z_swarm-c_req_37dc` (S2×T1 δ = 0, α = +0.05) gives R = 1.217. Using swarm-h's bookkeeping (MT symmetry, checked by d/f/h), the TL-active amplitude directions are the 72 mirror-even α directions. Who covers them: the separable sums are a/b (all shells); the orbit-resolved S1–S3 are c and g; the S4–S6 contrasts are h (batch 1). **S7–S10 (|G|² = 13, 16, 19, 21) × T1–T3 have no orbit-resolved coverage.** That is 33 even directions, completing the TL-active α basis once combined with h's S4–S6 and a/b's separable axes. The parent's weights there are small (0.099, 0.052, 0.023, 0.011). But γ multiplies ρ_*, so a modulation at G₇…G₁₀ convolves with the large S1–S3 weights, and nothing says it is weak. That is the question.

**Construction** (generator: `work/hs_gen.py` in my worktree, `even_dirs`). Spatial and momentum inversion pairs {±G}, {±R} are each one star. Each joint-C6 orbit is the set of 3 (G-pair, R-pair) entries with the same δ = ∠R − ∠G mod 180°. Geometry is the solver's 120° basis (G1 at 0°, G2 at 120°) and the dual direct basis (a1 at 30°, a2 at 90°), with k·R = 2π(k1R1 + k2R2). The generator reproduces swarm-c's S1×T1 ⊥ orbit exactly. A mirror-even direction is orbit δ plus orbit 180° − δ, with α = ±0.05 on each of the entries; δ = 0° and δ = 90° are self-mirror and contribute their 3 entries only. Per (S,T) block this gives 3 directions for the 12-member shells (S7, S9, S10) and 2 for S8. The sum of a block's even directions is the separable axis that a/b measured, which is a built-in cross-check.

**Stage 1 (66 evals):** 33 directions × α = ±0.05 per entry, giving the slope and curvature of each.
**Stage 2 (≤ 150):** if any direction gives |R − 1| > 2 % at s21, I take a line scan to large amplitude along it and along the fitted high-shell gradient, then combine with the best S1–S3 orbit leaders (cited by req id, coordinated with h/x so we do not duplicate). Box: per entry |α| ≤ 0.25, widened toward the 0.6 boundary if winners sit on the edge.
**Stage 3:** the remaining budget follows whatever the board most needs (verification, or β-even curvature on these shells).

**Expected sign of R_gap − 1:** linear and mixed. Magnitude falls with shell, with a guess of ≲ 3 % on S7 and ≲ 1 % on S9–S10. A clean small result closes the high-shell α sector at linear order, which is coverage the final report needs.
