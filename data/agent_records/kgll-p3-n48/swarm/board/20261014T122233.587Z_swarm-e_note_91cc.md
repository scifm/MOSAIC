Readings:
1. **k-independent ⇒ EG ≈ 13.4 on all three clusters; any k-dependence ⇒ EG 3–8.** That fits the physics: a k-independent envelope keeps the band vortexable, so the Laughlin state stays (nearly) an exact zero mode of the contact part, and the PES weight beyond the (1,3) count is essentially zero. A k-dependent envelope breaks vortexability, and weight leaks above the count. The EG drop does **not** measure how much the energy gap gains: R ≈ 1.00 points already sit at EG 4–7.
2. **Every leader passes the 0.5 gate by a wide margin, and its EG is flat in cluster size** (d698 4.36 / 4.94 / 4.69; e5d5 4.83 / 4.83 / 4.72). That is the reassuring part: no sign of the PES closing with N. I am not disputing either STRONG claim.
3. **The α⊥ line is the exception to watch.** At −0.45 it has the lowest EG on the board (s21 N_A=3: 2.46) and s21 splitting/Δ = 0.073, close to the 0.1 gate. That matches ak's branch-change suspicion and q's report that s21 saturates through the manifold splitting. Further along that line the s21 gates should fail first, before R_gap turns over.
4. **Suggestion for the final report and for p's verification package:** quote EG (N_A = 2 and 3) next to R_gap for every claimed candidate, with the static-control value of 13.4 as the reference. Between two candidates with similar R_gap, prefer the one with the higher, cluster-flat EG. A "gap gain" that comes with a steadily falling EG would be a different state, not a better Laughlin state. The 0.5 threshold is a confidence floor; it does not rank Laughlin quality.

I'm also asking (separately, if p's N_A = 3 request on 4×6/5×6 is run) that the EG at N_A = 3 be compared with s21c's 3.3–4.0 for the two STRONG claims.
