**To the human: (1) the conv46 rung is not independent of r46 for pure LL0 here, so the claims rest on two rectangular clusters plus s21. (2) Please consider a fourth cluster shape (e.g. the 27-site triangular, N = 9) for the final candidates.**

**(1) What conv46 adds.** For the "strong" claim `20261014T111452.976Z_swarm-c_req_ce1f`, the r46 and conv46 runs have different input hashes (`fc64640ad396` vs `7f2ed9c86725`: qmax 8→10, n_mesh 48→64, g_cutoff 8→10). Their gaps are 2.7923621820e-4 and 2.7923623320e-4, a relative difference of 5e-8 (from `swarm show`). The parent reference also has conv46 Δ = r46 Δ to the printed digits. swarm-c's kernel check (20261014T113319.979Z_swarm-c_result_bc24) agrees: weight outside the box is ≤ 4e-5. So for pure LL0 at d_s = 0.1 the convergence rung is converged by construction. It is effectively a third copy of the 4×6 number, and gives no independent evidence. That is fine as a numerical check, but the claim table should not read it as a fourth confirmation.

**(2) Why a fourth cluster matters.** The finite-size risk is the one that has bitten before. In doc 40 §4.6 the same deformation gave +0.51 % on 4×6 and −0.82 to −1.25 % on 5×6, 3×9 and the 27-site triangular cluster: 4×6 was the outlier. The current leaders are large (+15 to +40 %), and s21/r46/r56 agree in sign so far, so a reversal is unlikely. But:
- every leader is a joint (G,R) orbit at a specific angle, and swarm-d/f/h showed that **none of the three clusters is mirror-symmetric**, and the MT-image pairs differ by up to 2.4 % on s21 (20261014T113200.342Z_swarm-h_note_4bf9);
- r46 and r56 share the same b2 sampling (j/6) and differ only in b1. A cluster with a different point group (the 27-site triangular has C6, like s21, and different K/M content, cf. swarm-y's fsmatch) would test the angle-specific k-sampling that these orbits depend on.
