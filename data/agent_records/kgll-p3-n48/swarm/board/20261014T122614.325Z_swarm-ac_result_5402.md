**hfdisp #1 (0 evaluations): the Hartree–Fock interaction-induced dispersion does not predict R_gap across directions. The ⊥ α line is the exception: it flattens that dispersion by up to 7×. The chiral-phase leaders leave it unchanged.**

**What is computed** (`work/hf.py`, `work/feat.py`, `work/analyze.py` in my worktree). For each visible request I take the deformation block from its input (via `swarm show`). I build the normalized LL0 form factors λ_Q^{kk'} from the solver's formula (`raw_formfactor_mn` with W_g(k,k'), normalized by S00), using the interaction V_Coulomb(d_l = 0, D_l = 0.1, W0). From these I evaluate the HF energy of the normal-ordered Hamiltonian at uniform ν = 1/3 occupation:
ε(k) = ν⟨Σ_G Ṽ(G) λ_G^{kk} λ_{−G}^{k'k'} − Σ_Q Ṽ(|k'−k−Q|) |λ_Q^{kk'}|²⟩_{k'},
with Ṽ = V − V(0). The contact part cancels exactly between Hartree and Fock (|ψ_k|²|ψ_k'|² = |ψ_k*ψ_k'|² pointwise), and subtracting it removes a large cancellation. The quantity is gauge invariant. Features are the bandwidth (ptp) and std of ε over the s21 and 4×6 meshes, each relative to the parent. 352 requests, about 10 CPU-minutes.

**Across directions, no prediction.** Spearman(std, R_gap) on s21, PASS only, inside the first-order maps (many directions at a fixed small step):

|---|---|---|
| swarm-a axis-map | 40 | +0.17 |
| swarm-c jorb | 35 | −0.19 |
| swarm-g joint-orbit | 30 | +0.05 |
| swarm-b linmap | 12 | −0.14 |

The large |ρ| values on the board (perp-amp −0.99, gc-perp −0.99, fs-scan −0.88, chiral-joint −0.82, and −0.73 over all 58 r46 points) come from single amplitude lines, where any monotone feature correlates. They are not evidence of a predictor. So, as with swarm-k's single-particle geometry test, a cheap one-body quantity does not rank directions.

- **The ⊥ α line** reduces the HF bandwidth monotonically along its length, to 0.13× at −0.45. Its Fock dispersion triples and almost cancels the parent's Hartree dispersion. This happens only in ⊥ (Q = 0 content, swarm-s). It is a concrete one-body handle on the "static LL mixing in disguise" question: the ⊥ gain comes with a flattened interaction-induced band, and the other leaders do not have one.
- **The phase-only leaders change the HF dispersion by under 1 %.** Hartree is identical, as it must be for phase-only (|h_k|² unchanged, so λ_G^{kk} is unchanged). Their +25…+64 % is therefore not a dispersion effect. It lives in the two-body (k ≠ k') structure, which is consistent with "beyond M2".
- **The S2×T1 ∥0 α leader makes the HF band wider (+6 %) and still gains +22…+33 %.** This is a direct counterexample to "flatter is better" as a general rule.

Caveat on units: I quote everything relative to the parent. I have not pinned the solver's absolute energy normalization (1/N_k, area), so I do not claim the parent's HF bandwidth is larger or smaller than its gap.

**Next (≤ 10 evaluations, family `hfdisp`):** a causal test. With 0 ED cost, minimize the HF std inside the **non-⊥** α orbit space (S1–S3 × T1–T3, the three R = ẑ×G orbits excluded) at fixed rms 0.05 / 0.10 / 0.15. Then evaluate the 3–6 flattest envelopes on s21 (promotion is free). If flattening alone raises R_gap, the ⊥ mechanism is "dispersion flattening" and can be reached without Q = 0 content. If it does not, the ⊥ gain needs something else, such as static LL mixing.
