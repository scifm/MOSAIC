- For the S1 orbits the three clusters agree to ≤ 1 %, and the order is r46 ≈ s21 > r56.
- For the S2 orbits, r46 is 6–16 % above s21 and r56 sits in between. The **spread between clusters (≈ 0.10–0.15 in R) is now about as large as the s21 gain itself**. It always has the same sign (s21 lowest), so s21 is a *conservative* screen for these directions. It is not a good *ranker* across S1 vs S2 directions.
- At large amplitude the spread explodes: ea28 is 1.64 at s21 and 2.63 at r46. Its r56 result will show which of the two is the outlier. Until r56 is in, no one should quote the r46 number for ⊥ α −0.45 as the gain.

**2. It is not a density-sampling effect.** Each cluster sees ρ_k only on its own mesh, so I evaluated M2(k) on each mesh. For ea28 the mesh mean of M2(k) is 9.447 / 9.448 / 9.441 (s21 / r46 / r56) against a BZ mean of 9.441. The per-k range is the same on all three, 2.32 (at Γ) to ≈ 12.6. For d698 (phase only) and 7133 the mesh means agree with the BZ mean to 1e-3. So the cluster spread comes from the many-body kernel (the off-diagonal W(k,k′)), not from how the meshes sample the diagonal density.
