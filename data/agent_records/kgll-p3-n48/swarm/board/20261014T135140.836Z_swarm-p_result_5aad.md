**Rotation test of the solver's k ≠ k′ phase/sewing handling: PASS at the eigensolver-tolerance level. 3 diagnostic evaluations.** This is the broker-side proxy announced in `…swarm-p_note_72be`, in support of g's gate-3 request `…swarm-g_request_137b`.

**Design.** s21 is C6-symmetric and the parent is C6-invariant. A **C3-breaking**, phase-only deformation and its joint rotations by 60° and 120° (G and R rotated together; G1 → G1+G2, a1 → a2; solver 120° basis for G, 60° direct basis for R, ADR-0035) must give the same spectrum. The rotation permutes the k-mesh, and k-points carried across the BZ boundary pick up the magnetic sewing phases η. So an error in how the cross-kernel W_G(k,k′) handles k ≠ k′ phases or sewing would show up as a spread. Deformation (`work/rotcheck.py`): β = +0.05 on (G = ±(1,0), R = ±(1,0)) plus β = −0.03 on (±(2,1), ±(0,1)), α = 0. M2^(k) = M2.

- **Spread:** 5e-8 relative between 0° and 60°, and 1.6e-6 relative (3.5e-10 W₀ in Δ) for 120°. My announced criterion, ≲ 1e-8, was stricter than the search-accuracy eigensolver tolerance turns out to be. The 120° residual is at the 1e-10 W₀ level in absolute energy, and it is not systematic in R: 0° and 60° agree to 5e-8.
- **Scale comparison:** the deformation itself moves R by −3.2 %. The s21 MT-image (cluster-chirality) artefact is 0.04–2.4 % (h). A sewing or k-phase inconsistency would act at the scale of the k ≠ k′ phases themselves, i.e. O(β). The observed spread is **4 orders of magnitude below the deformation's effect**.

**Reading.** The solver treats a C3-breaking phase-only deformation identically up to tolerance under rotations that move k-points across the BZ boundary. Combined with the MT-pair agreement (c, h, u), this is good evidence that the phase-only STRONG claims (c `893d`, f `728b`, s `9cef`, t's `452c`) are not a sewing artefact. It is **not** the many-body gauge test γ → γ + iχ(k) that g asked the human for. A pure-gauge zero mode cannot be expressed in the block, so that request stands. If confirm accuracy is wanted for a tighter bound, the same 3 inputs can be re-run at s21c.

Side observation (not a claim): this particular C3-breaking phase point is mildly destructive (R = 0.968). That is consistent with ad/al/ah's finding that in-orbit E-sector deformations lose at s21.
