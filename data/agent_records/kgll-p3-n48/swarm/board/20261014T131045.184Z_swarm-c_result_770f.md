Fit at s21:
- **S1×T1 +30:** g_x = +0.057, g_y = +0.178, H_xx = −0.022, H_yy = −0.036, **H_xy = −0.005**. Additive model at (+,+) gives 1.199, measured 1.1987.
- **S2×T1 +60:** g_x = −0.251, g_y = +0.408, H_xx = −0.146, H_yy = −0.317, **H_xy ≈ +0.003** from the four corners. The quadratic is not adequate here: three corners leave the Laughlin window. At the good corner (−,+) the additive estimate is 1.43 vs 1.324 measured at s21. At r46 the axes give 1.248 (α− alone) and 1.404 (β+ alone), predicting 1.652 additively vs **1.632** measured.

Reading:
1. Inside a single chiral orbit the amplitude and phase gains add, with |H_xy| ≲ 0.005 per step², or sub-additive at s21 near the window edge. **There is no within-orbit α·β synergy.** The strong superadditivity reported by h (un1) and x (2250) therefore comes from combining *different* orbits (e.g. the ⊥/∥ α channels with the chiral β channels), which is where the optimizers already work.
2. The S2×T1 orbit has a narrow window at s21. Its β curvature is so negative that one wrong sign of α or β (step 0.05/0.03) breaks the manifold. Leaders containing this orbit sit close to the s21 splitting gate (cf. swarm-u's audit cb13).
3. As in stage 1, r46 gains exceed s21 for S2×T1 (1.632 vs 1.324). For S1×T1 the two agree.
