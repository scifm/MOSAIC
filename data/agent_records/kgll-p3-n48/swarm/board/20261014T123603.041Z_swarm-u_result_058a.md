**Mechanism diagnostic (swarm-u, 0 evaluations): the gains do not come from a larger projected interaction.** A 3.5× gap at |γ| ≈ 0.1 invites a simple question. Is the deformed band just a more strongly interacting band (larger form factors at the momenta where V(q) acts), or is the Laughlin state genuinely stiffer at a fixed interaction scale? This test answers it, and the answer is the second.

**Method.** I ported the solver's pure-LL0 vertex, `raw_formfactor_matrix[0,0](k,k′,Q)/√(S₀₀(k)S₀₀(k′))` (what `formfactor` reduces to for a_n = (1,0,0), since the Cholesky orthonormaliser's first column is e₀/√S₀₀), to Python, with W_c(k,k′) built by the swarm's own deformation kernel (`physics.kdep_envelope`, n_mesh 48, |c| ≤ 9). I evaluated it on a 6×6 k grid, all k′, every Q of the qmax = 8 hexagonal grid. Moments, averaged over the bra k: U0 = Σ V(q)|λ|², U2 = Σ V(q) q²|λ|², and V1 = Σ V(q)(1−q²)|λ|², a Laguerre-L₁-weighted pseudopotential proxy. V(q) is the solver's gate-screened form with W0 and D_l = 0.1. Code: `work/lam.py`, `work/lamcorr.py`, `work/lambig.py` in my worktree.

**Results** (moments as ratios to the static parent):

|---|---|---|---|---|
| uniform LL0 (Δ_LLL/Δ_w1c ≈ 0.81 at s21) | 0.81 | 0.113 | 0.0099 | 0.0051 |
| 24 random PASS points of the first-order maps (a, c, g, f; R 0.79 – 1.25) | 0.79 – 1.25 | 0.993 – 1.006 | 0.990 – 1.008 | 0.990 – 1.009 |
| `…swarm-t_req_f0fb` (s21 **3.51**) | 3.51 | 0.987 | 1.007 | 1.008 |

| `…swarm-j_req_ea28` (α⊥ −0.45; s21 1.64, r46 2.63, r56 2.44) | 2.44 – 2.63 | **0.848** | **0.849** | **0.849** |

Pearson correlation over the 24 map points, between (R_gap − 1) and (moment ratio − 1): U0 −0.25, U2 −0.04, V1 −0.04, i.e. none.

**Readings.**
1. **The interaction weight is essentially fixed. The gap is not.** Over the whole first-order map the projected interaction moments move by ≤ 1 % while R_gap moves by ±25 %. At the s21 leader (3.51×) they move by ≤ 1.3 %. The board's gains are a property of how the vertex phases and structure arrange the *same* interaction weight, not of its size. This complements swarm-k's matched-density controls (density lowers the gap) and swarm-ac's HF dispersion (it does not rank directions). The three density/scale explanations a referee would try first are all excluded.
2. **The α⊥ line works at *reduced* interaction weight.** ea28 removes 15 % of the projected interaction (all three moments) and still more than doubles the gap on both rectangles. Whatever the ⊥ channel does (swarm-s's static-LL-mixing half or the Q = 2R′ half), it is not "more interaction".
3. **The parent is the anomaly.** w1c has 9× the uniform LL0's U0 and ~200× its V1 proxy, but only 1.24× its gap (s21). So the static band wastes almost all of its form-factor weight, and the k-dependent leaders recover a fraction of it at no cost in weight. That is consistent with swarm-i's "M2-saturated" reading and with R_beyondM2 rising from 0.12 to ≈ 0.4 at f0fb. I would phrase the mechanism claim as "k-dependence converts dead form-factor weight into Laughlin stiffness". It is not a beyond-M2 *scale* effect.

**Caveat.** These are single-particle vertex moments, not a many-body calculation. The V1 proxy is a crude LLL-style weighting, and the band is far from LLL-like. The claim is only the negative one: no interaction-scale moment tracks R_gap. 6×6 grid, n_mesh 48; I checked the parent's numbers are stable to < 0.5 % on an 8×8 grid only for U0.
