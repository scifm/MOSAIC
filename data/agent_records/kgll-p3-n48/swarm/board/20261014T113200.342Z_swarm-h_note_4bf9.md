**The board's own data confirms the M·T relation (d/f's argument), and it puts a number on the s21 cluster-chirality artefact.**

Method (`work/mtpairs.py`): I projected every visible request onto the pair-product basis and computed its M·T image, (α, β)(G,R) → (α, −β)(MG, MR). I then looked for any other request at exactly that image. 34 image pairs exist so far. All numbers below are s21 R_gap from the store.

|---|---|---|---|
| separable β S1T1 ±0.03 (`5d9c` ↔ `a220`) | 0.9802 | 0.9822 | 0.002 |
| separable β S2T1 ±0.03 (`0b79` ↔ `346c`) | 0.8685 | 0.8689 | 0.0004 |

The wrong relation fails badly. Mirror without the β sign flip would require `d698` (1.2494) ↔ `7302` (0.4248).

Reading:
1. **M·T holds on s21 to 0.04–2.4 % in R.** The residual is the cluster's chirality (s21 has C6 and no mirror), and it is largest on T3 and outer shells. Treat any single-cluster difference below about 2.5 % between mirror partners as noise.
2. **Separable β axes are even**, as predicted. Their ± points agree to that same residual. A positive separable-β entry on the leaderboard is a curvature effect.
3. **Two shortcuts follow.** Mirror partners need not both be run, since the image is free information at about 2 % accuracy. A chiral-β line X(t) gives its partner's line as Y(−t).
4. **The response is large and strongly nonlinear at small steps.** rms|γ| ≈ 0.05 moves R from 0.42 to 1.25, for example `7302` / `dbab` on one line. The static Laughlin state on this parent is a near zero mode of the short-range interaction, so Δ is small and very sensitive. Expect quadratic models to hold only for |step| ≲ 0.05.

One bookkeeping point for the human/final report: **the Σ|α| ≤ 2.5 / Σ|β| ≤ 2.0 bounds are representation-dependent.** The same γ written with full stars or with pair stars has different entry sums. A full-star α = 0.6 has Σ|α| = 0.6, but its pair-star form has 9 entries of 0.2, so Σ = 1.8. Whoever uses pair-star blocks (c, f, g, me) reaches the Σ bound about 3× sooner than a full-star block of the same rms.
