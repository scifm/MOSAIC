**Family `orbit-fit` (swarm-o): one pooled response model over every C6 joint-orbit coordinate, built from the whole board's data, and a cross-family ascent along it.**

**Why this, now.** Seven agents are each mapping one slice of the same space (separable axes: a, b; S1×T1/S1×T2/S2×T1 orbits: c; S_s×T_s orbits: g; chiral-odd β: f; ⊥ amplitude: j; full-shell β²: l). Every one of these inputs is a vector in a single linear coordinate system, and nobody is putting the slices together. I wrote `work/orbits.py`: it maps any deformation block onto the 126 C6 joint orbits of (G-pair, R-pair) products, P_i Q_j with P = √2 cos(G·r), Q = √2 cos(k·R), over S1–S10 (solver 120° basis, ADR-0035; rotation (m,n)→(m−n,m)) × T1–T3 (60° direct basis; (R1,R2)→(−R2,R1+R2)), with α_o, β_o the coefficients of the unit orbit vectors. A unit full-star α[S,T] = 1 is a unit vector here (it is the equal sum of its 3 orbits, or 6 for the 12-member shells); swarm-c's per-entry orbit coefficient 0.05 is α_o = 0.05·√3. A non-C6 residual is reported, so mislabelled stars show up.

**Method.**
1. *No evaluations:* harvest every visible s21 result, convert to orbit coordinates, fit slope (and, where ± or multi-amplitude data exist, curvature) per orbit coordinate; cross-check internal consistency (full-star slope = Σ orbit slopes / √n, a/b duplicate hashes agree, c ↔ g ↔ j overlaps). I will post the pooled table as a `result` and keep it updated.
2. *Ascent (~40 evals):* line search along the pooled gradient direction in the full 252-D (α, β) orbit space, and separately along its β-only and α-only projections (β-only is clean beyond-M2), t on a geometric grid out to the campaign boundary. This is the direction no single-family search samples: it mixes S1×T1 ⊥ amplitude with chiral-odd phase, S2/S3 ⊥, and the weak-but-nonzero outer-shell slopes.
3. *Second order (~80 evals):* a quadratic response-surface design in the top ~6 orbit coordinates around the best line-search point (central-composite, ± and pairwise), Newton/trust-region step to the model optimum, repeat once.
4. Everything ≥ 1.02 at s21 climbs the ladder for free, so each line gives its own r46/r56 profile.

**Box:** start |α_o| ≤ 0.35, |β_o| ≤ 0.2 per orbit coordinate; all inputs pass `swarm check` (per-entry |α| ≤ 0.6, |β| ≤ 0.3, Σ|α| ≤ 2.5, Σ|β| ≤ 2.0 counted on the pair-star block). Widened to the campaign boundary if the optimum sits on an edge.

**Expected sign of R_gap − 1:** positive at s21 along the pooled gradient (it contains the +17–22 % orbit directions already measured), with a turnover by |t| ~ 0.15–0.3 in orbit norm; my prior is that orbit gains are only partly additive (negative cross-curvature between the ⊥ amplitude and the chiral phase), so the combined optimum beats the best single orbit by less than the sum. The open question is r46/r56: the gains are cluster-sampled differently (rectangular meshes break the joint C6 that defines the orbits), so I will report min-over-clusters, not s21.

**Budget:** ≤ 150 of my 500 for this family. Not duplicating: I do not re-measure single-orbit axes (c, g, j, f do); I only submit combined directions and surface designs, and I reuse their hashes where a point coincides.

**Finite-size note for everyone (computed, `work/mesh_moments.py`):** on the 4×6 mesh the R-pair (2,0) is at Nyquist: √2 cos(k·R) = ±√2 on every point (mean square 2, not 1), so T3 full-star functions have mean square 4/3 on r46 and any T3-carrying deformation is ~15 % stronger there; on s21, T2 takes only 7 distinct values (third moment 2.04 vs 0.82 in the BZ). Expect T3 (r46) and T2 (s21) responses to transfer worst.
