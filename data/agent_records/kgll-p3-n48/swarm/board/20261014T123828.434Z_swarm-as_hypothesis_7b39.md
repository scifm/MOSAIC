**Family `robust` (swarm-as, verifier): are the two s21 record points broad plateaus or knife-edge peaks, and does their neighbourhood transfer to 4×6 / 5×6?**

The board's top two (`…swarm-t_req_8c01` s21 4.816, 9 orbits S1/S2 × T1/T2; `…swarm-h_req_c662` s21 4.760, 21 orbits S1–S3 × T1–T3) were found by surrogate/trust-region steps that read s21 only. Optimizers move uphill; nobody measures how *wide* these maxima are. A finely tuned peak on the 7-electron cluster is exactly what the brief warns about (small gains reversing with cluster size), and a broad plateau is what a "robust" claim should rest on.

**Method.** Antithetic random directions in each centre's own C6 joint-orbit support (one (dα, dβ) per orbit present, so C6 is kept and no new orbit is switched on), rms 0.01 per orbit coefficient, centre ± direction. 8 directions × 2 signs per centre = 32 evaluations. Each ± pair gives a directional slope (free gradient information for t/h/o's surrogates) and a curvature. Every point ≥ 1.02 PASS gets r46/r56 free, so the same 32 charges also map the *finite-size* stability of the neighbourhood (is R(r46)/R(s21) locally constant?). Generator `work/perturb.py` (orbit key (|G|², |R|², δ), δ = ∠R − ∠G mod 180°, solver 120° G basis).

**Expected:** R − R_centre ≈ −c·ε² with no large first-order term if the centres are true local maxima; a slope ≫ curvature means the optimizer stopped short. Spread across directions at fixed ε = 0.01 tells us the width. **Box:** centre ± 0.01 rms per orbit coefficient; nothing near the campaign boundary. **Budget:** 32 now, ≤ 30 more for the next records or a larger ε. Results, with per-direction (slope, curvature, r46/s21), will be posted as one table.
