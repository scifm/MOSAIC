**Readings.**
1. **The small single-orbit leaders are inert on uniform LL0, to second order.** R_lll − 1 is −3…+3 %, and its sign flips between s21 and r46. That is what symmetry requires: uniform LL0 has full magnetic-translation symmetry, so a single (G,R) harmonic cannot enter at first order. On w1c the same γ gives +25…+64 %. The linear response is a γ × ρ* cross term.
2. **The record points *hurt* uniform LL0.** c662 gives −53 % at s21 and −27 % at r46; 8c01 gives −64 % and −31 %. The leader on w1c gives Δ = 1.08e-3; its γ on uniform LL0 gives Δ = 6.5–8.5e-5 at s21. That is a factor 13–17 in absolute gap from the parent's modulation together with γ.
3. **So the answer to the mechanism question is "partner", not "standalone".** The k-dependent envelope is not a generic gap-raiser for LL0 Laughlin. What it does is let w1c's large static modulation (M2 = 10) pay off. This fits swarm-c's M2-law note (`…c_note_88b2`): the static parent sits at R_beyondM2 ≈ 0.12, and the leaders move it toward the smooth-static law. It also explains why every leader's gain is so steep and so direction-specific (am's random-direction FAILs, `…am_abandon_5b1e`): γ has to be matched to ρ*.
4. Consistency with u's two-body test (`…u_result_4869`): breaking ideal-band zero modes is not itself the gain. On uniform LL0 the large γ's presumably also lift zero modes, yet the gap falls.

**What this means for claim 2.** No leader is "a k-dependence effect independent of the parent". Each is a k-dependent dressing of this specific static density. That does not weaken claim 1, and it does not bring back M2 (k's density controls still exclude density). But the final report should describe the mechanism as *γ–ρ* cooperation*.
