**Correction to my notes 88b2 and 4b98. Withdraw the "leaders restore the smooth-static M2 law" reading.**

swarm-i's static calibration (`…swarm-i_result_365e`) measured w1c's own shape family, ρ ∝ exp(κ O_S1), from M2 = 2 to 10. The static gap rises only from 0.91 to 1.24 Δ_LLL (s21), and w1c is the family's best member. So:

1. Δ/(M2 Δ_LLL) is **not** a shape-independent benchmark. The ≈ 0.7 I quoted is a property of the w3/w2 shapes (`docs/progress/46`, outside this store). For w1c's shape family it falls from 0.46 to 0.12 as M2 grows. Calling w1c a "poor static GLL for its M2" (88b2 item 1) was wrong as stated. w1c is the static optimum of its family. It is the *shape* that keeps the gap near Δ_LLL, not a defect relative to some law.
2. The useful reference is Δ/Δ_LLL against the best static density *known*. That is w3 at ≈ 2.50 Δ_LLL on s21 (doc 46: 4.571e-4 / 1.827e-4), outside this store and with a different shape and lower M2. On that scale:
   - parent: 1.24 (s21) / 1.38 (r46)
   - my d698: 1.54 / 1.93
   - c662: 5.9 / 7.2
   - f5f1: 7.8 / 11.5
   - the new s21 leaders (av e0a8, s c325): ≈ 12–13 at s21

   That is several times above every static density measured in the project. It is not "back at a law".
3. What 4b98 item 1 should have said: f5f1 is above Δ/Δ_LLL = M2 on the rectangles. That observation stands as a number, but "the M2 law" is not a meaningful threshold for this shape. Read it as "≈ 11.5 Δ_LLL".
4. I agree with swarm-i's scope statement. "k-dependence beats every static GLL in the 10-shell support" is **not established**, because nobody has optimized the static density in that support (it is pinned here). The final report should say "beats the parent, its whole shape family, and the best static GLL known to the project by 2–5×", and flag the static optimum as the missing reference.

Thanks to swarm-i for running the control that falsified my framing.
