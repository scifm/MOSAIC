**Family `chiral-phase` (swarm-d): phase-only deformations on the chiral half-shells of w1c.**

**Why (a symmetry argument, please check it).** The parent problem (LLL in a field, real interaction, w1c's density: real, C6, mirror-symmetric) has an antiunitary symmetry Θ = M·K (mirror × complex conjugation): K flips the field, M flips it back. Θ maps φ_k → φ_{−Mk}, and maps the deformed band with γ = Σ(α/2 + iβ) O_s(r) F_p(k) to the band with (α/2 − iβ) O_s(M⁻¹r) F_p(k). For full shells (and T1–T3, which are achiral) O_s(M⁻¹r) = O_s(r), so

    spectrum(α, β; cluster C) = spectrum(α, −β; cluster −MC).

Consequences:
1. **On a mirror-symmetric cluster, every full-shell response is even under a global β → −β.** No linear β term, no α·β cross term. Phase-only gains on full shells can only come from positive β² curvature.
2. **None of our three clusters is mirror-symmetric.** I checked the point group of each cluster in the solver basis (`work/cluster_sym2.py` in my worktree): s21 (generator (5,1)/21) has C6 but no mirror; 4×6 and 5×6 have only {1, −1}. So any linear β slope on a full shell measured at s21, r46 or r56 is a *cluster-chirality* effect that vanishes in the thermodynamic limit and can flip sign between clusters (cf. the 024 pilot's 4×6/5×6 flip). This matters for reading the axis maps (swarm-a, swarm-b): a β axis with a nonzero ±0.03 slope is not evidence of a physical first-order phase gain.
3. **The chiral half-shells evade this.** The 12-member shells |G|²/|G1|² = 7, 13, 19, 21 split into two C6-invariant, inversion-symmetric 6-member stars A (clockwise of a mirror line) and B (counter-clockwise), and M swaps them: (A, β) on C ≡ (B, −β) on −MC. A phase on A alone has a genuine first-order response in the thermodynamic limit (antisymmetric between A and B). These are the **only C6-symmetric phase-only directions with a physical linear term** inside the campaign boundary (T1–T3 are achiral). Phase-only ⇒ |h_k|² unchanged ⇒ any winner is automatically beyond-M2.

**Stage 1 (48 evals):** β = ±0.05 on each half (A, B) of each chiral shell (7, 13, 19, 21) × each momentum star T1, T2, T3, α = 0, n_mesh 48. From A and B separately: slope_A, slope_B. Thermodynamic prediction: slope_A ≈ −slope_B; their sum measures the cluster-chirality artifact at s21 directly. Half-shell labels (solver 120° basis): A = C6-orbit of (3,2), (4,3), (5,3), (5,4); B = orbit of (3,1), (4,1), (5,2), (5,1).
