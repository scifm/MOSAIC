**Readings**
1. **s_opt(t) ≈ 0.5, 0.75, 0.75–1 and ≥ 1 at t = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, close to √t** (0.50, 0.71, 0.87, 1). `dwave` posits γ ∝ (functional of log ρ_*) × F_T1(k). Since log ρ_t ≈ t(ρ_w1c − 1) at small t, that would give s_opt ∝ t. At t = 0.25 the data reject this: s = 0.25 (0.87 / 0.99) loses to s = 0.5 (1.07 / 1.24).
2. **√t is what you get if the gain is even in γ and linear in the modulation.** Model: Δ/Δ_LLL − M2-term ≈ c₁·t·s² − c₂·s⁴, i.e. a modulation-assisted gain quadratic in γ against a non-ideality cost quartic in γ. That gives s_opt² ∝ t. swarm-i (`…result_9c6d`) measures the even-in-γ share of R − 1 at the leaders as 76–86 %, which agrees. A linear-in-γ (handedness) term would push s_opt toward ∝ t. The data say that term is the minority.
3. **With s at its optimum, RbM2 > 1 at every t > 0.** The best RbM2 is about 1.07 / 1.24 (t = 0.25), 1.35 / 1.53 (0.5), 1.57 / 1.63 (0.75) and 1.56 / 1.65 (1). The excess over the static M2 law grows with the modulation and then saturates. At t = 0 (uniform LLL), s = 1 is destructive (tscan 2/3). Small-s probes at t = 0 are going in now to close that end.
4. **For a claim or the final report:** the frontier mechanism is a *modulation-assisted, mostly even-in-γ* effect. Its size is set by t. A leader optimised on w1c transfers to other modulated parents once the amplitude is rescaled by about √t. That is a structural statement about the k-GLL space beyond this one parent.
