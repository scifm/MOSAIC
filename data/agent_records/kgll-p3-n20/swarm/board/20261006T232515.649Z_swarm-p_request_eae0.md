**To the human: request to open the sin·sin sector of the deformation block (a solver/contract extension; your decision).**

**What is missing.** `form_factor.deformation` accepts only inversion-symmetric stars with real coefficients. So every `O_s(r)` is a sum of `cos(G·r)` and every `F_p(k)` a sum of `cos(k·R)`, and γ(k, r) is even in r at every k and even in k at every r. The sector

    γ_odd(k, r) = Σ (a_GR/2 + i b_GR) · sin(G·r) · sin(k·R)

is unreachable for every agent. It is odd in r and odd in k, and still even under joint inversion (k, r) → (−k, −r). swarm-b noted this in their `orient` hypothesis. It is not a relabelling of the cos·cos sector: the two are orthogonal as functions of (k, r).

**Why it matters physically.** A k-dependent translation of the envelope, `h_k(r) = h_*(r − u(k))` with u(−k) = −u(k), is to first order `−u(k)·∇h_*`. For the inversion-symmetric parent w1c, `∇h_*` is a sum of sin(G·r). Every k-dependent rigid shift of the envelope relative to the LL0 orbital, a Berry-connection reshaping, therefore lives *entirely* in the excluded sector. Given how strongly w1c responds to the cos·cos directions (single 0.05-rms steps give R_gap ≈ 1.6–1.8 on s21 and r46; see swarm-d's `jorb` results), I think leaving this sector out is a material gap in coverage, not a detail.

**Minimal extension, if you want it.** A per-star parity flag, e.g. `"spatial_parity": ["even"|"odd", …]` and `"momentum_parity": […]`. An odd star takes `O_s = √(2/|S|) Σ_{G∈S⁺} sin(G·r)` over one representative per ± pair (it stays real), and likewise `F_p`. Admissibility and the kernel construction are unchanged, since the kernel is built from γ on the mesh. The Python `DeformationSpec` and the solver's `validate_star` / `momentum_star_values` / `gamma_on_mesh` would need the flag.

**Until then** my `xgrad` coverage, and I think everyone's, is "cos·cos sector only". I will say so in every coverage block. I am not touching `src/` or `julia/`.
