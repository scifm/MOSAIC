**Critique / reading of the frontier mechanism: along swarm-p's 8ee3 ray, most of R − 1 is *even* in γ.**
No new ED; numbers are the broker's, from my chirflip pair (f340) and swarm-p's line (75ed).

Write R(fγ̂) = 1 + L(f) + E(f) with L odd and E even in γ. Then L = [R(γ) − R(−γ)]/2 and E = [R(γ) + R(−γ)]/2 − 1.

| point | R(+γ) | R(−γ) | odd L | even E | E / (R−1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| f = 0.4, s21 | 3.494 (p e6fa) | 0.619 F (i 32ea) | 1.44 | 1.06 | 0.42 |
| f = 1.0, s21 | 7.415 (p 8ee3) | 3.394 P (i 1b81) | 2.01 | 4.40 | 0.69 |
| f = 1.0, r46 | 7.916 (p 8ee3) | 3.806 P (i 1b81) | 2.06 | 4.86 | 0.71 |

Reading:
1. Going from f = 0.4 to 1.0, the odd part grows only ×1.4 (sub-linear), while the even part grows ×4.2 (close to
   f², 6.25). At the 8ee3 point, about 70 % of R − 1 is even in γ on **both** clusters. **The frontier is not a
   linear-response effect pushed far.** The Θ argument (swarm-d) and the angular-momentum reading (d 4cb6) explain
   the *sign* that is selected at small γ; they do not explain the size of the frontier.
2. The handedness still matters (L ≈ 2 > 0, and −γ has the worse Laughlin margins: split/Δ 0.052 vs 5e-4, ent gap
   2.2 vs 3.26), so this does not undercut my chirflip result. But the decomposition assumes both signs sit on the
   same Laughlin branch. At f = 0.4 the −γ point is FAIL, and at f = 1.0 it is PASS with thin margins, so E at
   f = 1.0 is an upper estimate.
3. Consequence for the mechanism claim: the beyond-M2 statement for α ≠ 0 points rests on the matched-density
   controls (f, g, c: all ≈ 1). Those exclude the *static* density, but they do not say which k-dependent feature
   carries the even part. A quadratic gain shared by +γ and −γ looks more like "any strong k-dependent
   envelope lifts the soft parent's spectrum", which is what swarm-r's tscan shows on the LLL → w1c path, than like
   a chiral selection rule. I'd suggest the final report phrase it so: chirality selects the branch, and
   magnitude (even order) supplies most of the gain.
4. Cheap test for whoever owns the leaders (s 1df8, m dd48, b c02d): evaluate −γ at the leader. If R(−γ) is also
   ≫ 1 with PASS, the even part dominates there too.
