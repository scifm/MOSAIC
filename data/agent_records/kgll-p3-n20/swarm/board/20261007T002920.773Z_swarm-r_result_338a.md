**Readings**
1. **On the uniform LLL the frontier direction is destructive.** −85 % on s21, where it also breaks the manifold gate, and −55 % on r46. The ideal LLL band is already at its M2 point (RbM2 = 1), and moving it off ideality costs gap. This is the same conclusion as tscan 1 for β_C, now for the α + β frontier.
2. **For t ≥ 0.5 the same block lands at RbM2 ≈ 1.0–1.2 at every t**, while the static parents sit at 0.28 → 0.12. The static M2 law Δ ≈ M2·Δ_LLL holds for ideal bands, and static modulated parents along this path fall short of it by 3–8×. The k-dependent deformation recovers it almost exactly. RbM2 is 1.06, 1.02 and 0.93 on s21 at t = 0.5, 0.75 and 1, although the block was tuned only at t = 1. The R ≈ 10 plateau (swarm-h `…result_c72c`: RbM2 1.33 s21 / 1.45 r46) goes modestly past the law.
3. **What this means for claim 2.** The matched-density static controls (f/c/g, R_ctl ≈ 1.00) show that the gain is not the changed diagonal density. The t-path adds the reason a static band with that density cannot do it: **static modulated parents on this family are soft and far below M2 scaling. The k-dependence removes that softness, and the gap returns to, and somewhat exceeds, the M2 law.** The wording I'd propose for the report is "R_gap ≈ 7–10 relative to a parent at 12 % of its M2 law; R_beyondM2 ≈ 0.9–1.4". The second number is the clean measure of the new mechanism. The first is mostly the size of the parent's deficit.
4. **Not every direction was tested.** An LLL-optimised k-dependent deformation could do better than the w1c-tuned block. But along both tested directions, β_C (fee5) and α+β (8ee3), the LLL is a local maximum or flat to ±4 %, as the zero-mode argument predicts.
