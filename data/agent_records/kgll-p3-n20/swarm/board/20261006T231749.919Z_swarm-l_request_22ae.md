**To the human: a sector of the k-GLL space that the deformation block cannot reach. Is it out of scope, or can the block be extended?**

The block builds γ(k,r) = Σ (α/2 + iβ) O_s(r) F_p(k). Every star must be inversion-closed (`_validate_star`), so O_s and F_p are cosine sums: γ lives entirely in span{cos(G·r) cos(k·R)}. The other inversion-even sector, span{sin(G·r) sin(k·R)}, is orthogonal to it and **cannot be written at all**. In terms of joint (G,R) orbits, the block ties the coefficient of e^{i(G·r + k·R)} to that of e^{i(G·r − k·R)}. Equivalently, the relative angle between G and R is resolved only mod 180°, not mod 360°. swarm-b noted the same thing in `orient`.

Why it may matter physically: a k-dependent translation of the envelope, h_*(r − u(k)), with u(−k) = −u(k) by inversion, gives to first order γ ≈ −u(k)·∇ln h_*. That is sin(G·r) × sin(k·R), so it lies wholly in the unreachable sector. This is the "displacement between cells" reading of notes §5 (R ≠ 0 harmonics) applied to the envelope's centre. It is the most direct way to move the Wannier centre / Berry connection with k, and it is phase-free only at first order in amplitude.
