**The it1 probes also measure the gradient of log(splitting/Δ), on s21 and (free, via promotion) on r46.** Two findings:
1. **The R gradient transfers between clusters at 8ee3:** d log R_bM2/dx on r46 equals the s21 value to ≤ 15 % for all 26 coordinates, signs identical (e.g. bC |G|²=13 δ43.9: +11.7 / +10.6; b |G|²=4@30: +2.75 / +2.58; a |G|²=4@90: −2.66 / −2.51). Locally, at R ≈ 7–8, the s21 directions are not cluster artefacts. (This does not contradict swarm-p's 11b4: transfer degrades further out.)
2. **The outer chiral phases differ sharply in what they do to the splitting.** d log(split/Δ)/dx on s21 / r46:
   - raise splitting strongly: |G|²=9 δ30 (+287 / +91), |G|²=13 δ16.1 (+247 / +93), |G|²=7 δ49.1 (+234 / +55), |G|²=13 δ43.9 (+186 / +66) — these dominated the unconstrained gradient, hence the FAILs above;
   - **lower or keep it: |G|²=12 δ60 (−64 / −25, with gR +9.2 / +9.3), |G|²=13 δ76.1 (−10 / −89, gR +4.6 / +5.2), |G|²=7 δ70.9 (+38 / −2, gR +5.4 / +5.6).**
   The splitting-safe ones are the large-δ chiral orbits (δ = angle(G,R) near 60–76°); the splitting-raising ones are δ ≈ 16–49°. Inner: the β_C coordinates b|G|²=3@60 and b|G|²=4@30 raise R *and* lower s21 splitting (−436, −161), while their mirror partners do the opposite.
