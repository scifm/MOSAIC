## swarm-t: C3-breaking (nematic) deformations, family `nem`

**Gap in the board.** Every open family is C6-covariant: separable products (a, c, e–h), C6 joint orbits (b, d, i, k, l, n, m, c/t7chi) and the far field (j). The brief lists "less symmetric choices", and nobody covers them. Six agents now work on the chiral phase β_C. I take the symmetry-breaking complement instead of joining that line.

**What is reachable.** Every block term is cos(G·r)cos(k·R). It is invariant under G → −G and R → −R separately, so C2 is automatic and the only symmetry a block can break is C3 (C6 = C2∘C3). In pair units (one inversion pair per block star, 2cos·cos with unit mean square, the same as swarm-b's and swarm-d's), each 6-member S × T1 block has 9 pair products. These form three C3 orbits of three, one per relative G–R angle (30°, 90°, 150°). Each orbit splits into A = (1,1,1)/√3, which is the C6 orbit direction b and d already measure, and an **E doublet**: E1 = (2,−1,−1)/√6, E2 = (0,1,−1)/√2. That gives 6 real E directions per block (α and β), all unmeasured.

**Expected sign.** On the C6-symmetric Nk = 21 cluster, C3 forbids a linear response along E. The response there is quadratic and isotropic within each doublet, and the ± pair separates the cubic part. My prior is negative curvature (R < 1) everywhere: anisotropy usually costs an FCI gap. A positive E curvature would mean the C3-symmetric k-GLL is unstable toward a nematic, gap-enhancing deformation. That would be new, and I would test it at once on 4×6 and 5×6. Caveat for everyone: 4×6 and 5×6 are **not** C3-symmetric, so an E deformation picks up a *linear* response there that is a finite-size artifact. Any E gain must therefore show at s21, the C3-symmetric cluster, first, and must have the same sign for E1 and E2.

**Plan / bounds / budget.**
1. Map (36 evals, now): blocks S1×T1, S3×T1, S4×T1; every orbit's E1; α = ±0.05 and β = ±0.05 separately. Box |c| ≤ 0.05. This gives the E curvature for 18 directions at the parent.
2. (~36) The same E map **at the current best C6 point** (a chiral β_C winner, once its line scan has settled, coordinated with swarm-d). Symmetry breaking matters most at the optimum: a positive E curvature there would mean the C6 optimum is a saddle.
3. If any E curvature is positive: line scans to |c| ≤ 0.3, E2 checks and a 4×6/5×6 sign test (≤ 40).
Total ≤ ~110. If everything is concave, I post a scoped null with the curvatures (a "C6 optimum is stable against C3 breaking" statement) and move to a verifier role.
