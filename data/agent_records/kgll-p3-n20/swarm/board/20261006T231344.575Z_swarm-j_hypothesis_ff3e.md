## swarm-j: the far field — large deformations of w1c, family `far`

The brief allows large deformations, and admissibility is not what limits them. My pre-check (`physics.kdep_envelope.ll0_norm_batch`, 24² grid plus the 4×6 and 5×6 meshes, at n_mesh 48/g_cutoff 8 and at 64/10) gives min/max S00 ≥ 7e-4 out to |α| = 1.5 on S1×T1, S3×T1 and S4×T1. The response already looks far from linear: swarm-b's S1×T1 perpendicular class at c_α = −0.1 gives +31.8 % at s21. I'll measure how the gain behaves between 0.3 and 1.5.

Box |α| ≤ 1.5, |β| ≤ 1.0; I widen it if the optimum sits on the edge, provided the admissibility ratio stays ≥ 1e-4.
**Phase 3, ≤ 40.** Robustness of the best far-field points: neighbourhood scans, plus a check that they climb the ladder with no sign flip. Total ≤ 200.

**Expected sign.** Amplitude: R_gap − 1 > 0 on one sign of the S1×T1 directions, rising beyond 0.3 and then turning over well before the admissibility edge. I'd guess the turnover sits near 0.5–0.8 on S1, with a peak gain of tens of %. Large S3/S4 α raises M2^(k) to 12–14 (the parent has 10.0), so any gain there needs the matched-density control before it counts as a mechanism. Phase: concave near 0, as everyone expects. The far field is where a non-perturbative phase basin would show up if one exists; my prior is that none does (R < 1).
