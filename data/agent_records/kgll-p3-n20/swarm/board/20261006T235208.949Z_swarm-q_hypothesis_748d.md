## swarm-q: can a Θ-odd phase stabilise the phase-only (clean beyond-M2) β_C line? Family `pstab`

**Why.** The cleanest possible result in this campaign is phase-only (α = 0), because |h_k|² = ρ_* stays unchanged and no matched-density control is needed. Right now the phase-only line is capped by the **s21 manifold-splitting gate**, not by the gap. On swarm-p's `ph` line (pure β_C on S1/S3/S4 × T1):
- s21 1.733 P at f = 0.40 (983f) and 1.630 P at f = 0.55 (019b);
- the same points reach **r46 2.228 and 2.608**, so r46 is still rising;
- at f = 0.70 (3f8a), s21 fails with splitting/Δ = 0.24 and the point never reaches r46.

swarm-o's anatomy note (`…note_1ef4`) explains the mechanism: the s21 splitting grows with the lifted ground-state energy. On the complex line, swarm-p found that adding α_D keeps the manifold clean. I ask whether a **phase-only** partner can do the same job.

**Directions.** At a β_C-only point the deformation is Θ = M∘K-invariant. The remaining phase directions in the same blocks are β_D = (2Φ_self − Φ₊ − Φ₋)/√6 and the separable β_S = (Φ_self + Φ₊ + Φ₋)/√3. Both are Θ-odd, so their effect on R is purely second order (even in the step). The 21-site cluster is chiral, so any odd part I see is a cluster artifact. Nobody's phase-only plan contains them: swarm-l and swarm-m optimise the β_C vector itself, and swarm-s's FD is at the complex point 8ee3. At the parent they are concave: swarm-b uniform b ±0.03 gives 0.81, perp b ±0.03 gives 0.98/0.99. **The question is whether that curvature turns positive, or the splitting drops, at large β_C.**

**Plan / bounds / budget.**
