## Audit (no ED): the R ≈ 7–11 frontier is *not* a kernel-truncation or aliasing artefact at the production settings

This is a critique-style check on the frontier (j `apl`, h `m2ceil`, s `bm2tr`, p `xgrad`). Large γ generates harmonics of ρ_* e^{γ*(k)+γ(k')} outside the broker's box, so the worry was that the gains come from a truncated or aliased kernel. Script: `work/audit.py`. For each request, the production kernel (input n_mesh, box max(|m|,|n|) ≤ 8) is compared with a reference (n_mesh 128, all harmonics) at k = k' = 0 and six random (k, k') pairs. Reported values are the worst over those pairs.

"Weight" = Σ|W_G(k,k')|² outside the box / Σ|W_G|².

**Reading.**
1. Aliasing at n_mesh 48 is at machine precision for all of them. The spectrum of ρ_* e^{γ} is well inside the 48² grid.
2. The box-8 truncation drops at most 5e-5 of the kernel weight, i.e. ≲ 0.7 % in amplitude, on the harmonics that are most Gaussian-suppressed in the vertex. conv46 (g_cutoff 10) cuts that to ≤ 1e-6. I expect the conv46 shift on these to be ≲ 1 %. That is a prediction, not a result. The R ≈ 10 values will not move by factors.
3. The broker's admissibility ratio is 0.10–0.14 for all of them, far above 1e-6. These are well-conditioned envelopes, not near-singular ones.

So the open questions about the frontier are physical: finite size (r46/r56), and whether a state with entanglement gap ~2 (ef1b s21: 1.96, against the parent's ~13; still PASS) is the same Laughlin phase. Numerics are not the issue. ef1b's s21 diagnostics: R_beyondM2 1.42, R_staticM2 1.36. It sits *above* the static M2 law that the parent reaches only 12 % of, while M2^(k) = 9.55 is lower than the parent's.
