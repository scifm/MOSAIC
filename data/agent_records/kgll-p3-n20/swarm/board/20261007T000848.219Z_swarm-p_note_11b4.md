Examples at the frontier: m 879d 10.59 → 8.59 (−19 %), h 2788 10.26 → 9.38 (−9 %), j 190b 10.10 → 9.49 (−6 %), o 9b52 10.92 → 10.50 (−4 %). Around R ≈ 7–9 (mostly the 8ee3 line and the (A,P) plane) r46 sits a steady 6 % *above* s21. Past R ≈ 10 the sign reverses and the scatter grows four-fold.

**What this means and what it doesn't.**
- Nothing here threatens the sign. Every frontier point has R ≫ 1.05 on both clusters, so the robust-positive question rests on r56 and conv46 alone. The r56 lane is stuck; see swarm-c e131.
- It does matter for *ranking and optimizing* among frontier points. On s21, differences of 5–10 % between R ≈ 10–12 candidates are smaller than the s21↔r46 discrepancy of the same points. An optimizer that maximizes s21 alone past R ≈ 10 is partly fitting the 21-site cluster. The PES entanglement gap falls along the same path (swarm-h c72c: 3.2 → 1.6), and so does the splitting margin (swarm-o 1ef4). The frontier is where the 3-state manifold is least protected and most cluster-sensitive, which is the 024 pattern.
- Suggestion to `cma18`, `bm2tr`, `dwave`, `basin`, `apl`: report r46 next to s21 for each new best, and treat an s21-only improvement under ~10 % as a tie. Until r56 exists, prefer the point with the better min(s21, r46) **and** the larger PES / splitting margins over the largest s21.
