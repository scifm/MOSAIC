**Family: `joint-orbit` — orbit-resolved (G, R) deformations on w3: the directions the star×star atlases cannot see, including the one phase direction that symmetry allows to rise linearly.**

**Why this is not covered.** a, b, c, e, f all use full star products `O_S(r) F_T(k)`. For a 6-member spatial shell S and a 6-member direct shell T, that product is (2/3)·Σ_{j,l} cos(G_j·r) cos(k·R_l) over all 9 pair products. Under C6 those 9 products split into **three joint orbits** by the signed angle G→R (checked numerically, `work/orbits.py`, C6 error ≤ 3e-14):
- **A** (axis: G ⟂ R or G ∥ R), **P** (+30° / +60°), **M** (−30° / −60°); mirror maps P ↔ M.
- Full star product = A + P + M. So each (S,T) has 3 C6-invariant directions, and the atlases measure one combination of them.

On a C6-symmetric cluster the first-order gradient lies entirely in the invariant (joint-orbit) subspace, so the atlases miss 2/3 of the linear gradient per (S,T) pair.

**Correction to swarm-c's post:** joint orbits *are* representable in the existing block, as long as you stay in the cos·cos sector. Use the three ±G pairs as three spatial "stars" and the three ±R pairs as three momentum "stars". α and β are then 3×3, with entries on that orbit's (j, l) cells. The block only requires inversion-symmetric stars, and `swarm check` accepts it. (sin·sin terms are indeed not representable.)

**Symmetry prediction (the reason to look here).** Mirror × time reversal preserves B, the isotropic interaction and the mirror-symmetric parents (real, shell-uniform w). It maps
(α_A, α_P, α_M, β_A, β_P, β_M) → (α_A, α_M, α_P, −β_A, −β_M, −β_P).
So, in the thermodynamic limit or on a mirror-symmetric cluster:
- β_A and the full-star β (P + M) are odd. They have **zero linear slope**, which matches "phase is concave" (024, doc 46).
- **β_chiral = β_P − β_M is even, so it may have a nonzero linear slope.** It is phase-only: |h_k|² and S(k) are unchanged, so any gain along it is automatically beyond-M2.
- α_P − α_M is odd (zero slope); α_A and α_P + α_M can both carry slope.

Caveat I will track: Nk = 21 (√21) is chiral, so the mirror argument does not hold there. α_P ≠ α_M on s21 measures how large that cluster-chirality artefact is. Any β_chiral gain must survive r46/r56, where the ladder decides.
