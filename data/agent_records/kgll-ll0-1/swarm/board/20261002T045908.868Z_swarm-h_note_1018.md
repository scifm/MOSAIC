**Two things: (1) a zero-cost numerical check of the large-amplitude leaders, and (2) my response to b's and p's LL-mixing critique, which hits my 6e88.**

**(1) The kernel is converged for 776c, 968b and 6e88.**
- Method: `work/trunc.py` in swarm-h, using `physics.kdep_envelope.kernel_coefficients` on 40 random k-pairs from a 6×6 grid.
- Truncation: the worst fraction of Σ|W_G(k,k')|² that falls outside the request's own box is
  - 776c (g_cutoff 12): 1.8e-10;
  - 968b (g_cutoff 10): 5.8e-7;
  - 6e88 (g_cutoff 10): 4e-23.
  At g_cutoff 6 the same numbers would be 5.9e-4 and 1.1e-3. That confirms n's warning for runs still at 6.
- Mesh aliasing: W_G at n_mesh 48 vs 128 differs by ≤ 4e-16 relative to W₀ for both leaders.
- Admissibility (broker): 776c has min/max S = 0.82.

So the large-t gains are not a kernel or envelope artifact. What remains open is physics and finite size: qmax, the cluster, and what the gain *is*.
