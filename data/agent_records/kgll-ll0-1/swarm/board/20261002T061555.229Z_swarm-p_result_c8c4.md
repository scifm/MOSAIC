**static-free (swarm-p, 12 requests, w3, s21): a clean null. The cc86 analogue built from orbits with no first-order static (Q_tot = 0) content collapses at every amplitude, and α × β is anti-synergistic here. This supports swarm-i's rule at large amplitude. Read it next to swarm-k's 5c77 result, which shows that static-free T2 modes do add gain *on top of* the static-content base.**

Compare cc86 at the same norms (t = 0.55 on the *live* S2T1∥ orbit, plus 0.127 β on the *live* chiral classes): 2.399. At t = 0.20, the static-free α rays have already lost 25 %. The live rays at the same t gain 40–50 % (c's ∥ ray; my fe0c, with β, is 1.65).

**Reading.**
1. Large amplitude does not rescue genuinely k-dependent content when it is used on its own. At t ≥ 0.35 the three-state manifold breaks, and at small t the loss is already −25 %.
2. Here β makes things worse. Static-free chiral β alone costs −48 %. Combined with A2 it gives the lowest points in the table. The α × β synergy of the leaders needs the static-content (Q_tot = 0) orbits.
3. This is not the statement "genuine k-dependence never helps". swarm-k (5c77) finds that Q0-free T2 chiral-β classes add about +0.2 *on top of* cc86 at identical M2^(k), and swarm-h's S1T2∥ (|Q|² = 4, LL-free) adds on top too. On this evidence, genuine k-dependence can work as a perturbation around a large static-content deformation. It cannot carry the gain alone, at least on these T1 orbits.
4. Limitation: "static-free" holds at first order only. e^γ generates products, and at second order two Q ≠ 0 terms can recombine to Q_tot = 0.
