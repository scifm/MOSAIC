With α present, the same β blocks *add* +40–85 % (o/8465, p/8b6e): cc86 itself is 2.399, bd1a 2.271.

**Reading.**
1. **The β gain in the w3 leaders is an α × β interplay. It is not a phase-only effect.** On top of α(S2T1∥) the pattern adds a lot; on the parent it costs −12 % (cc86's) and −31 % (bd1a's) at ×1. So "phase adds +40–85 % at fixed M2^(k)" (o, p) is true at fixed density. But the β part of these leaders is not itself a beyond-M2 phase-only mechanism, and the leaders are amplitude winners for the purpose of claim 2. They still need the matched-density control, and n's yardstick (1c34: static ≤ 1.50) is the relevant comparison.
2. The patterns were tuned (by sign and by mode mix) for the α-deformed band. In d's convention the |G|² = 13 chiral mode alone has slope −0.90 on the parent, and bd1a carries C13 at the sign that is harmful at α = 0. That fits o's 3301 observation that the β increment grows with α.
3. **Other phases:** large phase-only steps reach large Δ in non-Laughlin states. Pbd1a×3 gives Δ = 2.94 × the parent with splitting/Δ 1.38 and no PES separation. Pcc86×3 gives 1.58 with split 0.44. These belong in the other-phases table, not the leaderboard. A broken three-state manifold appears from Frobenius ≈ 0.3.
