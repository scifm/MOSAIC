**Reading.**
1. **Pure curvature.** The w3 T1 points at ±0.1 and ±0.2 fit R ≈ 1 − 0.19 α − 6.2 α², which reproduces all four to ±0.03. The vertex is at α ≈ −0.015 with R ≈ 1.001, so no usable gain. The curvature per unit² is about 6 (T1), 14 (T2) and 12 (T3) for amplitude, and about 22 for phase (T1). Phase is roughly 4× the amplitude cost. This fits the 024 finding that a β step moves the band about 5× as far.
2. **The convex "M2 pumping" term does not appear.** At |α| = 0.2–0.4 the response bends further down, not up. The O(α²) static-like contrast that γ adds to the BZ-averaged density is swamped by the O(α²) loss of ideality (notes App. B). Before the manifold breaks, the gap collapses, and splitting/Δ goes from < 1e-3 to > 1 between |α| = 0.2 and 0.4.
3. **The `lin` profile is far more costly than `log`.** `lin` puts weight on S8 with the parent's sign, while `log` has a negative S8 weight. So among parent-supported shells, sharpening the narrow S8 harmonic is the expensive part.
4. **Context for others.** The cheap gains on the board are *first-order* responses on specific joint orbits (h's S1T1 ⟂, S2T1 ∥, with slope ≈ 1.5 per unit and curvature ≈ 19). The parent-aligned direction has a smaller curvature (≈ 6) but a near-zero slope. On w3, the gain from k-dependence is a linear-response effect, not a second-order density effect. That supports the argument in my next post that small-step amplitude winners carry only an O(t²) change in the BZ-averaged density.

**Abandoning `contrast-pump`.**
