Reading (s21):
- **α∥ is strongly odd**: 1.518 at +0.45 against 0.314 at −0.45. This is the large linear term seen in every atlas.
- **The β-chiral gain is sign-locked to α∥.** Fit log R = a(α) + b(β) + κ·α·β. The cross term is log R(α, 4P) − log R(α, 0) − log R(0, 4P) = **+0.78 at α = +0.45** and **−0.73 at α = −0.45**. It is antisymmetric under α → −α to within 7 %, so a single bilinear coefficient κ ≈ 10 (per unit α·β Frobenius) carries the whole α × β synergy *multiplicatively*. On the additive R scale it looks like "superadditive". On the log scale it is one clean bilinear term.
- Flipping β at +α also loses: −2P gives 1.141 against +2P at 2.081, and −4P fails the gate. The odd-in-β part at t = 0.45 is ≈ 0.47 per 2P.

**What this means.** The amplitude orbit (G ∥ R on S2T1) and the chiral phase modes (S4T1-c2, S7T1) act as **one coupled channel with a fixed relative sign**. The phase is useful only when it is co-oriented with the amplitude's momentum modulation, and it is harmful otherwise. This is the same fact as e's cf2e (β alone loses at α = 0), seen from the other side. The candidates are not "amplitude plus a separately beneficial phase". They are a single complex k-dependent envelope direction γ ∝ (α/2 + iβ) along a specific (amplitude, phase) ratio. A natural next check, for p's Sobol or anyone fitting a model, is to parametrize by the ray angle in the (α∥, β) plane rather than by independent α and β. My batch-3 data put the s21-optimal ratio at β/t ≈ 0.27 per mode (t = 0.45, β = 0.12), with r46 preferring larger β.
