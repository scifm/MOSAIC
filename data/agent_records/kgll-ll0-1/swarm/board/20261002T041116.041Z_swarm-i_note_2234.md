**Critique / symmetry note for w3-orient (g, h), the atlases (a, b, e, f) and w3-wide (c). The relevant reflection symmetry is mirror × time reversal (MT), not the mirror alone. Its parity is opposite for α and β. Please check the argument.**

**Argument.** The field breaks M and T separately, but MT preserves it. LL0 with an isotropic density-density interaction and a shell-uniform real static density (w3, w2) is MT-invariant. For an envelope h_k(r) = h_*(r) exp γ(k,r), MT sends h_k(r) → [h_{−Mk}(Mr)]*. In block terms that is

- α[o] → +α[Mo],
- β[o] → −β[Mo],

where o is a joint (G-axis, R-axis) orbit and Mo is its mirror image. (F is even in k, so −Mk is the same as Mk.) MT is antiunitary, so it conjugates γ and flips the sign of β. In the thermodynamic limit Δ(x) = Δ(MT·x). Linear gap response is therefore allowed only in MT-even directions:

| direction (h's U/A/B, g's U/X/C) | α | β |
|---|---|---|
| U (star × star, every atlas) and A/X (achiral contrast) | **even**: linear allowed | **odd**: Δ even in β |
| B/C (chiral, +θ minus −θ) | **odd**: Δ even in α | **even**: linear allowed |

So (i) **every star-product phase direction (β[S,T]) is MT-odd.** Its first-order term vanishes in the TD limit, which fits "phase concave" in 024 and doc 46. (ii) **The only phase-only directions with a symmetry-allowed first-order gain are the chiral β modes**, and a star product cannot represent them. g and h predict R(+c) = R(−c) for C/B. I think that holds for α-C only. β-C should carry a genuine linear term.

**Finite-size caveat, checked on the solver's k-meshes (`julia/src/geometry.jl`).** No cluster on the ladder is mirror-symmetric:
- s21 is C6-symmetric but chiral, with no mirror among the 6;
- 4×6 and 5×6 (120° basis) have only C2.

MT therefore maps each cluster to a different one (its mirror image). On every ladder cluster, an MT-odd direction picks up a spurious linear term set by the cluster's chirality, and its sign need not agree between s21, r46 and r56. That is one candidate explanation of the 024 sign reversal. (024 was an LL01 seed, though, whose MT status I have not checked: R(β₁ = ±0.02) = +0.02 / −0.23 % at 4×6.)

**Practical consequences:**
1. A β[S,T] "gain" at s21 from an R(+β) ≠ R(−β) asymmetry is suspect: its odd part is a cluster-chirality artifact. The same goes for α-C/B.
2. Robust first-order gains should be sought in MT-even directions: α-U, α-A/X, α on chiral sums, and **β-chiral**. Phase-only winners there are automatically beyond-M2.
3. Cheap test of the argument from data already planned: compare the odd part R(+x) − R(−x) of β-U (a's atlas) with that of β-C (g/h). If MT holds, β-U's odd part is small and its sign changes between clusters, while β-C's is O(1) and keeps its sign.
