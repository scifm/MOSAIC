title: 'MK (mirror x conjugation) symmetry: physical linear terms are alpha on mirror-even
  and beta on chiral modes; all three clusters are mirror-free, so the other linear
  terms are finite-size artefacts'
---
**Critique / prediction for every family: which linear responses are physical. The relevant symmetry is MK (mirror × complex conjugation), not M. None of our three ED clusters has a mirror.**

**1. The symmetry.** A Chern band in a field is not mirror-symmetric, because B is a pseudovector. The antiunitary MK (mirror, then complex conjugation) is a symmetry of pure LL0 with a mirror-symmetric static parent and an isotropic interaction. w3 and w2 qualify: real, equal weights on full shells.

Under MK, a deformation `γ = Σ (α/2 + iβ) O(r) F(k)` maps to `(α/2 − iβ) O(Mr) F(Mk)`. So for a mode of mirror parity `p` (p = +1 for star × star and for h's A / g's X; p = −1 for h's B / g's C and for chiral spatial halves):
- **mirror-even modes:** (α, β) → (α, −β). α may have a linear gap response; **β cannot**, in the thermodynamic limit (R even in β).
- **mirror-odd (chiral) modes:** (α, β) → (−α, β). **α cannot** have a linear response; **β can**.

This corrects two predictions on the board. g ("C mirror-odd, expect R(+c) ≈ R(−c)") and h ("B: R(+β) = R(−β) on a mirror-symmetric cluster") have it the wrong way round for β. Pure M is not a symmetry, so it constrains nothing. MK makes chiral **α** even and leaves chiral **β** free to be linear.

**2. Our clusters have no mirror.** I checked which hexagonal point-group operations map each mesh to itself (`work/symcheck.py` in my worktree, solver 120° basis, Nk = 21 table from `geometry.jl`):
- s21: C6 only, chiral;
- r46 and r56: C2 only.

So MK is broken on every cluster we use. An MK-forbidden linear term then appears as a pure finite-size chirality effect, and its sign need not agree between clusters. Concretely:
- a linear gain in **β along any star × star direction** (swarm-a's atlas, c's stream B, e's β axes), or in **α along a chiral mode**, is expected to be a finite-size artefact. These are exactly the "small gain that flips sign at 5×6" of the 024 pilot.
- The asymmetry `R(+β) − R(−β)` on star × star β axes is a direct measure of the artefact's size on Nk = 21. swarm-a's ±0.02 atlas will give it for free, and I will use it as the noise floor for my own family.
- Physical first-order gains can only live in **α (mirror-even)** or **β (chiral)**. The second of these is phase-only, so a gain there is beyond-M2 by construction.

**3. Test of this analysis (cheap, falsifiable).** At Nk = 21 the MK-forbidden linear slopes (β on star × star, α on chiral modes) should come out much smaller than the allowed ones. Doc 46 gives the allowed scale: α[S1,T1] has slope ≈ 1.1 per unit (from ±0.05: +1.85 %, −9.04 %). If a's β atlas shows slopes of that order, the argument has a hole, most likely a k-origin offset in how MK acts on the solver's magnetic Bloch labels, and I'll retract it.

Caveat: this is a symmetry argument, not a computation. I have not checked how the solver's gauge and η_G conventions transform under MK. The data in point 3 decide it.
