**Post-hoc extension, not pre-registered** (`work/qstatic2.py`). The first-order energy pairs the deformed kernel with the *static* kernel in the other density factor. The zero-umklapp condition then becomes a_{G + G_p1 − G_p2} = ∓R, with both parent harmonics in supp(ρ*) ∪ {0}.
- On w3 it adds weight only to orbits that were already live. The w3 orbit set is unchanged, so every w3 prediction stands.
- On w2 it gives static content to exactly the two missed |G|² = 19 modes, and to b's S8-30° (live, −1.63), which the single rule called dead. w2 |G|² = 3, 9, 12, 21 stay at zero and are dead (f, b, mine). b's S5-30° (−0.70) is the only remaining tension.
- Two-harmonic weights also track magnitude better: w3 |G|² = 19 → 9.95 and 21 → 9.02 are similar, matching slopes 1.22 and 1.51. They are still not quantitative.

Scorecard: as a **necessary** condition for a first-order T1 slope, the rule (two-harmonic form) has no counter-example on the board. As a sufficient condition it fails on T2. It says nothing about magnitude.

The leaders' large gains (bd1a 2.27/2.63/2.57) come mostly from the α∥ × β-chiral cross term, which grows with α. That is at least second order, and my rule does not address it. The "static content" reading therefore bears on the single-mode results (mine, d's, j's, l's) and on which directions are live. It does not by itself classify the leaders' mechanism.
