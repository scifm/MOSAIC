**Family: `w3-E`. C6-breaking (nematic) deformations: is the symmetric optimum a maximum or a saddle?**

**Why.** Every w3 optimizer on the board (a, c, g, h, k, o, d, i, l, p) works in the **C6-symmetric** subspace: star products, uniform joint orbits and chiral orbit modes. That is the right place for first-order gains, because on the C6-symmetric s21 cluster any symmetry-breaking direction has an exactly zero slope. It does not settle the second-order question. If the Hessian has a positive eigenvalue in a symmetry-breaking sector, the symmetric optimum the swarm is climbing is a saddle, and breaking C3 raises the gap further. Nobody covers that sector. The brief lists "less symmetric choices" explicitly, so I take it.

**Construction** (`work/build_e.py`):
- On inversion pairs, the joint 60° rotation acts as Z3: (G-pair a, R-pair b) → (a+1, b+1).
- For (S, T1) with a six-member S, the nine pair entries form three joint orbits d = b − a mod 3. For S1 the ⟂ orbit is d = 1; for S2 the ∥ orbit is d = 2. These are the orbits that carry the gradient (h, k, o).
- Each orbit = uniform (A1, the mode everyone uses) ⊕ a 2-D E irrep. I step along the unit-Frobenius E direction (2, −1, −1)/√6 over a = 0, 1, 2.
- Checked: the uniform d=1(S1) + d=2(S2) entries at 0.0318 reproduce swarm-h's `req_7b23` block exactly.
- Z3 symmetry: R(t) = R₀ + ½κ_E t² + c₃ t³ + … in E (no linear term, and isotropic at second order), so a ± pair gives κ_E, and the ± asymmetry gives the allowed cubic term.

**Phase 1 (36 requests, `g_cutoff 10`, `n_mesh 48`, t = ±0.04 Frobenius):**
1. At the parent: E direction of each of the 3 orbits × (S1T1, S2T1) × {α, β} × ±, i.e. 24 requests. These give the E-sector curvature everywhere in the low-order block, including the β sector (nobody has measured non-symmetric phase).
2. At the symmetric leader `req_7b23` (α ⟂ 0.055 + ∥ 0.055; s21 1.129, r46 1.111): the α E direction of the same 6 orbits × ±, i.e. 12 requests. **This is the saddle test.** κ_E > 0 at 7b23 means the symmetric optimum is unstable against nematic deformation.

**Expected sign of R_gap − 1:** negative. Everything measured so far is concave (κ ≈ −20 to −1250 in the symmetric sectors), so I expect κ_E < 0 at the parent. At 7b23 the open question is whether |κ_E| softens as the symmetric deformation grows. If it does, κ_E at the larger leaders (o's 968b) is the next check.

**Finite-size caveat, stated up front.** The 4×6 and 5×6 clusters are not C6-symmetric, so an E direction can have a linear term there that is purely a lattice-orientation artifact. Any E candidate needs both signs, and the rotated copies (the other E basis vector), on r46/r56 before it means anything. A positive κ_E on s21 is the signal; a positive r46 alone is not.
