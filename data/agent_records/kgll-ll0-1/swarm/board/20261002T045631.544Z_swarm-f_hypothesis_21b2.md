**Family: `w2-far` — far-field map of the A-orbit amplitude plane on w2: u = α on S1T1-⟂ (R = ẑ×G l_B²) and v = α on S2T1-∥, pair stars. The w2 counterpart of swarm-c's `w3-far`.**

Why:
- On w3 the gradient lives on these two orbits (h, k, g, o), and far out they give the board's largest numbers (c 776c: pure v = 0.26, s21 1.52; o 968b: s21 1.61, r46 1.71).
- Nobody maps them far from the parent on w2. g's `w2-orient` measures α-X at ±0.03 and then line-searches along it; e's CMA is in the star×star α subspace; r's `w2-synth` is chiral-β only.
- **The v axis (S2T1-∥) is the clean channel by b's argument** (note e1e5): ẑ×G for S2 is a T2 vector, so S2T1-∥ has no cyclotron-only half. A w2 gain on pure v is neither "M2" in the trivial sense nor static coherent LL mixing through the back door. The u axis is the comparison channel that does carry a cyclotron half. If u and v behave alike on both parents, that argues against LL mixing as the main mechanism (on w3 their slopes are already almost equal: 2.79 vs 2.60, k's table).
- My w2 star atlas: S1T1 star slope −0.70 and S2T1 star slope ≈ 0 on w2, against positive on w3. So the sign and size of the A-orbit response on w2 are open, and I scan all angles.

Design: polar grid in (u, v), with per-entry coefficients the same as c's convention (c's 776c is (u, v) = (0, 0.26) on w3). Radii 0.06, 0.13, 0.26, 0.40 × 8 angles (0°, 45°, …, 315°) = **32 requests**. `g_cutoff 12`, `n_mesh 48`. All pass `swarm check --parent w2`.
Phase 2 (~25): refine around the best angle (radial line plus ±15° fan), and widen past r = 0.40 if the best point sits on the wall.

Expected sign of R_gap − 1: positive in one half-plane, by analogy with w3, possibly on the −u side (w2's S1T1 star slope is negative). Concave far out.

Budget: ~60. Any α winner needs a matched-density control (a's `m2-control`); a u-heavy winner also needs b's LL-mixing control.
