**Family: `chiral-orbit` — chirality-odd joint (G,R) orbits, phase-only first. A symmetry argument says these are the phase directions with a nonzero linear slope, and every separable star×star phase direction has none.**

**The argument.** LL0 in a field is not mirror-symmetric, but M·K (mirror × complex conjugation) is a symmetry of LL0 + a D6-symmetric parent (w3 and w2 are full real shells) + an isotropic interaction. Acting on the envelope it sends γ(k,r) → γ(−Mk, Mr)*. For γ = Σ (α/2 + iβ) O_G(r) F_R(k), a (G,R) combination maps to (MG, MR), so the relative angle θ between G and R goes to 180° − θ, and (α, β) → (α, −β). Rotations keep θ. So the 9 combos of a 6-member spatial shell × a 6-member momentum shell fall into three joint C6 orbits (θ = 30°/90°/150° or 0°/60°/120°), and M·K swaps the two chiral ones. Linear terms of R_gap must be M·K-invariant:
- g_β(θ) = −g_β(180°−θ), and g_β(achiral orbit) = 0. **Any separable full-star β (sum of all three orbits) has zero bulk slope.** That would explain why phase looks purely concave so far.
- The chiral phase direction β(θ) − β(180°−θ) is M·K-even and **may have a nonzero linear slope**, so one sign gains at small amplitude. Phase-only leaves |h_k|² and M2^(k) unchanged, so a gain here is beyond-M2 by construction.
- For amplitude it is the other way round: α(θ) − α(180°−θ) has zero slope.

Caveat: the Nk = 21, 4×6 and 5×6 clusters are not mirror-symmetric, so a "zero" slope can be nonzero on a cluster. A chiral slope that comes from the cluster's own handedness would flip between clusters. The ladder tests exactly that.

**Representability.** This is in the existing block: one ±G pair per spatial star, one ±R pair per momentum star, and the J×P α/β matrix holds one coefficient per (G pair, R pair). This answers part of swarm-c's question. Joint cos·cos orbits are representable. sin(G·r) sin(k·R) terms (e.g. a k-dependent shift of the envelope) are not, because stars must be inversion-closed. Script: `work/chiral.py` (orbit enumeration + block builder) in my worktree.
