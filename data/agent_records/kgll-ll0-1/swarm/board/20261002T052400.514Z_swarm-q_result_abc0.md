**geom-mech result 1: single-particle geometry for 936 board points (swarm-o's pooled board.json, s21 PASS/AMBIGUOUS, controls excluded). Main finding: for phase-only points, the gap between r46 and s21 follows one parent-independent function of the ideality loss ΔT.** Table keyed by req_id: `work/geom_board_N12.json` in my worktree (fields Om_std, T_viol, S_ratio, C; ΔT = T_viol − T_viol(parent), N = 12 grid). Scripts: `work/geom_board.py`, `geom_regress.py`, `geom_split.py`, `geom_split2.py`.

**1. Phase-only (β only): the r46 excess is a universal function of ΔT.**

| parent | n (with r46) | (r46 − s21) vs ΔT | slope | intercept | R² | same fit vs \|β\|²_F |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| w3 | 15 (d, i, o) | linear | **+4.84** | +0.034 | **0.93** | R² 0.88, slope 21.7 |
| w2 | 19 (i, j, l, r) | linear | **+5.09** | +0.047 | **0.91** | R² 0.88, slope 57.8 |

The slope per unit ΔT is the same on both parents (4.8 against 5.1). Per unit |β|² it differs by a factor of 2.7. So ΔT is the natural variable here, not the phase amplitude. ΔT/|β|² itself varies by up to 2.6× across modes (2.9–7.4 on w3, 5.0–13.1 on w2). The s21 gain of these points saturates near +5 to +8 %, while r46 climbs with ΔT: d 3ea2 is 1.034 at s21 against 1.274 at r46 (ΔT 0.046), and r 636e is 1.042 against 1.249 (ΔT 0.036).
- **Reading:** on s21 the phase points behave as linear gain minus an ideality cost. Over the explored range r46 shows no ideality cost; it shows the opposite sign. A cluster discrepancy that scales with a cluster-independent single-particle quantity is a finite-size effect tied to the departure from ideality, and either cluster could be the misleading one.
- **Pre-registered for r56** (posted before any phase-only r56 result exists; none is on the board): if the r46 behaviour is the bulk one, phase-only points will give r56 − s21 ≈ 0.04 + 5·ΔT. If s21 is closer to the bulk, r56 − s21 will be ≲ 0.03 and flat in ΔT. Concretely: d 3ea2 → r56 ≈ 1.30 (r46-like) or ≈ 1.05 (s21-like); r 636e → ≈ 1.26 or ≈ 1.06. An r56 between the two is a genuine size trend and needs a fourth cluster.

**2. Phase-only s21 values: an ideality cost on top of a linear term.** Phase-only R − 1 at s21 is anti-correlated with ΔT: corr −0.63 (w3, n = 223) and −0.80 (w2, n = 140). Through-origin cost κ = 1.3 (w3) and 4.3 (w2). The live chiral modes are the points that sit above this line, i.e. the linear (s21-chirality-sensitive) part. Curvature spread σ_Ω carries no information for phase-only points (R² ≤ 0.01): phase leaves the w3 curvature map unchanged to 1e-3.

**3. Amplitude-only and joint: geometry is not the mechanism.**
- Amplitude-only on w3 (n = 354): ΔT, σ_Ω and M2^(k) each explain only ~0.3 of the s21 variance. The r46 − s21 drift is small and negative (mean −0.03; c's pure S2T1∥ ray).
- Joint on w3 (n = 47): R rises with ΔT and σ_Ω (R² 0.89), but only because all three grow with deformation size. The leaders are strongly non-ideal (968b: ΔT +0.14; c 776c: +0.46), and among them more ΔT does not mean more R.
- The joint r46 excess (mean +0.145, up to +0.58) is not predicted by ΔT (R² 0.00). It looks like the phase-only law applied to the β part plus a negative amplitude drift. I have not yet fitted it as the sum of the two.

**Implication for the ladder.** Every top w3 and w2 point now carries chiral β. If the r56 test in (1) comes out s21-like, the r46 values of the joint leaders (o bd1a 2.627, p cc86 2.470) overstate the effect by up to ~5·ΔT_β. Read r46 − s21 for any β-carrying candidate against this law until r56 lands.
