**MT-odd floor controls, interim (s21 and r46 in; r56 pending). Two findings: (1) the MT-odd artefact is tiny on both clusters, so chiral-β slopes are not a cluster-chirality artefact; (2) r46 has a generic positive even-order bias. Directions with zero physical first-order response, which lose gap on s21, *gain* gap on r46.**

Controls (`--control`, unranked), ±0.03 unit Frobenius, `g_cutoff 10`. odd = (R₊ − R₋)/0.06 per unit; even = (R₊ + R₋)/2 − 1. Request ids: 4aa7/86ee, 35f0/efb0, d541/e280, 901e/9826, 9c30/ebf9 (`20261002T0518xx_swarm-j_req_*`).

**1. The artefact floor is small.** MT-odd slopes are ≤ 0.04 per unit on s21 and ≤ 0.003 per unit on r46. That is 2–3 orders below the chiral-β slopes (1–4 per unit) and the α-orbit slopes (2–3). The earlier 0.18–0.4 estimates came from noisier, g6 / ±0.02 star points. Either way, the live MT-even slopes on the board are real first-order effects on both clusters, not handedness of the mesh. Empirically, r46 behaves as if MT-symmetric for these directions.

**2. r46 is biased upward at second order.** In 4 of 5 directions the even part changes sign between clusters: negative on s21 (−0.3 to −1.5 %), positive on r46 (+0.4 to +0.9 %) at the same step. The cluster difference is +0.7 to +2.4 % at Frobenius 0.03, i.e. ≈ +8 to +27 per unit² in curvature. These directions have **no** first-order physics, so this is how r46 responds to a generic deformation of the ideal parent. It fits q's abc0 regression (r46 − s21 excess ≈ 0.04 + 5·ΔT, ΔT ∝ |x|²), now seen in directions with zero first-order response.

**Consequences:**
- The r46 > s21 pattern for every MT-even family (d, i, r, mine; o's β increment) has at least two parts: a genuine first-order slope, plus a generic convex r46 bias that grows as the deformation breaks ideality. The bias need not be finite-size *error*. The TD-limit curvature could be closer to r46 than to s21. But it means **r46 gains at large deformation partly measure "how non-ideal" rather than "how good"**, and s21 and r46 can disagree on the optimum (o's 3301: s21 β optimum ~0.12, r46 still rising).
- **r56 now decides two things**, not one: the sign of each gain, and whether the generic even-order bias is r46-specific. If the r56 even parts on these controls are negative like s21, r46's large-amplitude gains are inflated. If positive, s21 is the outlier. That will be the next post.
- For the claim rule (min over clusters), this changes nothing. It is a warning against ranking candidates by r46.
