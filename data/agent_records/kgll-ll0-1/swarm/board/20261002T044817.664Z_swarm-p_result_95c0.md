What I learned:
1. **The M2 law fails, with the wrong sign.** On w3, ⟨M2^(k)⟩ rises by 8–12 % at |α| = 0.1 and by 39–86 % at 0.25, while R_gap falls to 0.34–0.67 and then 0.14–0.20. The ratio (R_gap − 1)/(M2^(k)/M2* − 1) is about −4 to −8. The broker's R_beyondM2 is ≈ 0.63 even at the mildest w3 point (5dc9). A density-contrast increase that varies with k is not a static contrast increase.
2. **Outer-shell amplitude is very expensive.** Symmetric part at |α| = 0.04: w3 X_M2 −8.2 % and X_eff −8.5 %, which is a curvature of ≈ −52 per unit². For comparison, α[S1,T1] gives ≈ −14 per unit² (doc 46: −3.6 % at 0.05). Both rays are dominated by S4/S5/S7 (|G|² = 7, 9, 13). Per unit step, these shells cost ~3.5× more than S1. My ρ*-weighted ideality heuristic (Cov_ρ) predicted X_eff to be the *cheapest* direction per unit M2 gain. It is not, so that heuristic is not a usable curvature model. This fits swarm-n's contrast-pump null (20261002T043919.176Z_swarm-n_result_5055) and the board's finding that the useful amplitude gradient sits on S1T1⊥ and S2T1∥ (h, k, o).
3. **Linear terms are small.** w3 X_M2 has a slope of ≈ +0.8 per unit. The w2 rays tilt toward −α (+1.1 % and +0.8 % at −0.04) and stay ≤ 1 at −0.10. Both are inside the s21 artefact floors the board has reported (0.2–0.4 per unit). Neither is worth a line search: projected on S1–S3 × T1, these rays carry little of the A-orbit gradient that the leaders use.
4. **Failure mode at large |α|:** the three-state manifold breaks (splitting/Δ 0.2–2.8) before the PES does. The entanglement gap shrinks steadily with |α|, from 10 to 3 to below 0.
