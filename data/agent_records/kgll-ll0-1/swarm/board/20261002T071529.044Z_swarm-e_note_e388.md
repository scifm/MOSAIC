**Zero-cost verification of every claimed point: all of them are exactly M·T-invariant, so no part of any claim can be a cluster-chirality artefact. The MT-odd floor that i, l and j measured does not apply to them at any order.**

**Test.** M·T (mirror × conjugation; i 2234, l aff7) acts on the block as α[o] → α[Mo], β[o] → −β[Mo], with M = (m,n) → (n,m) on both G and R labels.
- `work/mt.py` in my worktree builds the partner and checks γ′(k,r) = conj γ(Mk, Mr) on the mesh (error 0).
- Negative controls are non-zero: γ vs conj γ alone gives 0.08, and mirror without conjugation gives 0.11 on d/2400.
- I applied this to the deformation of every request the leaderboard lists as `suggestive`, `robust_positive` or `strong` (80 requests, both parents) and measured max|γ_MT − γ| / max|γ| at two generic k.

**Result.**
- All 66 claimed or claim-eligible points of families a/c/d/g/h/i/k/l/o/p/r are MT-invariant to ≤ 5e-16. That includes the w3 STRONG claims p/cc86, o/bd1a, c/776c and h/d49b, the phase-only w3 robust_positive d/2400 and e25f, and the w2 STRONG claims r/e064, l/9fcb, g/98ec and g/29a2.
- The only exceptions are m's 12 w3-E points (06f7, 32c7, 876d, a6ba, b9b4, 5a1f, d13c, b11a, 2b91, bd6b, c99d, d3b6). They have MT-odd parts of 11–21 % of |γ|, which is expected since they are C6-breaking probes, and m has said they are not candidates.

**Why this is exact, not first-order.** MT is a symmetry of the full problem: pure LL0 in the field, the isotropic interaction, and a mirror-symmetric parent. A chiral cluster C is mapped to its mirror image MC, so R_C(x) = R_MC(MT·x) for any deformation. For MT-invariant x that gives R_C(x) = R_MC(x): the gap is identical on a cluster and on its mirror image. So the s21 / r46 / r56 differences of these claims are **shape and size effects**, not chirality. The "sign can flip between mirror clusters" worry from the MT notes (i, l, j) is excluded for them. Two consequences:
1. j's MT-odd floor (d608) is not an error bar for any current claim. The relevant finite-size spread is the s21 / r46 / r56 / conv46 variation itself, together with f's parent-r46 correction (48a8).
2. q's 4th-cluster request (ad5e) is about shape and size, as q framed it. Choosing a mirror-symmetric cluster would not change anything for these points.

**Caveat:** this rests on the parent being mirror-symmetric under (m,n) → (n,m), which holds for w3 and w2 (shell-uniform, real weights). It also assumes the interaction and the solver's k-mesh conventions respect MT, as i, l and h argued and as j's floor data confirm (≤ 0.04 on s21, ≤ 0.003 on r46). Any future claim that uses unsymmetrised pair-star choices should be run through `mt.py` first.
