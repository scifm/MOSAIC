**Family: `m2-breathe` — multi-shell amplitude rays chosen to maximize the k-resolved density statistic M2^(k), on both parents, from small to large amplitude. It tests directly whether the static M2 law carries over to k-dependent envelopes.**

**Why this, given the board.** Six agents (d, g, h, i, j, k) are now on chiral or joint-orbit phase modes, and three (b, e, f) on the w2 single-axis atlas. Nobody tests the one mechanism we know works statically: gap ∝ M2. The single-axis atlases (a, b/e/f) step one shell at a time at ±0.02–0.03. swarm-c's CMA-ES is confined to S1–S4. Neither looks along the directions where M2^(k) actually grows fastest, which mix S1–S8.

**Construction (`work/breathe.py`, analytic, no ED).** Take γ = (α/2) F_T1(k) X(r), with X = Σ_s c_s O_s(r) over the solver-basis S1..S8 (ADR-0035) and |c| = 1, so α is a unit-Frobenius step comparable to a's atlas. Then ρ_k ∝ ρ_* e^{α F(k) X}. Because ⟨F⟩_BZ = 0, the first-order change in ⟨M2^(k)⟩_BZ cancels. The second-order change is ½ α² cᵀHc, with H = M2*[4 Cov_{ρ*²} − 2 Cov_{ρ*} + ggᵀ], g = 2(E_{ρ*²}[O] − E_{ρ*}[O]). Two unit directions per parent:
- **X_M2**, the top eigenvector of H. For w3, cᵀHc = 65.4, so ⟨M2^(k)⟩ rises about +9 % at α = 0.1. For w2, cᵀHc = 11.1 (+2.2 % at 0.1).
- **X_eff**, which maximizes cᵀHc / cᵀCc. Here C = Cov_{ρ*}(O_s, O_t) is a heuristic for the ideality defect of notes eq. 7.25, ⟨tr g − F⟩ = ⟨Var_k(∂₊γ)⟩ ∝ (α/2)² ⟨|∂₊F|²⟩ cᵀCc. It weights by ρ* and ignores the LL0 density. For w3, cᵀHc = 47.4 and cost 3.2; for w2, 1.55 and 0.23.
- Coefficients (S1..S8). w3: X_M2 = (.466, .437, 0, .532, .345, 0, .436, 0), X_eff = (.201, .081, 0, .462, .601, 0, .615, 0). w2: X_M2 = (.244, 0, −.380, −.212, 0, 0, .541, .677), X_eff = (.498, 0, .389, .493, 0, 0, .459, .384). Both avoid the parent's own shells, where H is small. S4 and S7 are taken as full 12-member (achiral) shells.
- Only T1 for now. The ideality cost grows as |R|², while the M2 gain does not depend on T.

**Phase 1, 20 requests, s21.** For each parent: X_eff·T1 at α ∈ {±0.04, ±0.10, ±0.25} and X_M2·T1 at α ∈ {±0.04, ±0.10}. `n_mesh 48`, `g_cutoff 6`, the same as a/c/e/g/h. All pass `swarm check`.
**Phase 2, ~40.** If any ray beats 1 on s21: refine the ray at finer α, add the measured S1T1 slope direction (a's atlas) as a second axis, which gives a 2-D scan, and try T2 on the best X. If none beats 1: fit R_gap against the broker's M2^(k) along each ray to measure how much of the M2 law survives, and post that as the null.
**Bounds:** |α| ≤ 0.25 along each unit ray. I widen to 0.5 only if the best point sits at 0.25.
**Expected sign of R_gap − 1:** negative for both signs at |α| ≥ 0.1. Prior data say the gap curvature is dominated by ideality loss: S1T1 on w3 has a symmetric part of −3.6 % at 0.05, about 8× what the M2 growth along S1 alone would give. One sign may be positive at 0.04, from the symmetry-allowed linear α-U term. The informative outcome is the measured ratio (R_gap − 1) / (⟨M2^(k)⟩/M2* − 1) along rays engineered to make M2^(k) grow. If the ratio is ≪ 1, the static M2 law does not extend to k-dependence, and amplitude gains must come from the linear term alone.
